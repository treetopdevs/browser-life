# Next direction: make renewal possible, then test whether disturbance helps

Decision note, 2026-10-04. Proposed after adversarial Claude Fable 5 High review and discussion. **Unexecuted, not a preregistration or a claim of antifragility.** The completed construction study and its negative screens remain closed.

The [execution plan](RENEWAL-PLAN.md) fixes the remaining seeds, placements, local-maintenance thresholds, candidate-selection rule, confirmation gate and implementation/verification work. No experiment has been launched.

## The change in perspective

The useful question is whether a retained patch can support further living activity under the same local rules. The stationary witness proves a paid retention benefit. It deliberately suppresses movement, and its initial matter is entirely biomass. Its moving and drag extensions also start without an external nutrient reservoir. Those are demanding persistence experiments; they do not establish that new transport physics is necessary for expansion.

This does not make colonisation mathematically impossible: matter can be recycled and redistributed, and light supplies energy. Nor does it invalidate earlier nutrient-containing exploratory pilots elsewhere in the project. It identifies a cheap, controlled question about this particular witness family that should precede another law or broad search: **can a finite initial nutrient reservoir turn persistence into renewal under rule 1?**

My heuristic is to give a functioning retention mechanism something useful to expand into, then measure the tradeoff between retaining resources and accessing new ones. That can expose selectable differences which a single declining site cannot. Fable agreed to prioritize this over its original pressure/packet proposals. Success is uncertain; the point is to distinguish habitat limitations from transport limitations with a small diagnostic.

## One bounded diagnostic, before any new physics

Use the existing witness controller and rule-1 parameters, mutation disabled. Keep the 32×32 torus, uniform light, dtQ=0, motility off, B1024/E2048 at the founder, no initial P, and no abiotic recycling. The proposed finite pilot is:

| Factor | Fixed candidate values |
|---|---|
| Initial uniform A reservoir | 4 or 16 quanta per cell |
| Spread | 0, 1, 2; zero is the transport-disabled control |
| Phenotype/control | BUILD16 builder; matched BUILD0; builder with cost-retaining polymer-transport ablation; previously selected nonbuilder |
| Horizon and observation | 10,000 steps; census every 100 steps, exact cumulative reaction/transport accounting |
| Budget | 24 main pilot histories in one common seed block; controls below; at most one chosen configuration gets five fresh confirmation blocks |

The comparison is **within each habitat**, where arms have identical matter/free-energy arrays, light schedule and geometry; genomes and the specified mechanism switch differ. A4 adds 4,096A; A16 adds 16,384A. With the existing eA=0, both retain initial chemical energy 10,240 and free energy 2,048, but have different total matter 5,120 and 17,408. They are not equal-resource comparisons with one another or the original B1024-only witness. No compensating free energy or replenishment is added. Record offered light exposure and actual harvested `lightIn` separately: the latter depends on photosynthetic activity and is an outcome, not an input that can be forced equal without changing the model.

Before the main pilot, use a fixed, labeled set of controls:

1. **Capacity at the original density:** two separated B1024/E2048 sites in the spread-zero habitat, one per reservoir level, horizon 3,000. The doubled initial B/E is explicitly recorded; this is a sufficiency control, not a matched treatment. It asks whether the specified habitat can keep two known-density patches alive. A failure means this control did not establish capacity; it is not an impossibility proof about all two-patch layouts.
2. **Same-budget division:** two separated B512/E1024 sites, again one per reservoir level and spread zero. This preserves the main pilot's initial B/E totals and distinguishes division of that budget from coexistence at the known density. Failure can reflect low founder density, not just habitat capacity.
3. **Establishment probes:** single B64/E128 and B128/E256 founders, in each of the six reservoir/spread combinations. These twelve probes measure persistence/growth at disclosed starting sizes. Two sizes cannot establish a universal minimum viable mass, or rule out cooperative clusters, state dependence or non-monotone effects. Keep their unequal initial budgets separate from the main comparison.

The capacity/division controls establish facts only for spread zero. They do not certify capacity under spread 1–2, where leakage and interference differ; those regimes have their own small-founder probes, with the stated limits.

That is at most 40 pilot/control histories, with no parameter tuning during the batch. Use controls to interpret outcomes, not to invent a new success threshold after seeing the main runs. Before execution, freeze exact seed IDs (fresh relative to earlier work), placements, source hashes, a numeric establishment threshold and persistence window, the configuration-selection tie-break, and the confirmation decision rule. These details belong in a new protocol, not in a retrospective amendment to the old experiment. This note schedules no run.

Track two distinct outcomes:

- **Renewal capability:** any phenotype establishes sustained, metabolically active biomass outside its initial footprint while a viable source remains. Distinguish growth from transported residue using local reaction flux and mass balance; record sustained support at every census as *sampled*, not continuous survival. Connected expansion is admissible. Disconnected components are an observable, not an imposed organism definition. This establishes an expansion opportunity, not indefinite self-renewal or autonomous offspring.
- **Construction's contribution:** compare the builder with the matched and strongest disclosed nonbuilders and the cost-retaining ablation. If nonbuilders also expand, construction necessity fails, but that does not erase progress on finding a renewable ecology. Do not discard a useful evolutionary regime because it is not builder-specific.

All pilot results remain visible. Select at most one candidate by the prewritten rule and confirm every arm on the five fresh blocks; count blocks, not correlated arms or repeated readouts. A negative fixed batch stops this reservoir experiment. It does not authorize another dose, a nutrient-placement search or an automatic new law. If capacity/probe controls fail, report the unresolved diagnosis rather than claiming transport was disproved. If only external support sustains the new sites, name that dependence; a later isolation or dependency assay must establish what can persist independently.

## What comes after a demonstrated renewal regime

**First, selection in a population.** Start mixtures of nearby inherited variants, with explicit standing-variation and mutation treatments. Measure differential persistence and local expansion under shared conserved resources, lineage coexistence, extinction and competition from rarity. A changing best genome at one stationary site is insufficient. Genotype-neutral propagation controls and terminal-only, equal-resource reconstitution distinguish drift, inherited effects and accumulated habitat. Common random numbers may form paired blocks; they do not create extra independent evolutionary histories. Do not require detached, organism-shaped offspring before looking for selection in connected populations.

**Then, a demanding project test of antifragility.** Fix the level of claim (population or ecology), challenge distribution, intensity, duration and observation horizon. Compare bounded disturbance histories with calm histories under the same initial matter/energy and matched offered-energy budgets. Require a beneficial effect on later population performance and inherited capability, not just recovery. Preserve every original seed block, assign population extinctions zero performance, and report the cost during the exposure period alongside later benefit.

Use equal-resource genome reconstitution to isolate genomic inheritance and separately controlled state transfers to investigate ecological memory. Neither standardized transfer can replace the original-population endpoint or conceal the cost of losing most source populations. Mutation-off improvement from selection on standing variation is legitimate; it limits a claim about de novo mutation rather than invalidating selection. Test the trained challenge class and a prospectively withheld class. Transfer to withheld challenges is a deliberately stronger project gate, not a universal definition of the word “antifragility.” Quantitative effect thresholds, replicate counts and failure criteria must be fixed before that study.

## Why the direction could compound

These are opportunities to test, not results:

- Retention versus access creates a local tradeoff: strong barriers may preserve recycling but restrict new nutrient intake. Different environments could favor different phenotypes instead of one universally stronger BUILD setting.
- Expansion can generate nutrient gradients, recycled waste and persistent polymer structure. Those changes could create a second opportunity for another phenotype. Demonstrate it with conditioned-habitat/common-garden comparisons at matched resources, rather than counting a new shape or dead polymer as ecological novelty.
- Coexisting strategies can make disturbances change which resources and spaces are available. Selection could then favor histories that retain useful diversity or reconstruct function faster after a later challenge. This would connect environmental modification, inheritance and subsequent selection—the causal chain the stationary assay currently lacks.

Failure modes are equally plausible: the reservoir merely extends a finite decline; one fast exploiter monopolizes all resources; polymer blocks feeding; cheaters erase the construction benefit; apparent benefits come from seed dependence, survivor filtering or additional harvested energy mistaken for an equal-input result. Long evolution runs should wait until the short experiments can distinguish these outcomes.

## Deferred options

Pressure transport and a convex mass-dependent outflow law remain untested alternatives; neither has demonstrated priority. If eventually considered, require local genotype-blind rules, exact conservation, bounded arithmetic, no viability check or prescribed offspring packet in the physics, explicit stochastic rounding and extracellular-P/genome-inheritance semantics. A constructed diagnostic must precede an evolutionary search. Uniform treatment of B/P/E is one design option, not the only principled one.

Existing ponds/current scaffolding can test selection under imposed transfer. It must be identified as external ecological scaffolding and should not duplicate work already underway in other workspaces. It cannot establish that reproduction emerged from local transport. Neither a new transport law nor a new pond experiment is part of the present review remediation.
