# Travelling oases

At `/particles/oases/`, watch the same movement recipe under three environments: still food patches, slow travel, and faster travel. Each comparison uses one seed and the shared comparison tick; an extinct world stops advancing while the others continue.

The patches carry their food with them. Slow and faster travel move at 0.0001 and 0.00025 world widths per tick. Rows move in opposing directions across a world whose edges join. Food renewal and organism rules remain unchanged; there is no hidden rescue, forced birth or relocation of organisms. A weak attraction toward food is already built into every organism and remains explicitly engineered.

Choose a movement preset or seed, pause to inspect, step one tick, reset the comparison, or download an observation. The graphs show populations and the readouts show contact with food. An extinct world stays empty while other worlds continue. Saved observations do not resume a simulation.

The world picker remains a still-environment recipe tool. Supported older version2 recipe-choice files are validated against their original canonical history before upgrading their version metadata; importing them starts still worlds at tick zero. They are never silently reinterpreted as moving-environment histories.

This exploration asks whether changed opportunities produce following, regrouping and population dips or rebounds. Those observations are distinct from improved survival or adaptation. Frozen groups, repeated patterns and extinctions are valid outcomes to record.

## Observed outcomes

The [pursuit pilot](oases-pilot.md) kept all nine worlds populated through 6,000 ticks, with broadly similar ranges. The [flock follow-up](oases-flocks.md) showed larger and mixed effects: moving food changed which founding families persisted, but did not uniformly improve population.
