# The activity repeats; the three-family balance does not

The unchanged split-band design passed the frozen screen on seed 1203. It remained populated, kept both feeding regions occupied, and visibly reorganized between ticks 2,500 and 3,000. It ended with 35 bodies and two families at 22 gold / 13 lilac. The earlier seed's attractive three-family balance did not recur: mint was first absent at the tick-1,720 sample.

| Seed 1203 at 3,000 | Bodies | Mint / gold / lilac | Programs | Maximum-ever generation |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Historical contiguous band | 29 | 12 / 0 / 17 | 11 | 15 |
| New split bands | 35 | 0 / 22 / 13 | 16 | 14 |

The numerical gate required at least two families, not three, so this is a valid pass under the frozen rule. It reproduces permissive activity and spatial change, not seed 1202's 10/8/9 balance. Historical split seed 1202 remains separate and was not rerun. There is exactly one new outcome here; the contiguous comparison was already known.

| New split late window | Births | Deaths | Ticks at 48-body cap |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1,501–2,000 | 94 | 82 | 19 |
| 2,001–2,500 | 81 | 96 | 16 |
| 2,501–3,000 | 89 | 87 | 2 |

All numerical windows passed. Occupied body tiles in the two feeding bands changed from 290 / 165 at 2,500 to 126 / 448 at 3,000, above the required 10 in each. The balance of occupied area shifted strongly toward the second band. This is a description of saved occupancy, not evidence that organisms learned to migrate or that the bands contain independent colonies.

The body-only frames show the compact upper-middle/right group becoming a wider branching group near the upper edge. A dense lower-left group clears while a much longer vertical branching cluster forms lower-middle/right. These occupied-tile and gap changes pass the own-world visual gate. Parent review was explicitly not blind to the historical baseline and numerical outcome; each receipt observation states that limitation. No overlap diagnostics were run.

The split world accepted 278,816 food units, versus 288,634 in the historical contiguous world, from 460,800 offered to each. It ended with more bodies and programs but lower maximum-ever generation and less accepted food. Those descriptive differences do not establish overall superiority, adaptation, improved fitness or lasting coexistence. The historical contiguous world's gold family was first absent at sample 2,680; the new split world's mint was first absent at 1,720. Absence times have 20-tick sample resolution.

One predetermined 3,000-tick contiguous regression matched all 151 samples and frames, 3,000 tick rows and five complete snapshots before the new split run and its independent repeat. It wrote no new baseline artifact. There was one successful attempt, no retries, no seed 1202 or 1204 run, and no tuning or horizon extension. The frozen model and all prior sources and evidence remain unchanged.

Stop here as planned. Keep static split bands as an active prototype, while dropping any expectation that they reliably preserve all three families. For a separate creative slice, alternate replenishment between the two bands while preserving the total offered budget, then compare against this saved static split world. That could produce visible growth and retreat as feeding changes places. The switching schedule, seed, horizon and stops must be reviewed and frozen before any run; this suggestion is not implemented here. Food already deposited would remain, so it would test pulsed supply and local intensity together, not a clean migration mechanism. No extra seed is needed merely to complete the earlier two-seed proposal.
