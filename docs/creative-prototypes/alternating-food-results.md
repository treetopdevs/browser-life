# A large shift, a deep trough, and recovery

Alternating feeding produced a visibly changing world on this first seed. Seed 1203 reached 3,000 ticks and passed the four frozen screens: activity, continued occupancy in both bands, at least one seasonal growth/retreat block, and manual body-only change. It ended with 31 bodies, three families at 7/2/22,11 programs and maximum-ever generation 15. All three families are present in every saved 20-tick sample; this is not evidence of durable coexistence.

The strongest shift occurred from 1,500 to 2,000: occupied tiles in band 0 fell 519→88 while the fed band 1 rose 116→539. The body-only frames show dense upper-left and lower-left clusters largely clearing while broad upper and lower clusters grow in band 1. This is occupied-area change, not evidence that individual organisms migrated or learned to follow food.

The next interval was a broad contraction, despite band 0 receiving supply. Population fell from 48 at 2,000 to 11 at 2,500, reached a minimum 8 at 2,533, then recovered to 31 at 3,000. The late body-only frames show new branching clusters and occupied area growing again. Recovery here is descriptive; there is no ancestor-versus-descendant assay demonstrating adaptation or improved fitness.

Only one of three blocks passes. The second fails because the fed band does not gain 20 tiles; the third fails because the unfed band also grows instead of losing 20 tiles. Those failures remain part of the result. The plan required one qualifying block, not reliable tracking at every switch.

The historical static split world also meets the same gain/loss screen in its 2,500–3,000 block: −164/+283. Thus the pattern also occurs without alternating supply, and this permissive screen did not distinguish its presence between designs. The new result does not establish that alternation causes growth/retreat or is superior. Seed 1203 was selected from known surviving static runs.

Both worlds were offered 460,800 food units. Alternation retained the same total budget but concentrated each pulse into one band, changing timing and local intensity together. Existing food persisted. New accepted food by band was 145,366/102,110; overflow was 85,034/128,290. Differences in accepted food prevent treating the offered-budget match as matched intake.

All three late activity windows passed. Births/deaths/cap 48 ticks were 102/90/13; 60/97/10; and 52/32/0. Both-band occupied tiles were 79/65 at 2,500 and 155/262 at 3,000, above the 10-tile continuity threshold. The tiny gold family ends with only two bodies; all-three retention should not be described as balanced.
