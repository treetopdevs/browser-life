# Which world should we deepen next?

26 October 2026. Editorial comparison of existing evidence; no new simulation or preference study. Fresh worktree from PR98 tip 637b6f59. [Watch the comparison](comparison.html) · [Validation](comparison-validation.md).

## Choice: growing bodies that must compete for food

Deepen the developmental world first. It already makes recognizable branching bodies from inherited instructions, so changes in shape can tell a visible story. Its present weakness is unusually concrete: all six recorded runs end at 47–48 bodies against a 48-body ceiling ; 931–977 eligible birth attempts per run are blocked, while 190–192 deaths are age-triggered and only 2–7 are starvation. That is a convincing body generator, but much of its turnover is imposed by the clock and ceiling.

Next, make where a body grows matter more to feeding and reproduction. Keep the current world intact as a reference. Add an explicitly engineered scarce-food comparison, with uniform versus patchy allocation at equal offered budget. The practical question is whether growth fronts, gaps and replacement become more varied and legible—not whether we have proven evolution.

## Alternatives and evidence

| World | What earns attention | Why it is not first |
|---|---|---|
| Developmental bodies | Connected branching shapes, inherited programs, seven generations; 20–25 living program variants with mutation, including potentially neutral changes | Chosen, with cap/age domination as the first limitation to expose |
| Cyclic ecosystem | Longest retained competition: five families survive 20,000 ticks in two seeds; a third loses two families | Strong baseline, but late mutations mostly revisit known combinations; another trait tweak risks more of the same |
| 3D pursuit | Real depth motion, separated groups and dispersed individuals; all nine preset/seed runs populated at6,000 | Good movement sandbox; less legible individual morphology. Moving food in 3D is a viable second choice, not yet a demonstrated improvement |
| Motifs, seasons and oases | Twelve contrasting recipes; long seasons retain three families in all three tested seeds | Useful controls and browsing palette; much visual structure stays food-centered. Not automatically continuous following or adaptive recovery |
| Bonds | Strings/open polygons versus dense knots; links continually change | Supplied transient links, without inherited group reproduction |
| Nutrient currents | Pump-enabled worlds persist through 6,000 while still controls die by 1,000 | Strongest narrow persistence intervention, but late behavior is small patches in a narrow band |
| Matter without currents | Rain/burial bookkeeping works | All recorded worlds lose biomass; do not repeat as a survival solution |
| Vision judge / regime diagnostic | Retained negative results | Remain stopped; no new search authorized by this choice |

This is a judgment about what seems most worth watching and improving. Different horizons, seeds, cameras and definitions of “alive” make these unsuitable for a pooled score or survival ranking. Numbers come from the linked validation reports, not from reading compressed pixels.

## Bounded next implementation slice

**Working name: Hungry shapes.** Separate optional environment mode and comparison page; preserve the current model/default outputs and all historical evidence. Do not remove the age limit, raise the population ceiling, introduce body movement or add predation in this first slice. Those would obscure whether food allocation alone produces a useful difference.

1. Plan/review the resource-allocation contract before coding: a deterministic scarce-uniform reference and a deterministic scarce-patch treatment, with identical offered food per pulse and initial food. Record offered, accepted and overflow separately; use the same founding sites and inherited programs. Choose the exact budget/patch geometry in the implementation plan before viewing pilot results. Do not claim equal realized intake just because offers match.
2. Keep connected-body, unique-occupancy, bounded-storage, parent/child and exact integer-energy checks. Add replay and matched-budget fixtures. Expose food clearly and distinguish starvation, age deaths and cap-blocked births.
3. First run both conditions at seed 1201 through 3,000, with mutation enabled and an unchanged original-world reference. Show early, middle and late intervals at a fixed camera. This is a creative screen, not an adaptation test. Do not silently rescue or reseed a failed world.
4. Stop immediately on accounting, connectivity, replay or provenance failure. If both new worlds empty out before a second generation, or remain ceiling-bound with no visible difference from the original, revise the food design once in a separately labeled version; retain the failed run. If that second version also fails, stop this slice and revisit3D moving food.
5. If the first screen shows continuing births and a visible difference in growth/empty-space patterns, extend the frozen version to seeds 1202 and 1203 through 3,000. Retain every outcome. Keep it as a promising prototype if at least two seeds show late births with living bodies and the late window spends less than 20% of ticks at the body ceiling. Report age deaths and blocked births even if the scene looks good. These thresholds are proposed creative retention rules, not statistical evidence of adaptation.
6. Only after the three-seed comparison is worth watching consider 10,000 ticks or changing age rules. No automatic escalation, vision search, merge or deployment.
