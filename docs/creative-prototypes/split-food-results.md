# Two feeding bands keep three families in play

The first split-band world passed the frozen 3,000-tick screen. It ended with 27 bodies divided almost evenly among three founder families: 10 mint, 8 gold and 9 lilac. Both feeding regions remained occupied and the bodies visibly reorganized late in the recording. This is a promising creative prototype on one deliberately chosen seed, not proof of improved fitness or stable coexistence.

| Seed 1202 at tick 3,000 | Bodies | Mint / gold / lilac | Programs | Maximum-ever generation |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Historical contiguous 48-column band | 28 | 0 / 26 / 2 | 11 | 14 |
| New separated 24-column bands | 27 | 10 / 8 / 9 | 10 | 15 |

The population totals are similar, but the family balance differs. All three split families remain present at every 20-tick sample. The historical contiguous world first lost mint by the tick-2,740 sample. That baseline was already known, including its later 6,000-tick result; it is not a second new outcome. The split world has not been extended beyond 3,000.

We engineered columns 0–23 and 48–71 as two feeding regions, separated by two 24-column gaps on the torus. Total replenished area and offered food match the contiguous baseline. Both begin with food everywhere; deposited food is never removed. The intervention changes physical food placement and its history. It does not isolate a general fragmentation effect or demonstrate independent colonies.

Every numerical gate passed. Occupied body tiles in the two feeding bands were 119 / 262 at 2,500 and 109 / 290 at 3,000, above the frozen minimum of 10 each. These counts show both regions are inhabited; they do not establish spatially independent populations or refuges.

The body-only comparison showed a small upper-middle group becoming a broad branching group near the upper edge. A dense lower-middle cluster cleared and separated, while a wider lower-left branching group appeared. The own-world visual change gate therefore passed. The parent knew both the historical baseline and the new numerical outcome; the receipt explicitly records this nonblind judgment. No overlap diagnostics were needed or run.

The split world accepted 271,467 of 460,800 offered units; the historical contiguous world accepted 284,691. Equal offered food and area did not produce equal usable food. The split endpoint retained more founder families, but had one fewer distinct program and accepted less food. No overall superiority or adaptation claim follows from this one trajectory.

Exactly one new split seed ran with an independent repeat. Before it, the new model's unchanged contiguous mode reproduced all accepted baseline samples, frames, tick rows and five full snapshots exactly in a separately disclosed regression check. That check wrote no new baseline artifact. No retry, extra seed, parameter change or longer run occurred.

Stop here as planned. Keep this version and propose unchanged replication at seeds 1203 and 1204 under a separate reviewed plan. The latter has no accepted contiguous baseline, so that next plan must explicitly budget and label a new control outcome or choose a justified alternative before any run. Do not silently treat a new control as historical or use these results to tune geometry. The aim is to find whether the legible groups and varied family balance recur, not to declare evolution from a favorable first seed.
