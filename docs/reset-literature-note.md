# Focused conceptual check

Date: 2026-09-29. This note informs operational distinctions; it supplies no empirical result about this simulator. Access was limited to publisher summaries/search excerpts for several items, not a complete full-text review. Numerical thresholds are our assay choices, not standards established by these sources.

- Griesemer's *Reproduction and the Reduction of Genetics* develops a joint perspective on genetics, development and reproduction. Our corresponding design choice is to measure transmission and developmental reconstruction separately from genome equality. [Publisher chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/concept-of-the-gene-in-development-and-evolution/reproduction-and-the-reduction-of-genetics/4A3F07773AC07C64117AE9E7539810D4).
- Godfrey-Smith treats bottlenecks, reproductive specialization and integration as dimensions relevant to Darwinian individuality. Our inference is to avoid a single membrane or purity criterion as a universal individuality verdict. These are diagnostic dimensions, not imported simulator acceptance thresholds. [Bottlenecks, Germ Lines, and Queen Bees](https://academic.oup.com/book/4808/chapter-abstract/147097048).
- Grosberg and Strathmann's review places multicellular life cycles, genetic conflict, chimeras and unicellular bottlenecks in the same evolutionary problem. Our design implication is that mixing and higher-level reproduction should be measured jointly rather than treated as mutually exclusive by definition. [The Evolution of Multicellularity](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.ecolsys.36.102403.114735).
- Ratcliff and colleagues experimentally distinguish cluster formation, cluster-level selection and subsequent adaptation. Our methodological inference is to distinguish physical grouping from repeated reproductive success and to preserve the difference between an imposed experimental selection regime and spontaneous origin. [Experimental evolution of multicellularity](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/3277146/).

The practical conclusion is modest: a causal, multidimensional audit is better suited to the question than equating a tracker birth with reproduction. None of these sources establishes that most offspring material must originate in the parent, that three transfers suffice for organismality, or that the current transport lottery must be replaced.
