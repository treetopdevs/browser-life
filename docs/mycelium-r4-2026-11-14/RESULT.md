# Mycelium world, rung R4: is the kept trail used, or only paid for? Result (14 Nov 2026 UTC)

**Label: TOLL-ONLY.** Under the toll rule (`moveCost` 12, strength 64) the evolved controllers show no use of the trail gradient above the pre-registered margin on any of the four endpoints (movement alignment, concentration on trail, the acute lesion effect, general output dependence): every 95% interval lies strictly inside +-15% of what a plain gradient-following rule showed on dev, the particles request the one unit that buys the discount (99.6%), and nearly every particle stands where the discount is already complete. Two analyses agree (TypeScript and an independent Python re-derivation: 0 of 1,520 readout mismatches, same statuses and label), with one declared Monte Carlo allowance exceeded on a bootstrap endpoint, described below. Everything is local and uncommitted, run on native WebGPU on one Mac; nothing is deployed and the `/mycelium/` page was not touched.

TOLL-ONLY is a statement about the directional-use readouts the protocol defines, not "nothing reads the trail": use of the local trail *level* (input 2) is untested (section "What this does not show"). It also does not say the toll is useless: the toll is what keeps the trail at all (the keyless arm kept it in 0 of 16 runs, the toll inherit arm in 15 of 16).

Plain reading: in the toll world the trail field is everywhere (about 99 units per particle, roughly half the matter of the world), the particles lay almost exactly the one unit a step that qualifies them for the discount (a median of 99.6% of particles request exactly one unit; the lowest run, inherit 7014, 81.6% with 18.4% requesting two or more), and at the cells they stand on the discount is almost always already complete (a median `sat` of 1.000; the lowest run 0.993). At the sampled locations there is therefore almost nothing left for trail-following to buy, and the evolved controllers do not follow the gradient beyond the margin: their intended movement relates to the gradient about as a blind controller's does (ALIGN -0.087 against -0.115, a difference whose interval is [-0.004, +0.038]; both negative for a mechanical reason, below), and zeroing the gradient inputs changes what they would do by about as little as it changes a random controller (SENS 0.0031 against 0.0038 for blocked, whose weights were never selected on anything). The physarum anchor, at the same deposit rate, follows the trail almost perfectly and concentrates 7 times over the field mean, which is why the readouts can see use when there is some.

The negative ALIGN baseline (about -0.1 to -0.15 for every arm that cannot or does not use the gradient) is the wake of a moving depositor: it lays trail behind it, so the gradient at its cell points back along its path. That is why only contrasts mean anything.

## What this shows

- Under the toll rule at `moveCost` 12, strength 64, the inherit arm's kept trail is a **toll, not a tool, as far as directional use goes**: on four endpoints at once the controllers' movement and output dependence on the trail gradient lie within +-15% of what a plain gradient follower at the same deposit rate showed on dev, around zero, with every 95% interval inside that margin on both sides. That is equivalence to the stated margin, not exact absence.
- The state is the floor the screen suspected: a median of 99.6% of particles request exactly one unit (inherit 7014 is the exception at 81.6%), the median `sat` is 1.000 (the discount is complete at essentially every sampled particle position, lowest run 0.993), the realised deposit is 0.9995 per particle per step. Where the discount is already complete almost everywhere, following the trail buys almost nothing at the sampled locations, which fits the null result; it is not a proof that no trail-following benefit exists anywhere in the world.
- The toll condition keeps the trail: without the discount keys (keyless, same `moveCost`), 0 of 16 runs kept it; with the toll, 15 of 16 (Fisher p 5e-8). That compares two conditions at one setting; it is not a claim about any other way of keeping trail.

## What this does not show

- **Not "nothing reads the trail".** The lesion blinds the two trail-gradient inputs. Use of input 2 (trail here, the local level) is untested: ALIGN, ACUTE and SENS cannot see a controller that, for example, eats more or splits sooner on thicker trail. OCC would see gathering, and it shows none, but a null OCC does not rule out level-based use.
- It does not show that the toll is the *only* reason the evolved controllers lay trail, or that they evolved *because* of the discount (no lineage-level fitness is measured), or what happens at other strengths, other `moveCost`s, the public mode, other worlds or horizons.
- The in-state comparison conditions on keeping the trail (15 of 16 and 13 of 16 runs). Retention is tested apart and shows no detected difference (Fisher p 0.60), but equal retention would not make the survivors comparable: the contrasts describe kept-trail worlds, not causal lesion effects among a common set of survivors.
- The margin is 15% of one anchor (a particular trail-following rule) and was fixed from 20 dev runs per arm whose own sampling error is not propagated. "Within the margin" is a statement relative to that rule, not an absolute behavioural scale. A real effect between about 4% (the detection floor, a quarter of the margin) and 15% of the anchor would be MINOR-USE or EQUIV by design; the confirmation's ALIGN interval sits inside the detection floor (upper end +0.038 against a floor of 0.041) and so does OCC's (upper end +0.005 against 0.23); ACUTE's interval is [-0.007, -0.002], below its floor of 0.037 in absolute size, and SENS's upper end 0.0040 is above its quarter-margin floor 0.0035 but SENS has no SMALL category by design: it is EQUIV whenever the upper end is below the full margin 0.0139. A true SENS value between 0.0035 and 0.0139 would therefore also read EQUIV.
- It says nothing about heredity against a trail-following null, structure, individuality or open-endedness, and it does not rescue R2b's label. The screen's earlier shaping hint of a small positive ALIGN difference (about +0.05 on three scratch seeds) did **not** reproduce: dev +0.008, confirmation +0.014.

## Two analyses

The TypeScript analysis (`r4-analyze.ts confirm`) and an independent Python re-derivation (`rederive.py`, reading the raw `series.json`, sequential accumulation, its own exact Mann-Whitney dynamic programme, numpy bootstraps, its own Holm, Fisher, guard rule, status and label logic, and an independent recomputation of the margins from the dev files) agree on 0 of 1,520 readout mismatches (bit equal), the counts, both gates, Fisher p, the matched deposit, the anchor, the floor, the guard, every shift and Cliff's delta (to 1e-12), every p and Holm-adjusted p (exact, identical), every status and the label. They share the protocol's definitions, so neither can see a flaw in the design choices, only in the execution. **One declared allowance was exceeded:** the Python script's pre-declared allowance for the bootstrap endpoints (15% of the interval width) was exceeded by 4% on the lower end of the SENS interval (TypeScript 0.00268, Python 0.00244; allowed difference 0.000237, observed 0.000247), so its summary line reads DISAGREEMENT (1): SENS. The exact (Monte Carlo free) bootstrap interval of the median of the 15 in-state SENS values is [0.002436, 0.004019] (`exact-median-ci.py`, `bootstrap-exact-check.txt`): the Python endpoint is the exact one and the TypeScript endpoint sits at the neighbouring order statistic, which a 10,000-resample bootstrap does when the 2.5% quantile falls on a probability jump of a 15-value median. The ACUTE interval agrees exactly. The status (EQUIV: the upper end 0.0040 against a margin of 0.0139) is the same either way, so no decision depends on it. The allowance is a property of `rederive.py`, which is outside the frozen manifest; it was not changed after seeing the result.
