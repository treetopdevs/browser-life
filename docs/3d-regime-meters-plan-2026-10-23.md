# Regime meters plan: where does the slab sit dynamically? (2026-10-23)

Candidate 8 of the 3D line proposes a diagnostic, not a new rule: before anything is searched or redesigned, measure whether the slab's dynamics are ordered, critical or chaotic. *Before redesigning the car, check that the engine is running: tap the world and see whether the ripple fades, explodes or wanders.* M4 found no setting of the slab that is neither dead or sparse nor a carpet (`docs/3d-rung1b-plan-2026-10-23.md`, amendment of 2026-10-23); this note asks whether the dynamics themselves say why, and which constant moves them. It fixes the meters, the sweep, the ground-truth gate and the decision rule before any code is written or any run is made, as every check in this repo is.

The normative rule is `runs/regime/rule-draft.md` (draft 2 with the main session's decisions of 2026-10-24 applied, in the gitignored `runs/`; the main session freezes it after Stage 0 and before the held-out gate, commits the frozen copy as `experiments/3d-slab/regime-rule.md` and records its SHA-256 in `experiments/3d-slab/REGIME-FROZEN` and `runs/regime/RULE.sha256`). This note pins the same values and says why. Where they differ, the rule wins. Code references are to the tree at `caf9a431`. Nothing is implemented and no gate or sweep job has been run for this plan; the numbers quoted from smoke tests, timings and pilots come from read-only probes and pilots on development seeds (`runs/regime/probe/` and `runs/regime/review/`, gitignored) and are labelled as such. **This is the third version of the note.** The first was reviewed by Codex (20 findings) and by an adversarial reviewer who also ran pilots on seeds 9001 to 9004 (20 findings); the second version left ten decisions open for the main session, and this one applies the decisions the main session took on 2026-10-24 (group D joins the sweep, which becomes 32 settings and 96 jobs; the rule is cleaned of draft history). The build was then reviewed and fixed, and the main session answered the review's open decisions on 2026-10-24: the 2D reference worlds are read at 50,000 and 100,000 steps only, the dust floor and the threshold trigger are read per rung, no threshold is ever re-derived, and a ranking with no candidate is NO-RANKING (section 13, "The build review"). Section 13 lists every finding, its verdict and what was done, the decisions, and the draft history and pilot figures moved out of the rule.

## 0. What gets built and decided

- **Observer-only.** New files only. No physics, no `RULE_VERSION`, no golden pin, no `WorldConfig` key changes (section 1).
- **Three meters** on twin runs (a device-side fork of the reference, one side kicked): a 1,000-step **trajectory divergence** with a fitted exponent, a **Derrida dose-response** (a response ratio pooled over small doses, decided at step 30), and a **spatial entropy rate** of the biomass field (descriptive). Damage is a **coarse-grained block count** (blocks of 8 x 8 x 8 voxels whose net biomass differs by at least half a kick, or in which at least 8 voxels differ; the development pilot picks which); voxel-level counts, the genome-set divergence and the census divergence are columns (sections 3 and 4). The classes are **finite-amplitude, same-noise, block-scale response classes**, not the infinitesimal-slope phases of the literature (section 1).
- **Four controls** on every metered job: Z (no kick: the instrument is exact), U (a kick an eighth the size or less: only responses that depend on the size of the kick vote), N (the same state under another noise seed: the ceiling of complete decorrelation) and, in the gate, F (the same state with advection and motility off: a frozen scar, the ORDERED end) (section 3.4).
- **A sweep** of 32 settings x 3 seeds = 96 slab jobs at 256 x 256 x 8 (groups A to D and the `mutRate` control), burn-in 20,000 steps, 54 of whose jobs overlay M4's runs (section 5).
- **A development pilot (Stage 0) and a held-out ground-truth gate.** Stage 0 runs the instrument on development seeds (9001 to 9003) on the gate's worlds, picks the rung and decider by a pre-stated order and checks that no decision rests on a coin flip. The gate (seeds 101 to 103) then tests that one combination once: a known-dead slab, a known carpet, the project's two registered 2D alive worlds read at 50,000 and 100,000 steps, a dust control, and the N and F controls at the two ends. If the meters do not separate them, or only through a response that ignores the size of the kick, the sweep is not read (section 6).
- **A decision rule** with an outcome for every case: invalid run, gate failure, no contrast, band found, transient band, late check inconclusive, empty window, too many unmeterable, thin pool, sensitive everywhere, under-supported, unresolved, crossing bracketed, no band, ties (section 7).
- **About 4.0 hours** nominal on this Mac for the base programme (Stage 0 0.6 h, gate 0.5 h, main stage 2.8 h, captures 0.1 h), about 7.7 hours loaded; plus 12 to 18 minutes per late-checked target setting (at most 6) and about 6 minutes per extended setting; the work Mac is not needed (section 9).

## 1. Scope (pin 1)

**Observer-only.** The sweep calls the existing sims and changes nothing they compute. Concretely:

Every other path must show an empty diff, in particular everything under `packages/schema/src/` (all of it is re-exported by `index.ts:1-18` and feeds the dynamics: `int.ts` holds the PRNG and `mulFrac`, and `layout.ts`, `layout3d.ts`, `kernel.ts`, `kernel3d.ts`, `genome.ts`, `genome3.ts`, `presets.ts`, `founders.ts`, `genetics.ts`, `sun.ts`, `config.ts`, `world.ts`, `world3.ts`, `accounting.ts` too), `packages/sim-ref/src/`, `packages/sim-gpu/src/` apart from the new file, and `packages/sim-ref/test/golden-hashes.test.ts`. New code reaches the sims only through their public fields (`device`, `n`, `cells`, `currentGenome`, `ledger`, `step`, `run`, `encodeStep`, the readers), as the probes do.
- **Pins.** All 16 slab pins and every 2D pin stay byte-identical. **`pnpm test` covers 11 of the 16 slab pins**: the five 64 x 64 x 8 cases are skipped unless `BL_SLAB_BIG=1` (`golden-hashes.test.ts:88-103`; `docs/3d-rung1b-step-spec.md:539`). So `pnpm test` is the check at every milestone, and before the final acceptance the full set is run: `BL_SLAB_BIG=1 pnpm vitest run packages/sim-ref/test/golden-hashes.test.ts` and `deno run -A --no-lock tests/deno/gpu_golden.ts` (both are lengthy; neither was run in this phase). `defaultConfig()` gains no key (the sweep uses keys that already exist: `defaultMu`, `defaultSigma`, `lightK`, `diffA`, `diffC`, `diffS`, `gateK`, `dtQ` and `spread` (group D), and `mutRate` for one control; the controls use `seed`, `dtQ` and `motility`).
- **`kernelRadius` is fixed at 3 for the slab** (`validateSlab`, `config.ts:775`; `buildKernel3(3)` is a literal at `shaders3d.ts:57` and `:220` and `step3d.ts:170`; the affinity tile `AFF3_SW = [14, 14, 10]` is 7,840 B, and R = 4 would be 12,288 B, past the Deno single-declaration cliff near 8 KiB: `docs/3d-rung1a-bench-2026-10-22.md`, the section "The Deno workgroup-storage cliff"). An R sweep is therefore a kernel change (shaders, `RefSim3`, tile, pins, `RULE_VERSION`) and **out of scope**. The readout lists R under "not swept", with `thetaMass`, `massUnit`, `kCatHalf`, every `k*` rate, `mutRate` (except one control), `mutStep`, the energies, light base and amplitude, seasons and `tileD`.

**What the meters can and cannot support.** The candidate says to find the band where divergence "grows sub-exponentially but doesn't vanish", and that finding none is a "first-principles proof" the physics must change. The literature pass behind this plan says three things that change the design, and the reviews of draft 1 added a fourth:

1. Under a local rule the number of damaged voxels can grow at most polynomially (a light cone), so "sub-exponential and not vanishing" holds in the chaotic regime too and cannot separate regimes (Bagnoli, Rechtman and Ruffo 1992; Wolfram 1984; Mitchell, Hraber and Crutchfield 1993). What does separate them is the **multiplication factor** (the slope of damage after tau steps against initial damage, at the origin), the **front speed** of thresholded damage, and, at a critical point, the power-law decay of damage survival (Derrida and Pomeau 1986; Torcini, Grassberger and Politi 1995; Hinrichsen 2000). The only numeric line the literature supports is "multiplication factor = 1"; every window around it is a judgement call, and this plan says so wherever it uses one.
2. Linking criticality to evolvability is contested (Mitchell et al. 1993; Touboul and Destexhe 2010). So the outcome of this plan is **a pre-stated trigger for a physics investigation, not a proof**; the word "proof" is retired, and the "which constant" ranking is an ordering by effect on the meters, not a causal claim.
3. Damage spreading depends on how noise is shared between the two copies (Hinrichsen, Weitz and Domany 1997). Here every PRNG draw is keyed on (seed, step, cell) (`cellBase`, `draw`, `int.ts:40-46`), so a twin and its reference share all noise. Every reading is **same-noise damage** and says nothing about other couplings.
4. **The classes are finite-amplitude.** The smallest kick here is 12 units on a voxel that holds about 12 to 40: 92 percent of the biomass of a voxel with B = 13 and 31 percent of one with B = 39 (the densest voxel the original probe saw; the later pilot's maximum was 45), and the ball kick halves the biomass of up to 123 voxels. Each class is therefore the response of this world to a kick of that size, counted in blocks of 8 x 8 x 8 voxels over at most 1,000 steps, and the two kick sizes (the rungs) can give different classes for one world: in the pilot the carpet spread damage over about half its blocks by step 100 under the one-voxel kick, while under the ball kick it left a flat scar of 17 to 40 damaged blocks (as reported by the review). Nothing here estimates a slope at the origin, and no independently labelled critical world exists: the controls give an ordered end (F, a frozen scar) and a chaotic end (N, the noise-swapped twin), and a CRITICAL reading means "between them, by the rule's window". The gate labels each reference class AMP-ROBUST or AMP-DEPENDENT (it reads both rungs anyway) and the criticality wording is printed only for AMP-ROBUST.

**Where "ordered", "critical" and "chaotic" are defined here** (sections 4.2 and 4.3 give the numbers): *ordered*, a kick is absorbed or frozen (the damage count has fallen below a tenth of its start by step 1,000, or is shrinking faster than t^-0.5, or the multiplier at step 30 is below one half, or the damage persists but its extent does not grow: a scar); *critical*, a kick leaves about one damaged thing that spreads slowly (multiplier within a factor 2 of one at step 30, flat within t^+-0.5 to step 1,000, extent growing by at least a block); *chaotic*, a kick is amplified (multiplier above 2 at step 30, or growing faster than t^0.5, or spread to 5 percent of the living blocks with at least a doubling per site).

## 2. What the code says about damage in this world

These facts, from reading `step3d.ts`, the probe and the development pilots, fix the design choices that follow.

- **Twins share their noise.** All draws are `draw(cellBase(seed, step, i), slot)` with the step from the ledger word; there is no stream position. Which draws are consumed can depend on state (the mutation draw is taken only when `newB > 0`, `step3d.ts:636-646`) but a draw's value never does. Noise is attached to cell indices, so a twin forked from the same state with the same config stays bit-identical until something differs. Event slots are appended in arbitrary order (`shaders3d.ts:752-758`) but nothing in the physics reads them: a twin drained twice and a twin never drained had equal `stateHash` at step 800 (probe `twin.json`), and an undrained slab's event counter reaches at most 524,288 x 1,000 = 524,288,000, below the 2^31 that would set the saturation flag, so draining the twins is unnecessary. Because the generator is stateless, every job is deterministic: a rerun cannot change an outcome (section 7).
- **A fork is complete.** The state is `cells[0]`, the current genome buffer and the ledger; `U`, `disp`, `light` and `roles` are derived each step. The probe forked on the device (`copyBufferToBuffer` of `cells[0]`, `currentGenome` into both genome buffers, and the ledger; then `to.step = from.step`) in 14.8 ms at 128 x 128 x 8 and 24.6 ms at 256 x 256 x 8, and the twins had equal hashes after 50 steps. The ledger copy carries the undrained event counter, so the reference is drained first. The N twin (another `seed`) and the F pair (`dtQ` 0, motility off) are not forks in this sense: they are created with the overridden configuration and filled by the same copies, as the pilot built its N twin (`create({...state, cfg})`, no `upload`), and their checks differ (section 3.3).
- **How far a difference can travel per step.** Transport reads 27 sources whose displacement depends on `U` (mass within the kernel radius 3) and on mass gradients (Sobel, 1), so a difference in a voxel can change the state up to 5 voxels away in x and y after one step (kernel 3 + Sobel 1 + transport 1), and the whole column in z through the light pass (`lightColumn`, `step3d.ts:477`). That is an upper bound. In the probe's sparse 128 x 128 x 8 world a one-unit kick changed 1, 29, 100 and 190 B words at t = 0, 10, 100 and 300.
- **Integer transport keeps small quanta where they are.** The transport shares are three nested floors, x then y then z (`share3`, `step3d.ts:70-76`; WGSL `shaders3d.ts:371-377`), and every quantum not sent stays at its source (`step3d.ts:380-405`). At rest (zero displacement) the per-axis weights are 8, 64, 8 over 80 (`w1d` with `spread` 8) and a voxel keeps this much of its biomass in one step, recomputed from `share3`: all of it for B <= 16 (12 stays 12, 13 stays 13, 16 stays 16), 18 of 20, 23 of 27, 24 of 30, 29 of 39, 42 of 60 and 64 of 100. Draft 1's continuum value `(64/80)^3 = 0.512` is reached nowhere in this world's range (B is median 12, 90th percentile 25 and maximum 45 in the pilot). A kick on a low-B voxel is therefore retained passively, and only displacement (`dtQ`, motility, the Sobel terms) moves it. Draft 1's argument that a purely passive kick leaves at most 0.39 of itself in its block at step 100, so that a block still damaged at step 100 "was sustained or amplified by the dynamics", is **withdrawn**: a persistent block deficit is not evidence of amplification. The frozen control F (section 3.4) and the FROZEN form of the trajectory class (section 4.2) exist because of this.
- **Rounding creates dust, and block sums do not cancel it once the cascade has spread.** Transport shares are deterministic floors (`mulShareD`, `step.ts:789`); reactions use stochastic rounding with shared draws (`mulFrac`, `int.ts:52`). A one-unit difference becomes a cloud of plus-or-minus-one voxels: in the probe L1 of B went from 1 to 473 in 300 steps from a one-unit kick, while the mean difference per damaged voxel was 1.3 to 2.5. And kicks of 1, 4 and 16 units left 190, 175 and 183 differing B words at t = 300 (100, 84 and 91 at t = 100; L1 of B 473, 397 and 442): by step 100 the voxel-level response does not depend on how big the kick was. Draft 1 therefore decided on **block** counts, expecting block sums to cancel the plus and minus parts of the dust. The development pilot (seeds 9001 to 9004, rung P1, net block count, 12 to 16 kicks of 12 units against the same sites at 1 unit, and a twin under another noise seed) says that this holds only until the cascade has spread:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| slab B0 `mu30-s8-k256` | 330 | 15 / 26 / 171 | 0 / 5 / 149 | 172 / 247 / 271 | 0.19 | 0.87 |

  Seeds 9003 and 9004 give the same picture for the 2D worlds (U / S 0.22 to 0.63 at step 30, 0.85 to 0.91 at step 100). At step 100 a one-unit kick at twelve sites damages 45 percent of B0's living blocks, the 12-unit kick 52 percent, and the noise-swapped twin 82 percent: the block count at step 100 is saturated and does not depend on the size of the kick. At step 30 it still does, by a factor of 2 to 5 for an 8-fold smaller kick (an interpretation, not tested: for an exponentially amplified perturbation a smaller kick delays the cascade by a number of steps that grows only with the logarithm of the ratio). Doses of 8, 32 and 128 kicks in B0 gave 17, 71 and 145 damaged blocks at step 30 (2.4, 2.4 and 1.5 per kicked block, the 128-kick dose already marking 99 blocks, 30 percent of the living blocks) and 143, 201 and 211 at step 100. So draft 2 decides at step 30 (TAU_DEC), evaluates the dust control there, drops the doses that mark more than a tenth of the living blocks, and tests saturation with a doubling as well as a coverage (section 4).
- **Living is `B + P > 0`.** Transport clears the lineage of a voxel that receives nothing and the lottery gives a lineage only to voxels that receive bound mass; react clears a living voxel whose B and P reach 0 (`step3d.ts:420-422`, `:662`). So the living-status Hamming can be computed from the cells buffer alone, and the plan checks the identity against `readStats().living` (check C7). It also means a world can die inside the twin phase, which draft 1 did not handle (status DIED_IN_PHASE, section 3.1).
- **The scale of B is small and state-dependent.** The nutrient is 32 per voxel and the world is closed, but that is an average resource level and not a bound on B: the pilot's B0 state at 20,000 steps has B quantiles 5, 9, 12, 21, 25, 30 and 45 at 10, 25, 50, 75, 90, 99 percent and the maximum (the original probe saw 39), and the scale in carpets is not known. The kick and the threshold are therefore derived from the state (the median B of living voxels), not fixed; and a carpet whose B is 12 or less everywhere would have no eligible voxel (status NO_TARGET, which makes the gate stop with CARPET-UNDEFINED rather than prove the meters wrong).
- **The states M4 reached are transients.** `mu30 s8 k256` rises from 0.06 to 0.44 living between step 20,000 and 50,000 (`m4-results.tsv`); at 20,000 steps 23 of the 54 M4-overlay jobs of this sweep are in the livable window (1 to 85 percent living), at 50,000 steps only 5 of the same 54 are. A meter on these states reads a stage of a transient. The plan says so, and a late check at 50,000 steps decides whether a band is standing or transient (sections 3.5, 7). Of the 54 overlay jobs only B0 (seeds 101 to 103 at 0.44, 0.45 and 0.51 living at 50,000) has two or more windowed seeds at 50,000 steps (the other two windowed jobs there are single seeds of `mu30 s16 k256` and `mu30 s20 k1024`), so among the overlay settings only B0 can reach BAND-FOUND by the three-seed vote at T1; any other BAND-FOUND comes from the settings of groups C and D, which M4 never ran (checked against M4's exact JSON traces in the Codex review).
- **The cascade is carried by advection through integer thresholds.** On the seed-9001 B0 state (300 steps, reported by the adversarial review; the printed output of `variants.ts` was not saved): `dtQ` = 0 froze the cascade (the 12-unit kick stayed in 12 of 12 blocks at steps 10, 30 and 100; the one-unit kick's differing voxels were 26, 22 and 20); `spread` = 0 slowed it (`n_1(100)` 378 against 23,646); switching all three dissolved diffusions off left it unchanged (23,793); `mutRate` 0 left the block series identical to step 100 (S 171, U 149 in both runs, saved in `twins-B0-s9001-t20000-nomut.json`). The sweep therefore carries `dtQ` and `spread` as group D, and its group C (diffusion and gate constants) probably cannot move the cascade (section 5).
- **mu and sigma are a confound.** `defaultMu` and `defaultSigma` move three things at once: the founder genome (`generalistGenome3(mu, sigma)`), the neutral reference words, and the growth field of every non-living voxel. In the evolving arm a living voxel reads its own mutating PARAM0. The sweep keeps them coupled on purpose so the overlay with M4 is exact; a decoupled arm is a follow-up.

## 3. The twin protocol (pins 3 and 8)

### 3.1 A job, its burn-in and its status

A job is (world, setting, seed, start step T, rung). The burn-in builds the world exactly as `experiments/3d-slab/m4-calibrate.ts` does (`presetConfig(slab-spots, seed, overrides)` then `initWorld`), runs in blocks of 1,000 steps in batches of 100 with `drainLedger` and `readStats` after each block, and stops at extinction. **T0 = 20,000 steps** for every slab job (the registered 2D reference worlds are read at T1 = 50,000 and T2 = 100,000 steps only, because they are still filling at 20,000: section 3.5 and rule R1); the late check (section 7) re-runs the settings that matter to T1 = 50,000.

At T the job gets a status (rule R3), in this order: **EXTINCT** (no living voxel at any 1,000-step check: no twins, no class); **NO_TARGET** (fewer than 64 eligible voxels, section 3.2, or no voxel with B >= 1: no twins, no class); otherwise **METERED**, with flag **CARPET** if the living fraction f is at least 0.85 and **RELIC** if f is below 0.01. The **livable window** is 0.01 <= f < 0.85. Three more statuses are settled during or after the twin phase: **FEW_SITES** (fewer than 6 sites placed: the trajectory meter is off), **FEW_COLONIES** (the sites lie in fewer than 2 colonies: the trajectory class is NA), **FEW_DOSES** (fewer than 2 doses pass the cap, section 3.4: no class), then at step T + 1,000 **DIED_IN_PHASE** (the reference's living count reached 0 inside the phase: a world that dies reads "ordered" because both copies die) and **DRIFT** (the living fraction left the window during the phase). The meters never crash on a dead world because the runner checks `living` before it reads anything else, every denominator that can be zero is floored or NA (R0), and a dead or full world can never read as critical: only jobs inside the window at both ends, with a dust-clear response, vote in a setting's class (R8). Carpets and relics are still metered, so the gate and the map show what they read.

### 3.2 Scale: the kick and the thresholds come from the state

`B*` is the lower median of B over the voxels with B >= 1 at T. Then

- the kick size is `k = max(12, floor(B* / 2))` (half a typical voxel's biomass, at least 12 quanta);
- the **block threshold**, which decides, is `thetaB = ceil(k / 2)`; the **voxel threshold**, a column and the threshold of the occupancy decider, is `thetaV = max(2, ceil(k / 4))`;
- the eligible voxels are those with `B >= k + 1` (they stay living after the kick); `N_E` is their number.

A **block** is 8 x 8 columns by all layers, aligned to the grid (1,024 blocks in the slab). `dB_blk` is the signed sum over a block of the twin's B minus the reference's B, and under the **net-sum decider** a block is damaged if `|dB_blk| >= thetaB`. Under the **occupancy decider** a block is damaged if at least `OCC_V` = 8 of its voxels have `|dB| >= thetaV`. Which decider is used is chosen by Stage 0 (section 6); both are always measured.

- *Why blocks decide.* The voxel-level response forgets the kick by step 100 (section 2). A block sum was expected to cancel the plus and minus parts of rounding dust inside the block while counting differences in where biomass is, or how much there is, at the scale of an organism. The pilot found that it does so only until the cascade has spread (about 30 steps): by step 100 the one-unit twin damaged 45 percent of B0's living blocks against 52 percent for the 12-unit twin. That is why the deciding time is step 30 and why the dust control (G6) is a gate condition and a per-job gate on voting (section 6).
- *What `thetaB` is, and what a persistent deficit is not.* `thetaB = ceil(k / 2)` is a judgement constant with no derivation. Draft 1 argued from passive transport (at most 0.39 of a kick left in its block at step 100, so that a block still damaged then was sustained or amplified); that argument is **withdrawn** (section 2): the integer transport keeps all of B at rest when B <= 16, so a kick on a low-B voxel is retained passively and a persistent deficit says nothing about amplification. Three things are read separately: retention (persistent count, no growth of extent: the FROZEN form, checked against the frozen control F), rounding propagation (a response that does not depend on the size of the kick: the dust control) and amplification (extent grows and the size depends on the kick).
- *Why `k` is at least 12.* It keeps `thetaB` at least 6, above the few units by which dust crossing a block boundary can shift a block sum before the cascade has spread (the pilot's 1-unit twin had 5 of 331 blocks at or above 6 units at step 30, 149 at step 100). That is an estimate, and Stage 0 prints the measured dust floor (median `|dB_blk|` of the U twin at step 30 and the share of living blocks it damages) next to `thetaB`; the go/no-go requires the share to stay below the saturation coverage. If the median exceeds `thetaB`, or the share exceeds that coverage, the threshold trigger fires in that rung's bank and the rung's two combinations fail (`DUST-THRESHOLD`, with `DUST-FLOOR` as well when the share is the cause); `thetaB` is never re-derived from the U or N twin (decided 2026-10-24; R9.0 and section 13). `thetaV` is at least 2 because exact differences of 1 are rounding dust.

Both thresholds are recorded per job with `B*`, `k` and `N_E`, and the readout prints them so a reader can see the scale each setting was read at.

### 3.3 The kick, its ledger entry, and what is checked (pin 3)

The kick (**P1**) at a site v: `B_v <- B_v - k`, `C_v <- C_v + k`, and the ledger's `heatOut` rises by `k * (eB - eC)` (8 per unit with the default energies). It touches no other channel and no genome, lineage or MOT word. It is the decay law `B -> C` (`bdecay` in `react`, which exports `eB - eC` per unit as heat) applied by hand, with `applyLesion` (`step.ts:879-901`) as the template for the accounting. Matter is conserved exactly and so is the energy ledger. A site must have `B >= k + 1`, so the voxel stays living and `validateState` (`world.ts:159`) passes.

How it is applied: the reference R is drained, read once (`readCells`), the sites chosen (section 3.4), and each twin is a device-side fork of R. The kick is two whole-channel `queue.writeBuffer` writes (B and C, modified on the CPU) and one write of the two `heatOut` words, queued before the next submission, so it lands between steps. The probe checked this route against `readState`, modify, `upload`: equal `stateHash` after 300 steps for k = 1, 4 and 16, ledger residual 0, matter delta 0, `validateState` clean; with the heat entry left out the residual was exactly `-k * (eB - eC)` (-32 for k = 4), which is the unit test's negative control.

The twin is "otherwise identical" by construction for the Z, S, U and dose twins: the same `cfg` object, the same seed and step (the ledger copy carries the step word), the same genome and cells. The N twin and the F pair are controls whose configuration differs from R's in a stated key (section 3.4); check C8 verifies that and nothing else about them is assumed. The checks, any of which makes a job **INVALID** (excluded, named; the readout's outcome is INVALID-RUN and it exits non-zero):

| C2 | for R and every twin (N and F included) at the end of its run: matter equals its initial total and `ledgerResidual` is 0, both against **R's own** initial totals | `totalsOf`, `ledgerResidual` (`accounting.ts:32, 48`); the N and F configurations are never rebuilt with `initWorld` |

### 3.4 Sites, controls and the twin bank

At T the job runs R and up to nine twins (Z, S, U, N and up to five doses) in lockstep, one diff pass per twin per step; a gate or Stage 0 job also runs the frozen pair F:

- **Z**, no kick, 100 steps: the zero control (C1).
- **U**, the unit-kick (dust) control, 300 steps: the same kicked set as S with a kick at most an eighth the size (`kU = max(1, floor(k / 8))`, so at most k / 8, and 1 for k from 12 to 15; under P2 `max(1, floor(k_v / 8))` at every voxel S kicks, so U is never empty). At step 30 the ratio of U's damage to S's is the **dust state** (R6.12): CLEAR below 0.25, LIMITED at 0.5 or more (the response does not depend on the size of the kick: rounding dust or sensitive dependence, which this comparison cannot tell apart), AMBIG between. **Only CLEAR jobs vote** in the sweep and the gate needs two CLEAR jobs in each group (section 6). A U dose above a quarter of the S dose makes U unavailable.
- **S**, the trajectory twin, 1,000 steps: up to `M_S = 16` sites, chosen by a seeded hard-core rule. With `h0 = lowbias32((imul(seed, 7919) ^ T) >>> 0)`, try candidates `E[draw(h0, j) % N_E]` for j = 0, 1, ... (at most 4,096 tries) and accept one if its horizontal Chebyshev torus distance (x, y; z ignored) to every accepted site is at least `D_SEP = 40`. Sixteen is far below the packing limit of 36 on a 256-wide torus, but the limit that binds is the shape of the livable region: the pilot's B0 placed 12 sites using all 4,096 tries, in 3 colonies (connected components of living columns) of 10,856, 5,854 and 609 columns, and a 79 x 79 patch (9.5 percent of the world) hosts at most four. Sites in one colony share its fate, so stability is bootstrapped over **colonies**, not sites (R7). Fewer than 6 accepted sites make the job FEW_SITES (the trajectory meter is off; the Derrida meter still runs); sites in fewer than 2 colonies make it FEW_COLONIES (the trajectory class is NA). Inadequate spatial support is reported as such (outcome UNDER-SUPPORTED when it dominates the pool) and is never read as the absence of a band.
- **Doses**, 300 steps each: `m` in {4, 8, 16, 32, 64} kicks at the first `m` voxels of the eligible list sorted by the key `lowbias32((v ^ h0) >>> 0)`, so the sets are nested. A dose is **run** only if `m <= N_E / 2` and the blocks it kicks, `n0(m)`, are at most a tenth of the living blocks; the **origin set** is the doses run (at least two are needed, else FEW_DOSES). Draft 1's doses went up to 8,192 and its origin set was the three smallest, so in B0 at 20,000 steps (330 living blocks) the 128-kick dose marked 99 blocks, 30 percent of the living blocks, and carried 73 percent of the pooled denominator (7 + 30 + 99 kicked blocks), and in the same setting at 10,000 steps (seed 9002, 170 living blocks) it marked 87 blocks, half of them: it was not at the origin. With the cap the pooled ratio is made of kicks that leave the world mostly untouched, and the plotted x-axis is the kicked-block fraction.
- **N**, 300 steps, no kick: R's state under the configuration with another `seed`, so every stochastic rounding and lottery draws differently. Its damage is the **ceiling** that complete decorrelation of the noise gives at the same block scale; the ratio of S's damage to it is a descriptive column. In the gate N must saturate (G7).
- **F** (gate and Stage 0 jobs only), 1,000 steps: a pair of copies of R's state under `dtQ` 0 and `motility` false (R_F the reference, X_F carrying the S kick). With zero displacement a voxel with B <= 16 sends nothing (section 2), so the kick stays where it is: F is the instrument's own picture of a frozen scar. In the gate its trajectory class must be ORDERED (decaying or frozen), never CRITICAL or CHAOTIC (G7).

Per-site attribution: a damaged block belongs to the S site nearest its centre (horizontal Chebyshev torus distance in voxels, ties to the lower index, no cutoff). The sites are at least 40 voxels (5 blocks) apart, so each has its own block, and a site's damage stays separate while it is within 20 voxels; a site is **spread** if its damage ever reaches 20, and a job in which more than a quarter of the sites are spread is **OVERLAP**: its exponent and survival are dropped (a saturated reading still stands) and its Derrida point kept (R6.4, R6.5, R7). How far a cascade reaches is not known from the probe, which recorded counts and not positions (draft 1's "radius of at most about 8 voxels" from 190 differing words was a non sequitur: 190 columns can lie at any distance); the pilot's one-unit cascade had a radius of about 13 voxels at step 100 (reported by the review), so overlap is expected when damage genuinely spreads, where it is itself a reading.

Ten sims of 235 MiB each (R, Z, S, U, N and five doses) are about 2.35 GiB at 256 x 256 x 8. A gate job also holds a frozen copy of R for the second rung (+1 sim) and runs the F pair before the main bank and releases it (+2; R2.6 puts it first, and the runner does). The adapter's 20 GB figure is its maximum single-buffer size, not a statement about aggregate memory, so M1 measures the allocation of the full bank; if a device refuses, the runner keeps one frozen copy of the state at T and runs the doses one at a time against fresh forks of it (+2,100 sim-steps, about +14 s per job at the measured lockstep rate).

### 3.5 Start times, and owning the transient (pin 8)

Every slab job has **one start time, T0 = 20,000** (the gate's 2D reference worlds are read at 50,000 and 100,000 steps instead, because the registered 2D worlds are still filling at 20,000: rule R1 and R0.2 item 4). This is a choice with a cost: the settings nearest the carpet are mid-transient at 20,000 (`mu40 s8 k256` is 59 to 75 percent living and `mu30 s8 k256` 6 to 21 percent, both still rising), so the meters read a stage of a transient. It is made because the livable window is populated at 20,000 (23 of the 54 M4-overlay jobs at 20,000 steps, against 5 of the same 54 at 50,000) and because M4's own labels are transients too (it judged step 50,000 while two of three nearest-miss seeds were still rising). The late check of section 7 then asks, for each setting that reads in the target class at 20,000, whether it still does at 50,000: **BAND-FOUND needs the 50,000-step reading to agree, otherwise the outcome is TRANSIENT-BAND**, which is reported as such and is still a place to look. The late check re-runs the burn-in from scratch (about 4 to 6 minutes per job) for at most six target settings, ordered B0 first and then by the fewest changed axes; the runner keeps no checkpoints because a slab run refuses them (`slabSpecError`, `runner.ts:553-577`) and a 117 MB state per job is not worth saving. (Continuing every windowed job from its T0 twin phase to T1 instead would avoid the re-burn but cost about 2.5 hours for the sixty-odd windowed jobs, against 12 to 18 minutes per target setting.) A late check whose evidence is insufficient ends as LATE-INCONCLUSIVE and is not rerun: every draw is a function of seed, step and cell, so a rerun returns the same jobs.

### 3.6 Fallback rung P2 (balls)

A single-voxel kick may be too small to show anything above the dust in some states, or the block net sum may be blind to what the kick did. The rule therefore pre-states a second rung, **P2** (a ball wound), and a second decider (the occupancy count, section 4.1), for Stage 0 to select among. The site becomes a ball of radius 3 (the founders' size; at most 123 voxels) and the **kicked set** of a twin is the **union** of its balls' voxels with `B >= 3`, each kicked **once** by `floor(B / 2)` of its value in the frozen reference at T (never recomputed from a partly kicked state, so a voxel inside three balls cannot be kicked three times and the unsigned B channel cannot underflow), with the matching heat entry. Doses are 4 to 64 balls centred on the same nested keyed voxels, unseparated, with the same union rule and the same cap on kicked blocks. The thresholds `thetaB` (half the median kicked-block damage at step 0) and `thetaV` (half the median per-voxel kick) come from the **S twin's** kick set and are used by **every** twin of the job (a ball puts hundreds of units and tens of damaged voxels in a block, and thresholds of a few units would count dust); the multiplier is taken against the number of kicked blocks (R6.3). The unit-kick control kicks `max(1, floor(k_v / 8))` at every voxel S kicks (draft 1 kicked only where `k_v >= 8`, so a ball of voxels with B = 13 to 15 left U unperturbed and the dust control passed vacuously). Stage 0 runs both rungs on the same states and evaluates the four combinations of rung and decider in the order (P1, net), (P1, occ), (P2, net), (P2, occ); the first that passes with margins is fixed in the rule for the held-out gate and the whole sweep, else candidate 8 closes at Stage 0 (section 6). P2 costs one more twin phase per gate job and nothing extra in the sweep.

## 4. The meters (pins 4 to 7)

### 4.1 Damage columns, and which one decides (pin 4)

Per step, from one GPU diff pass of a twin against R (section 8.2, one workgroup per block):

- **Block damage, the two candidate deciders.** *Net sum:* `n_blk` (blocks with `|dB_blk| >= thetaB`, where `dB_blk` is the signed sum of the twin's B minus the reference's B over the block), `n_blk2` (`>= 2 thetaB`), `L1c` (the sum over blocks of `|dB_blk|`, the coarse L1) and `liveblk_R` (blocks with a living voxel in the reference, for normalising). *Occupancy:* `n_occ` (blocks in which at least `OCC_V` = 8 voxels have `|dB| >= thetaV`). Stage 0 chooses which one is `n_dec`, the deciding count (section 6).
- **Voxel (fine) damage, columns only:** `n_thetaV` (voxels with `|dB| >= thetaV`), `n_2thetaV`, `n_1` (`|dB| >= 1`, the dust level), `n_live` (living status differs), `n_vox` (exact Hamming: any of A, B, C, P, E, S, MOT differs), `L1_A, L1_B, L1_C, L1_P` (sum of `min(|d|, 1023)`, the voxel L1 matter distance), `SB_R` and `live_R` (for normalising) and the twin's own `SB_X` and `live_X` (for the census divergence, section 4.6).
- Per S site: the attributed count of damaged blocks (for each decider) and the reach in voxels.

**`n_dec` decides every class**, and it is a block count. Exact Hamming is a light-cone count that grows in every regime of a local rule (1, 29, 100, 190 differing B words at t = 0, 10, 100, 300 in the probe) and discriminates nothing. The voxel L1 and the voxel counts include rounding dust and forget the kick (section 2), and the sum over species lets the healing of one species show as the growth of another. Living status flickers at colony edges. The other columns are printed beside the decider: the voxel counts show the fine structure of the same damage, the coarse L1 `L1c` its total net amount, and the relative L1 `L1_B / SB_R` (damage as a fraction of the reference's biomass) is the growth-corrected view for the transient states.

**The two block counts see different things, and neither is known to be right here.** The *net sum* counts blocks whose net biomass differs by half a kick or more; the plus and minus parts of the dust inside a block cancel in it, but not once the cascade has spread (section 2), and a persistent count is not evidence of amplification (the integer transport retains a kick on a low-B voxel). It is also **blind to rearrangement at constant block density**: two twins with the same biomass in every block, organisms displaced by a voxel or two, or different lineages in the same places, read `n_blk = 0`, which is ORDERED. The project's alive 2D worlds (spots that regenerate after lesions) may well be like that. The *occupancy count* sees that rearrangement, but it also sees the dust cloud, so it is the more likely to fail the dust control. Stage 0 therefore tries both, net first (section 6).

### 4.2 Trajectory divergence and its exponent (pin 5)

- **Window and cadence.** 1,000 steps from T. The GPU logs every step; the fit uses a geometric grid `t_j` = 100, 139, 193, 268, 373, 518, 720, 1000 (seven intervals per decade), each point the mean of the pooled series over steps `t_j - 5` to `t_j + 5` (clipped to [1, 1000]).
- **Series.** `N(t)` is the mean over the S sites of the attributed `n_dec` (a damaged block belongs to the site nearest its centre); `r(t) = N(t) / n0_S` is the multiplication of damage since the kick, where `n0_S` is the number of kicked blocks per site (1 under P1, whichever decider).
- **Fit.** `b` is the least-squares slope of `ln r` on `ln t` over the grid, with `r` floored at `1 / (2 m_S)` so a series that touches zero has a defined slope; `b` is the exponent the map colours, as `e`: +2 if the series is saturated, else -2 if `r(1000) < 0.1` (the damage has died), else `b` clamped to [-2, +2].
- **Classes (T-class), in this order.** NA if FEW_SITES (no S twin ran) or FEW_COLONIES (no stability can be established). **CHAOTIC if saturated**: at any grid time the damaged blocks cover at least 5 percent of the living blocks **and** the damage per site has at least doubled (`r >= 2`); a reading that does not depend on the site attribution, so it stands under OVERLAP. (Draft 1 tested coverage alone. Under P1 coverage is `m_S * r / liveblk_R`, so with 16 sites a flat response `r = 1` read CHAOTIC in any reference with 320 living blocks or fewer, the threshold multiplier being 0.31, 0.63, 1.0, 1.6 and 3.2 at 100, 200, 320, 512 and 1,024 living blocks; under P2 the footprint of 16 balls, 28 to 43 kicked blocks in the pilot, alone covered 5 to 13 percent of a sparse world. If coverage is reached without the doubling the series carries the flag FOOTPRINT and is fitted as usual.) NA if OVERLAP. **ORDERED if `r(1000) < 0.1` or `b < -0.5`**: the damage has died, or fades faster than t^-0.5 between steps 100 and 1,000. **CHAOTIC if `b > +0.5`**. **ORDERED, form frozen, if the damage persists but its extent does not grow**: the median surviving site's reach grew by less than one block (8 voxels) between steps 100 and 1,000 (`speed < 8/900` voxels per step). This is what the frozen control F reads by construction, and it matters: in the pilot's what-if with advection off a 12-unit kick stayed in 12 of 12 blocks, which draft 1 would have read as persistent, flat, not spreading, that is CRITICAL. **CRITICAL otherwise**: persistent, flat within t^+-0.5, and spreading by at least a block.
- **Decay, cone and exponential.** Decay is the ORDERED branch. A growing series is CHAOTIC whatever its shape, because its extent is growing; the **form label** says which kind: `exponential` if the exponential model (`ln r` against `t`) has the smaller residual sum of squares than the power model; `cone` if the power model wins and `b <= 2.5` (about 1 for a spreading front line, about 2 for a filled disc in the quasi-2D slab); `super-cone` if `b > 2.5`, faster than the quasi-2D cone bound t^2 by more than 0.5, so more than spreading; `frozen` and `decays` for the ORDERED branches. The band `b` in [-0.5, +0.5] contains the 2D directed-percolation value 0.230 for the growth of the mean damage at criticality (Hinrichsen 2000, from the literature pass, not re-verified here); its width is a judgement call, as are the fraction 0.1, the saturation coverage 0.05 and the doubling 2.0.
- **Stability.** 1,000 bootstrap resamples of the **colonies** that hold S sites (seeded; all sites of a drawn colony go with it, a colony drawn twice counts twice; the draw index is `resample * n_col + position`) recompute the T-class; the job is stable if at least 80 percent reproduce the point class. A colony is a connected component of living columns in R at T. (Draft 1 resampled sites as independent, but the sites of a colony share its fate: the pilot's 12 sites lay in 3 colonies.)

### 4.3 The Derrida curve (pin 6)

- **Initial damage sizes.** The doses `m` in {4, 8, 16, 32, 64} kicked voxels (section 3.4), each run only if the blocks it kicks are at most a tenth of the living blocks (and `m <= N_E / 2`), i.e. `d0 = m / live_R(T)` from about 10^-4 to a few times 10^-3 of the living voxels, each produced by `m` identical conserving B-to-C kicks (total damage `m k`, matter exact) in its own fork of the same state. The origin set is the doses run; at least two are needed.
- **Time step tau.** The deciding value is **tau = 30**; tau = 100 and tau = 300 are columns. The reaction times are 25 to 100 steps (kGrow 128/4096 and kPhoto 160/4096 per step at most), so a step-count of tens is the first at which ecology can show; but in the development pilot kick size changed the block count only before the cascade saturated, and by step 100 every metered world was saturated and dust-limited (section 2), so draft 1's tau = 100 would have read the saturation and not the kick. Tau = 30 is a judgement call (J) that Stage 0 checks.
- **Response ratio and pooled ratio.** `D_m(tau) = n_dec(tau) / liveblk_R(T)` (the fraction of living blocks that are damaged) against `d0` is the curve; the **response ratio** of a dose is `s_m(tau) = n_dec(tau) / n0(m)`, the damaged blocks per kicked block after tau steps, and the **pooled ratio** is `s(tau) = (sum of n_dec(tau) over the origin set) / (sum of the kicked-block counts over the origin set)`. Under the net decider and P1 every kicked block is damaged at step 0, so `s` multiplies the initial damage and is the finite-amplitude cousin of the multiplication factor of the literature (Derrida and Pomeau 1986; Bertschinger and Natschlaeger 2004) at the scale of a block; under the occupancy decider the initial damage count is 0, so `s` counts blocks that reach 8 differing voxels per kicked block and is not a multiplier of anything, and the readout says which. `D_m / d0_m = s_m * (n0(m) / m) * (live_R / liveblk_R)`, so the plotted reference is `s = 1` against the kicked-block fraction, not `D = d0` (draft 1 plotted a unit-slope line that `s_m = 1` does not follow). A linearity ratio `s_m(tau) / s(tau)` for every dose shows where the response stops being linear.
- **Window around 1.** **ORDERED** if `s(30) < 0.5`; **CRITICAL** if `0.5 <= s(30) <= 2.0`; **CHAOTIC** if `s(30) > 2.0`. The centre 1 is the sourced criterion; the factor-2 width is a judgement call, set by what the origin set resolves (damage counts are heavy-tailed, so the pooled estimate has a relative error near 20 percent in a good case). It is stated as a judgement call wherever it is used, and the readout prints a table of every setting's class with each band edge moved by 25 percent. The D-class is **stable** if the two largest doses of the origin set and the pooled estimate fall in the same class. A frozen scar has a response ratio near 1, which this window calls CRITICAL; the trajectory class's extent test is what tells it apart (section 4.4).

### 4.4 The job class

The two meters measure different things: the D-class is the short-time response to a small kick (a local ratio, at step 30), the T-class is the 1,000-step fate of one kick (does it die, hold or spread). A job's class is the common class if they **agree and both are stable**; **MIXED** if they differ (listed as a near-band candidate: a long-lived transient or a slowly spreading front); **UNSTABLE** if they agree but a bootstrap or dose check fails; and a job whose trajectory meter is off (FEW_SITES, FEW_COLONIES, OVERLAP) is classed by the D-class alone (flag `D_ONLY`), with a CRITICAL D-class downgraded to MIXED because one meter cannot certify a band. One rule makes the extent test count: a T-class ORDERED with form frozen together with a D-class CRITICAL **agree on ORDERED** (flag FROZEN), because a persistent scar has a response ratio near 1 and both meters are reading the same fact. A job votes only if its class is ORDERED, CRITICAL or CHAOTIC **and** its dust state is CLEAR (section 3.4; a FEW_SITES job has no U twin and so never votes): a response that does not depend on the size of the kick is listed but never supports a band (draft 1 printed the flag and changed no class). MIXED and UNSTABLE never count as in-band; they cast no vote, so a setting with too many of them is SPLIT, a setting whose valid jobs were kept from voting by the dust rule is DUSTY, and both count in the pool (section 7).

### 4.5 Spatial entropy rate of B (pin 7): descriptive only

Computed on R's B channel at T and T + 100. The field is quantised to four symbols with bin edges 1, 4 and 16 (empty, 1 to 3, 4 to 15, 16 and above); the entropy of a continuous field depends on that resolution, so it is stated, and the finest bin is dust-level on purpose. Reported: the marginal entropy `H0`; the **spatial conditional entropy** `h_sp` of a voxel given its causal template (W, NW, N, NE in the layer; 256 contexts against 524,288 samples, plug-in, bias below 0.0011 bits, printed), pooled over layers; the vertical `h_z` (given the layer above); the **space-time conditional entropy** `h_st` of the voxel at T + 100 given its symbol and the number of its six face neighbours with bin >= 2 at T (28 contexts), which is the dynamical variant; and a **compression ratio** `c_ratio`, the deflate ratio (`compressionRatio`, `complexity.ts:66`) of the symbols in Hilbert order divided by that of a seeded shuffle of the same symbols (1 means no structure). **None of these enters a class, the gate or an outcome.** A single-snapshot entropy is a texture measure, not a regime measure, and the literature gives no numeric line for it; the columns exist to describe the three example runs and to check that the entropy separates the gate worlds in the obvious way.

### 4.6 Other descriptive columns

The **genome-set divergence** `J_g(t)` at t = 100, 300, 1000 (the symmetric difference of the (lineage id, genome fingerprint) triples of living voxels in S and R over their union, with the fingerprint a 32-bit hash of all genome words, read from the genome buffers: six reads of about 100 MB) shows whether the twins' histories split into different genotypes, which is the heritable side of damage; `J_id`, the same on lineage ids alone, is kept for comparison. Equal ids can hide different genomes: a mutant's id is (step + 1, cell), and the transport lottery (`step3d.ts:426`) can pick different parents in the two twins, so draft 1's `J` could read 0 over a heritable difference; and a genome difference with no expressed cell effect is invisible to every deciding counter, which is why the zero control compares full state hashes (C1). The **census divergence** (`|living_S - living_R| / living_R` and `|SumB_S - SumB_R| / SumB_R` at the same times) and the **scale curve** (relative L1 of the column-summed B field coarse-grained to 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 columns, from two 2 MB channel reads) say at which scale the twins differ: in the pilot (B0, seed 9001) at step 1,000 the totals differed by 0.09 percent and the living counts by 0.05 percent while 9,931 of about 70,600 living cells carried a different lineage id, and the relative L1 of column sums fell from 11 percent at one column to about 0 at 16 columns (reported by the review, not saved). So a CHAOTIC class at the block scale is a statement about that scale; the readout prints the census divergence and `J_g` in every outcome string so that it cannot be read as "ecology is unpredictable". The **speed** (median over surviving sites of the growth of the reach between steps 100 and 1,000, per step) separates a spreading front from a confined scar and decides the FROZEN form; the per-site median and inter-quartile range of the damage counts are printed because site responses are heavy-tailed (the pilot's one-unit cascade at step 20 ranged from 1 to 290 voxels over 12 sites). None of the others decides anything.

## 5. The sweep (pin 2)

All jobs: `slab-spots` at **256 x 256 x 8**, light uniform (preset values), the evolving arm, seeds **101, 102, 103** (M4's calibration seeds; the extension seeds 104 to 106 are used only by the UNRESOLVED clause; the registered check seeds 1 to 6 are never used; development seeds 9001 to 9003 are used only in Stage 0; the rule's development log, R0.2, lists the exceptions: probes before the rule used seed 101 at 128 x 128 x 8 and 256 x 256 x 8 up to step 1,900, and seed 1 once on the 2D worlds), burn-in **20,000 steps**, twin phase 1,000 steps. Overrides are passed to `presetConfig` as `m4-calibrate.ts` does.

| group | what varies | settings | M4 data |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | lightK 256; mu in {20, 30, 40} x sigma in {8, 12, 16, 20} | 12 | all 36 jobs |
| B | lightK in {1024, 4096} at (mu, sigma) in {(30, 8), (30, 16), (30, 20)} | 6 | all 18 jobs |
| C | one at a time from the base B0 = `mu30 s8 k256`: `diffA` in {100, 240} (base 200), `diffC` in {100, 240} (base 200), `diffS` in {75, 240} (base 150), `gateK` in {16, 256, 1024} (base 64) | 9 | none: M4 never touched these axes |
| D | one at a time from B0: `dtQ` in {26, 102} (base 51), `spread` in {4, 16} (base 8) | 4 | none: M4 never touched these axes |
| control | B0 with `mutRate: 0` (not in the band logic or the ranking) | 1 | none |
| | | **32 settings, 96 jobs** | 54 jobs overlay M4 exactly |

- **Why this subset of M4's grid.** The overlay with M4's dead-or-carpet map needs M4's exact values, not all of them: mu 20 (starves), 30 (the transition) and 40 (carpets, with `s8` and `s20` mid-transient at 20,000 steps) cover the boundary; mu 50 and 60 are carpets (88 to 100 percent living, reached by 20,000 steps) in every setting M4 ran and cost the most per job, so one carpet (`mu60 s8 k1024`) stays as a gate world and the rest are dropped. The lightK slice sits at the three (mu, sigma) pairs where M4's rows differ most across lightK. B0 is the nearest miss, and the new axes are one at a time from it so the per-axis ranking of section 7 is well defined.
- **The new axes.** `diffA` and `diffC` are 200 of a maximum 256 (78 percent of a voxel's nutrient or waste leaves per step); `diffS` is 150. "High" is 240, not the range limit 256: at 256 the rounding `mulFrac(q, 4 * De, 10, r)` has a factor of exactly 1024 / 1024 and returns `q` (`int.ts:52`), so a voxel sends its whole pool every step, a lattice-parity limit and not "high diffusion" (draft 1 used 256). `gateK` only bites where polymer P exists (the gate factor is `gateK / (gateK + P_t + P_n)`, exactly 1 when P is 0); the generalist builds a thin membrane (`BUILD` bias 12) so P is not empty in general, but the readout checks: a `gateK` setting is **INERT** if, for all three seeds, the config-free digest of R's state at T (the `stateHash` with the configuration replaced by B0's, covering cells, genomes, ledgers and step) equals B0's, and it is then listed and left out of the ranking. (Draft 1 compared the living count and the sums of A, B, C, P; equal sums do not prove equal states.) Group D (decided 2026-10-24) adds `dtQ` in {26, 102} against a base of 51 and `spread` in {4, 16} against a base of 8: both lie inside the config ranges (0 to 1024 and 0 to 32), the slab validator (`validateSlab`) does not constrain them, and M4 never varied them, so C4 does not apply to group D.
- **Group D, and what group C can rank (decided 2026-10-24).** In the pilot's what-ifs the three diffusions switched off and `mutRate` 0 left the early cascade unchanged, while `dtQ` = 0 froze it and `spread` = 0 slowed it (section 2). So group C (diffusion and `gateK`) probably cannot move the meters that matter for the cascade, and the constants that carry it are swept as **group D**: `dtQ` in {26, 102} (base 51) and `spread` in {4, 16} (base 8), one at a time from B0, 4 settings and 12 jobs (about 23 minutes). That makes 32 settings and 96 jobs, 31 sweep settings in the TOO-MANY-UNMETERABLE count, and `dtQ` and `spread` ranking candidates adjacent to B0 (R12.2). Group C stays as drafted and the 54 M4-overlay jobs are unchanged. The Stage 0 constant screen (about 70 s) still runs, as a descriptive line that decides nothing. The setting ids `-dtQ26`, `-dtQ102`, `-sp4` and `-sp16` follow the pattern of group C's ids.
- **Which settings can reach BAND-FOUND.** Of the 54 overlay jobs only B0 has two or more windowed seeds at 50,000 steps (section 2), so among the M4-overlay settings only B0 can pass the late check; any other BAND-FOUND would come from groups C and D. The sweep therefore tests B0 and its near neighbours at the two times; it does not scan a wide grid.
- **The mu and sigma confound** (section 2) is kept on purpose: it keeps the overlay with M4 exact. Not swept: `kernelRadius` (fixed 3), `thetaMass`, `massUnit`, `kCatHalf`, every `k*` rate, `mutRate` (control only), `mutStep`, `eA..eP`, `lightBase`, `lightAmp`, seasons, `tileD`. If the ranking ends in NO-AXIS (section 7) the next step is to sweep exactly these.
- **Grid size: 256 x 256 x 8, decided and justified (not 128).** 128 x 128 x 8 is about 3 times cheaper per step (the probe: about 810 against about 245 steps/s warm on the M1 Max) but it is a different world: the same preset places the same 8 founders in a quarter of the area (four times the founder density), the torus is a quarter the width of the damage reach, fill times shorten, and M4's labels no longer apply, so the overlay would be by setting name only. At 256 x 256 x 8 the burn-in should **reproduce M4's** for the same (setting, seed): check C4 compares the living count at 5,000, 10,000, 15,000 and 20,000 steps (and the extinction step) with M4's exact per-run JSON for 54 of the 96 jobs (the 180 JSONs live in the sibling worktree `.claude/worktrees/3d-rung1b-slab/runs/m4-calibration/` and must be copied before the gate; the TSV is rounded to four decimals, +-26 voxels, and the gate refuses it). Agreement of four living counts is consistent with an identical burn-in, not a proof of one (M4 stored no state hash), so every job also saves the full state hash and a config-free digest, and one job compared once between the two Macs would turn this into a determinism check. The saving from 128 would be about 1.7 hours of about 4.1, which is not worth that loss given the burn-in still dominates. If the user wants the cheap path anyway, the draft's fallback is 128 x 128 x 8 with 2 founders (equal founder density), no overlay claim, and job ids marked `g128`.
- **Burn-in and window.** T0 = 20,000 steps (section 3.5), livable window 0.01 <= f < 0.85. The M4 traces say what to expect at 20,000 for the 54 overlay jobs: 15 extinct, 7 relics (under 1 percent living), 23 livable, 9 carpets.
- **Reproducibility.** One JSON per job, written to `.tmp` then renamed, skipped if present, sharded by `--shard=i/n` and `--reverse`, as `m4-calibrate.ts`; each carries its full per-step damage log, the living trace every 1,000 steps, the C4 comparison with its source and mode, and the state hashes, so the reader needs nothing else (section 8.4).

## 6. The development pilot and the ground-truth gate (pin 9)

Two stages, one of them before the rule is frozen.

**Stage 0 (development; before the freeze).** The instrument (M0 and M1) is run on development seeds 9001, 9002 and 9003 on the gate's worlds (the two 2D alive worlds read at 50,000 and 100,000 steps, the slab carpet) and, for information, on slab B0, with every control (Z, U, N, F) and both rungs from one frozen copy of R, so four **combinations of rung and decider** come from two twin banks. The combinations are tried in the order (P1, net), (P1, occ), (P2, net), (P2, occ). The first that satisfies the gate conditions below on the development seeds, **and** has no decision within a margin of a threshold, is **selected** and written into the rule (R1 RUNG and DECIDER). The margin condition: no dust ratio within 0.10 of 0.25 or 0.5; no pooled ratio `s(30)` within a factor 1.25 of 0.5 or 2.0; no bootstrap stability within 0.05 of 0.80; no grid time at which both the coverage and the doubling are within a factor 1.25 of their thresholds. If none qualifies, Stage 0 fails; the finalizer may then change judgement constants and rerun it at most twice (three runs in all, each in the development log, R0.2), else candidate 8 closes at Stage 0 with the causes of all four combinations. Stage 0 also prints three go/no-go numbers (the Z twin is exactly zero everywhere; the one-unit cascade covers no more than 5 percent of the living blocks at step 30; the median block-net dust next to `thetaB`; the last two also form the threshold trigger of R9.0). The first fails all four combinations; the second and the trigger are read in each rung's bank and fail that rung's two combinations only (`DUST-FLOOR`, `DUST-THRESHOLD`), and nothing is ever re-derived (decided 2026-10-24). Stage 0 also runs a one-state **constant screen** (about 70 s): for `dtQ`, `spread`, `thetaMass`, `massUnit`, `diffA`, `diffC`, `diffS`, `gateK` and `mutRate` at half and twice the base value, how much the early cascade moves, as a descriptive line that decides nothing (group D is already in the sweep, section 5).

**What the pilot predicts for Stage 0.** At step 30 the pilot's dust ratios under (P1, net) are 0.53 for the carpet, 0.49 for `gradient-m3` and 0.27 for `spots-m3` on seed 9001 (0.22 to 0.63 on seeds 9003 and 9004; plan 2; the 2D ratios were read at 20,000 steps, a time Stage 0 no longer reads for the 2D worlds, so for them this predicts the reads at 50,000 and 100,000 steps only loosely), so with the clear line at 0.25 G6 is expected to fail for (P1, net), and that combination's `gradient-m3` ratios (0.45 to 0.49) would also sit within the margin of the 0.5 line. The other three combinations have no pilot data at step 30 (the P2 figures the review reported were read at step 100). Expect Stage 0 to use more than one of its three runs, and read nothing in this note as a forecast that P1 passes; that uncertainty is the reason Stage 0 exists, and a CLOSED result is a legitimate outcome.

**Why a development stage.** Draft 1 ran the four combinations on the held-out seeds and used the first that passed, with no world whose class was known by construction. Under an illustrative null in which every job's class is an independent draw from the three classes, one combination passes the class conditions with probability `2 (7/27)^2 (14/27)` = 7.0 percent (G2 needs the two presets to hold the same non-ORDERED class, G3 needs the carpet to hold a different one) and the four-step ladder `1 - (1 - 0.0697)^4` = 25.1 percent. This is not an estimate for the real, correlated ladder, only a measure of how little a pass would have meant. The pilot's one combination that came near passing, (P2, occ), was also fragile: four of seven dust ratios lay between 0.41 and 0.49 against a line at 0.5, and its carpet read CRITICAL by a flat scar of 17 to 40 damaged blocks (reported by the review). Draft 2 therefore searches the four combinations on seeds whose results the rule may use, fixes the winner, and tests that one combination once on held-out seeds.

**The held-out gate** runs after the freeze, on seeds 101 to 103, with the selected combination only. Each metered gate job keeps a frozen copy of the reference at T and runs the rungs P1 and P2 one after the other, each from its own fork of that copy, so both rungs see the same state; the other three combinations are printed as information and never select.

The gate **passes** if G1, G2, G3, G5 (no gate job INVALID), G6 and G7 hold. If it fails, **candidate 8 closes** and no sweep is read; there is no second selection on seeds 101 to 106. The sweep runner refuses to start without a passing `gate.json` carrying the frozen rule's hash, the selected combination, the amplitude label and the SHA-256 manifest of the M4 JSONs it compared with.

The gate asks that the dead, the carpet and the alive worlds be told apart by a response that depends on the size of the kick, with the two control worlds at their ends; it does not ask that the carpet be ORDERED, because a dense carpet may well spread damage. G6 is there because the probe and the pilot showed the voxel-level response, and by step 100 the block-level response, forgetting the kick: if a much smaller kick does as much block-level damage as the full one, the meters are reading what any kick does and cannot rank settings. G7 is there because a world in which nothing happens (F) has a response ratio of exactly 1, the project's target, unless an extent test says otherwise, and because a meter that does not saturate under a complete noise swap (N) cannot see chaos. No independently labelled CRITICAL world exists, so a pass shows only that the instrument separates an ordered end, an alive reading and a chaotic end. Two caveats stay: the 2D references are a different kernel and a different block population (64 voxels per block against 512) and the gate compares readings, not mechanisms; and the density of the living region alone can separate G2 from G3 (the pilot's `spots-m3` was 0.78 living, B0 0.12), which the controls do not fully remove.

**What a failure means, in the wording the readout will print:**

- `REF-ORDERED` means *the alive reference worlds read ORDERED under the selected block decider at this kick scale; their decay, frozen-scar, trajectory and dose readings are listed* (rule R9.6). It does **not** say that the damage healed, and under the net decider it does **not** mean they do not rearrange: ORDERED is also what a persistent scar that does not spread reads, and a twin that rearranges biomass at constant block density reads ORDERED under the net sum. Under the occupancy decider it means *fewer than 8 voxels per block stay different*. (Draft 1 expected this as the most likely first reading; in the pilot no job under any combination read ORDERED.)
- `REFS-DISAGREE`: the two registered alive presets read different classes, so there is no single alive signature for these meters. `REF-UNSTABLE`: a preset's class changes between 50,000 and 100,000 steps, so the reference is a stage of a transient.
- `CARPET-NOT-SEPARATED`: the carpet reads the same class as the alive worlds, so the meters cannot tell them apart.
- `DUST-LIMITED` and `DUST-AMBIGUOUS`: **observed dose insensitivity**. In the listed presets, times and seeds a much smaller kick left at least half (or between a quarter and a half) of the damage of the full kick at step 30. The readout does not say that the response is independent of the kick at every scale and decider (draft 1's wording did, and printed it whenever any single combination had one dust-limited group), and it does not name a mechanism: rounding dust and sensitive dependence give the same comparison. A close with only dust causes is a finding about the reach of this instrument.
- `N-NOT-SATURATED`, `FROZEN-NOT-ORDERED`, `NO-CONTRAST-WITH-CONTROLS`: a control failed to read at its end, so the instrument cannot be trusted on that world.

**What a pass means:**

- **refClass CRITICAL.** The project's alive 2D worlds read in the CRITICAL class at this kick scale (and, if AMP-ROBUST, at the other scale). If no slab setting does, that is the pattern the candidate predicts; the criticality reading applies, a physics change is indicated, and the ranking of section 7 says which constant to try first.
- **refClass CHAOTIC.** The alive worlds read chaotic. The sweep is still read and the ranking still runs, toward "chaotic-like", and the readout states that this does **not** test the criticality hypothesis. A slab setting that reads chaotic with the 2D worlds is "alive-like" by these meters. In the pilot every world read chaotic and saturated under the one-voxel kick, so this is the more likely branch.

The probe's 2D smoke test (a kick of 4 units at the densest voxel of `spots-m3` went from 6 to 3,740 differing B words in 300 steps) and the pilot's block counts (the 2D worlds saturate by step 100) suggest the 2D worlds are sensitive at both levels; which class they fall in under the selected combination is what the gate measures. The 2D worlds are not a like-for-like comparison (R 9, one layer); the gate compares readings, not mechanisms.

## 7. The decision rule (pin 10)

The rule file (R7 to R12) is the normative text. In outline:

- **Job class** (section 4.4) and **setting class**: a setting's valid jobs (in the window at both ends of the twin phase, with a class) **vote** ORDERED, CRITICAL or CHAOTIC only if their dust state is CLEAR (MIXED, UNSTABLE and dust-limited or dust-ambiguous jobs vote for nothing); the class is the one with 2 of 3 votes, else SPLIT; a setting whose valid jobs were kept from voting by the dust rule is DUSTY (its would-be class is printed); fewer than 2 valid jobs is INSUFFICIENT; a setting that is extinct, has no target or dies inside the phase in 2 of 3 seeds is UNMETERABLE, one that is a carpet or a relic in 2 of 3 is excluded from the window.
- **"A band exists"** means: a setting in the livable window reads **refClass on at least 2 of 3 seeds at 20,000 steps and again at 50,000** (the late check), with at most six settings checked (B0 first, then fewest changed axes). One reading is not a band, and a reading that 9 in 10 of the class-bearing settings share is not a band either (NO-CONTRAST).
- **Outcomes**, the first that applies:

| outcome | condition | what it licenses | next |
|---|---|---|---|
| INVALID-RUN | any job fails a validity check, or the readout sees mixed rule or source hashes | nothing; no outcome | fix the cause, rerun those jobs |
| GATE-FAIL / CLOSED | the selected combination failed the held-out gate | nothing; the candidate ends here (a close with only dust causes is a finding about the reach of this instrument: observed dose insensitivity) | none |
| NO-CONTRAST | at least 90 percent of at least 8 class-bearing settings read refClass | nothing; the meters do not separate these settings | none, or a different instrument |
| PENDING-LATE-CHECK | target settings exist at 20,000, late jobs are missing | nothing yet | run the listed jobs |
| BAND-FOUND | at least one setting reads refClass at 20,000 and at 50,000 | the settings are the band; no physics change is indicated by these meters | look by eye inside it |
| LATE-INCONCLUSIVE | no setting agrees at 50,000 and at least one has fewer than 2 valid jobs there after all late jobs ran | nothing: the evidence at 50,000 is insufficient, not negative; a rerun cannot change it | look by eye, or more seeds |
| TRANSIENT-BAND | target at 20,000 but at none at 50,000 | a stage of the transient, listed | look by eye at the 20,000-step states |
| EMPTY-WINDOW | no setting has 2 valid in-window jobs | nothing | re-centre the sweep |
| TOO-MANY-UNMETERABLE | more than half of the 31 sweep settings are extinct, have no target or die in the phase in 2 of 3 seeds | nothing | re-centre the sweep |
| THIN-POOL | fewer than 8 class-bearing settings, or they differ from B0 along fewer than 3 swept axes | nothing: too little to infer an absence | widen the sweep |
| SENSITIVE-EVERYWHERE | more than half the pool's valid jobs that have a unit-kick twin are dust-ambiguous or dust-limited | nothing: no band can be read or excluded | none for these meters |
| UNDER-SUPPORTED | more than half the pool's valid jobs lack a trajectory meter (too few sites or colonies, or overlap) | nothing: a lack of spatial support is not a lack of a band | larger or denser worlds |
| UNRESOLVED(-FINAL) | more than half the pool splits across seeds (still, after seeds 104 to 106) | nothing; the meters are too noisy here | more seeds, or close |
| CROSSING-BRACKETED | two adjacent settings along one axis read ORDERED and CHAOTIC (refClass CRITICAL) | the critical manifold lies between them | refine along that axis before any physics change |
| NO-BAND | none of the above; label all-ordered, all-chaotic or scattered | with refClass CRITICAL (and AMP-ROBUST): a physics change is indicated; with CHAOTIC: no swept setting is alive-like | the axis ranking |

- **Which constant to change** (outcomes CROSSING-BRACKETED and NO-BAND, B0 in the pool with a class). The measure of "toward" is the log response ratio `ell = ln s(30)` (floored at `ln 0.01`), the median over a setting's voting jobs. The distance to the target is `d = max(0, ln 0.5 - ell, ell - ln 2)` for refClass CRITICAL and `d = max(0, ln 2 - ell)` for CHAOTIC. For each swept axis the candidates are the class-bearing settings **adjacent to B0** on that axis (one on each side where one exists; a minimum over more candidates is biased toward the axis that has more of them), the others at B0; `Delta = d(B0) - min d(candidate)`. The axis with the largest `Delta` is named first, with its direction (raise or lower); axes within 0.10 of the top are tied and ordered by the smaller change of the constant (`|log2(x_best / x_B0)|`), then alphabetically; equal minima on one axis break on the same quantity and then the setting id. If the top `Delta` is below **0.35** (a factor 1.4 in the ratio) **or below B0's own seed-to-seed range of `ln s(30)`**, whichever is larger, the outcome is NO-AXIS: no swept constant is a lever, and the next sweep covers the constants not swept (section 5) or the structure (kernel radius, topology). With no valid B0 (no class, or fewer than two voting jobs) the readout prints NO-RANKING. INERT `gateK` settings are listed and excluded. When B0 itself reads refClass at both times, no ranking is printed: the target is reached.
- **Never printed:** "proof". The text says "indicated by these meters" and names the ranking as an ordering by effect on the meters. The criticality sentences are printed only for refClass CRITICAL that is AMP-ROBUST; every sentence ends with the reading scale (the kick size, the block size, the deciding step, the census and genome-set divergence at step 1,000, the number of jobs kept from voting).
- **Ties, in words.** Several target settings: all listed in the late-check order. A 3-3 split with 6 seeds is SPLIT. Equal `Delta` within 0.10: both axes named, the smaller change first.
- **Determinism.** Every draw is a function of seed, step and cell (`int.ts:40-46`), so a job rerun returns the same result: the late check never "waits for valid jobs" once they have completed. A completed late check with fewer than two valid jobs is an outcome (LATE-INCONCLUSIVE), not a pending state; only new seeds could add evidence, the rule runs the extension seeds (104 to 106) for the UNRESOLVED clause alone, and running them for a late check would be a dated amendment.

### 8.2 The GPU instrument

- **Fork** (`forkSim`): `copyBufferToBuffer` of `from.cells[0]`, `from.currentGenome` into both `to.genome[0]` and `to.genome[1]`, and `from.ledger` into `to.ledger`; then `to.step = from.step`. The twin is first created with `GpuSim3.create(device, state0)` (config equality is enforced by `upload`'s `configDiff`, `gpu-sim3.ts:61-66, 192-193`). Each `create` compiles seven pipelines (36 to 94 ms warm, 563 ms cold in the probe). The N twin and the F pair are created with `GpuSim3.create(device, {...state0, cfg: cfgX})` (the overridden `seed`, or `dtQ` 0 and `motility` false) and then filled by the same copies; no `upload` is called on them, so `configDiff` never sees the difference, exactly as the pilot built its N twin (`twins_any.ts:71-72`). Their checks are C2 against R's initial totals and C8; C1 does not apply.
- **Kick** (`applyKick`): the CPU copies of the B and C channels (from R's `readCells`) are modified at the sites and written back with `queue.writeBuffer` at byte offset `ch * n * 4`; the two `heatOut` words at `LEDGER.HEAT_LO * 4` are rewritten with the 64-bit sum. Base heat is read once from R (`readStats().heatOut`).
- **Diff pass** (`createDiff(R, X, opts)`): one compute pass per twin per step, encoded after both sims have stepped, with **one workgroup per 8 x 8 x D block** (1,024 workgroups of 64 threads, each thread walking the layers of its column). Bindings: `R.cells[0]`, `X.cells[0]`, `R.ledger` (for the step word), the site list (S only), the log and a small uniform with the thresholds (six bindings in all; the uniform lets one compiled kernel serve every job and every twin, whose thresholds differ). A workgroup reduces the signed `B_X - B_R` over its block in shared memory (no global atomics for the block sum), accumulates the voxel-level counters in workgroup atomics, and adds each counter once to the log row; the per-site counters are touched only by damaged blocks (rare). Row index is the ledger step minus T, so there is no ring wrap in a 1,000-step run. Global counters per row: `n_blk, n_blk2, n_occ, L1c, liveblk_R, n_thetaV, n_2thetaV, n_1, n_live, n_vox, L1_A, L1_B, L1_C, L1_P, SB_R, live_R, SB_X, live_X` (18 words); then four words per S site (net count, occupancy count, and the two reaches): 18 + 4 x 16 = 82 words, so a 1,024-row log is about 330 KB. It is read once per batch of 100 steps. The F pair uses the same kernel with X_F and R_F as its two bindings. The probe's voxel-level version (`diffpoc.ts`, one thread per voxel) matched a JavaScript recomputation exactly at steps 651, 660 and 700 at both sizes and cost 0 to 5 percent at 256 x 256 x 8 (7 to 17 percent at 128 x 128 x 8); the block kernel is new and is tested the same way (section 8.3, test c).
- **Memory and traffic.** About 2.35 GiB for ten sims at 256 x 256 x 8 (235 MiB each), 2.6 GiB in a gate job with its frozen copy; fallback in section 3.4. The diff pass reads both cell buffers (14.7 MB each) per twin per step: about 3,200 passes per job, some 94 GB (87.5 GiB) of GPU memory traffic, not host readback; the probe measured 0 to 5 percent overhead for the voxel-level pass, and M1 benchmarks the block kernel with the full bank before any time is promised. Host reads are cheap: a full state is 117,440,640 bytes and took 75 to 110 ms (median 75) in the probe, a `readCells` 14.7 MB, a `readChannel` 2.1 MB at 13 to 18 ms; a job reads about ten full states (C1's hash, C2 per sim) and six genome buffers (J_g), under two seconds in all (draft 1's "up to about 23 MB" was the snapshot size, not a full state).

### 8.3 Tests

- **vitest, pure** (`regime3.test.ts`): every row of the rule's boundary table (R14, including the flat-response saturation rows, the dose cap, the three dust states, the FROZEN edge, the colony counts, the window ends, the late-check and extension rows and every Stage 0 and gate cause); site selection is deterministic and respects `D_SEP`; the torus attribution; colonies on a hand-built living mask; `B*`, `k`, `thetaB`, `thetaV` on hand cases (`B* = 6` gives `k = 12`, `thetaB = 6`, `thetaV = 3`; `B* = 40` gives `k = 20`, `thetaB = 10`, `thetaV = 5`; these test the formula, not a state the world reaches); the block sums and the occupancy count on a hand-built pair of fields, including a kick that straddles a block boundary and a block with 7 and with 8 differing voxels; the union rule for overlapping balls; the fit recovers `b` from a synthetic `r(t) = (t / 100)^b` to 1e-9 and an all-zero series gives ORDERED with `e = -2`; the bootstrap is deterministic and its indices never repeat across resamples; entropy estimators on synthetic fields (a constant field gives 0 bits; i.i.d. uniform symbols give 2 bits within the printed bias; a first-order Markov field gives its known entropy rate; a shuffled field has `c_ratio` near 1 and a striped field far below 1); the decision functions on synthetic job tables, **one per outcome string** (every R10, R11 and R12 case included: INVALID-RUN, NO-CONTRAST, LATE-INCONCLUSIVE, THIN-POOL, SENSITIVE-EVERYWHERE, UNDER-SUPPORTED, NO-RANKING, NO-AXIS, a tie, an INERT `gateK`) and the gate and Stage 0 cases (carpet equal to refClass, presets disagreeing, an unstable reference, a dust-limited carpet, a failing control, a marginal combination, each of the four combinations being the first to pass, all four failing, run 3 closing).
- **Deno, GPU** (standalone scripts like the rest of `tests/deno/`): (a) *no kick, no difference*: a fork stays equal to R for 200 steps at 32 x 32 x 8 and 128 x 128 x 8, and for 100 steps at 256 x 256 x 8 with R as the continuing burn-in sim (as the probe did), by full `stateHash` and by zero diff counters; (b) *the kick conserves*: matter delta 0, `ledgerResidual` 0 and `validateState` clean after the kick and after 300 steps, and the negative control (no heat entry) gives exactly `-(sum of kicks) * (eB - eC)`; overlapping balls kick each voxel once and never underflow; (c) *the counters are right*: the GPU log equals a CPU recomputation from `readCells` at steps 1, 10 and 100; (d) *the oracle*: `RefSim3` at 32 x 32 x 8 started from the same kicked state matches the GPU `stateHash` after 50 steps (the kicked state is a legal state of the specification); (e) *2D*: the same fork, kick and diff on a 64 x 64 2D config; (f) *whole job*: one job re-run reproduces its JSON except the volatile keys (`timings`, `seconds`, `stepsPerSec`, `uptimeStart`, `uptimeEnd`; and `device` and `host` across machines); (g) *retention and the controls*: `RefSim3` and the GPU at 32 x 32 x 8 with `dtQ` 0 and `motility` false keep a 12-unit kick on a B = 13 voxel at its site for 100 steps, the `share3` at-rest table (B = 12, 13, 16 stay whole; 20, 39, 100 keep 18, 29, 64) holds in both, the N twin's configuration differs from R's only in `seed` and the F pair's only in `dtQ` and `motility`, and C2 passes for them against R's totals.
- **Scope check at every milestone:** `git diff --name-status caf9a431` lists only the allowed paths of section 1 (a one-line shell filter against that list; no new file), `pnpm test` is green (11 of the 16 slab pins and every 2D pin; section 1), and before the final acceptance the full pin set runs (`BL_SLAB_BIG=1` and `tests/deno/gpu_golden.ts`).

### 8.4 The runner (`regime-sweep.ts`)

Modelled on `m4-calibrate.ts`: preset plus overrides, one `requestDevice3` for the process, `GpuSim3.create`, one JSON per job written to `.tmp` then renamed, a job whose JSON exists is skipped, `--out`, `--reverse`, `--only`, and added `--shard=i/n`, `--stage=dev|gate|main|late`, `--rung=P1|P2` and `--decider=net|occ` (set from the rule for the held-out gate's printed combinations and from `gate.json` for the main and late stages), `--t=20000|50000|100000` (the slab at its stage's time; the 2D references only at 50000 and 100000), `--m4=<dir>` (default `runs/m4-calibration`, which must be a copy of the 180 exact JSONs; the TSV is a fallback the gate refuses), `--capture` (writes the damaged-voxel positions of a job at t = 10, 100, 1000, at most 4,000 points each, for the example runs). A runner parameter, not a rule constant, limits each command buffer to 10 lockstep steps of all sims. 2D jobs run in a separate process with the 2D `requestDevice`. The runner refuses the `main` and `late` stages without a `gate.json` that passes and carries the frozen rule's hash, refuses `gate`, `main` and `late` with uncommitted changes in the four source files unless `--dev`, and stamps every job with `ruleSha`, `codeCommit`, `srcHash` and `treeClean`. A job JSON holds everything the rule needs: the setting, status and flags, `B*`, `k`, `thetaB`, `thetaV`, `N_E`, the sites and colonies, the living trace, the C1 to C8 results, the M4 source and mode, the state hash and digest at T, the per-step global counters of S (1,000 rows), of every dose (300 rows) and of U, N and the F pair, the per-site series at the stored steps, the entropy, genome-set, census and scale columns, and timings. It stays well under 1 MB; the sweep is under 100 MB.

### 8.5 The reader (`regime-readout.ts`)

`--dev` evaluates Stage 0 (R9.0) and prints its outcome and the constant screen; `--gate` evaluates R9 and writes `gate.json`; `--sweep` applies R3 to R12 to every job JSON and writes `regime-results.tsv`, `regime-settings.tsv`, `regime-readout.md` (tables, the development log, the repro line with its modes, the sensitivity table and the outcome string with its reading scale) and `regime-results.json`. In `--gate` and `--sweep` it exits non-zero on an INVALID job or on mixed `ruleSha`, `codeCommit` or `srcHash`, and prints INVALID-RUN; `--dev` is the exception (R5 at Stage 0): an INVALID Stage 0 job is the cause INVALID-JOB of every combination, the readout lists its failed checks and the exit code stays 0. The result is copied to `experiments/3d-slab/` as `regime-results.tsv` and `regime-readout.md` (the reader's own file name), as M4 did; the copy is part of M6.

## 10. Risks

1. **The net-sum decider is blind to rearrangement at constant block density, and the block counts saturate.** Two twins with the same biomass per block but organisms displaced by a voxel or two, or different lineages in the same places, read zero under the net sum. That risk stays. Draft 1 named it the most likely cause of a first reading of `REF-ORDERED` under `(P1, net)`; **the pilot did not bear that out**: no job under any combination read ORDERED, and the dominant fact was saturation, a one-unit kick damaging 45 percent of B0's living blocks by step 100 (section 2). So the REF-ORDERED wording of rule R9.6 (the alive reference worlds read ORDERED under the selected block decider at this kick scale, with their decay, frozen-scar, trajectory and dose readings listed; it does not say that the damage healed) is kept for the cause but is not expected, and a CLOSED result after Stage 0 (about half a day) remains a legitimate, cheap outcome.
2. **The block measure sees dust once the cascade has spread, and a persistent deficit is not amplification.** Draft 1's guard, thresholds above the few units that dust moves across a block boundary, held only before the cascade spread (the one-unit twin had 5 of 331 blocks at or above 6 units at step 30 and 149 at step 100), and its estimate that block sums cancel dust was not measured; the pilot measured the opposite at step 100. Guards now: the deciding time is step 30, the dust control must be CLEAR for a job to vote and for the gate to pass (two thresholds with a margin between), Stage 0 prints the measured dust floor, and the frozen control F is a gate condition. Real amplification that spreads over many blocks can still leave each block below `thetaB` and read as nothing.
3. **Transients.** Twenty thousand steps reads a stage of the fill (section 3.5); the late check at 50,000 and the TRANSIENT-BAND outcome handle the two times measured, not times between or after. The 2D reference worlds are transients too: they are read at 50,000 and 100,000 steps and not at 20,000, where they are still filling (rehearsal: `spots-m3` 0.981 living at 20,000 steps), and a reference whose class changes between the two times fails the gate (REF-UNSTABLE).
4. **Sites are not independent.** Sixteen sites in one colony share its fate. Guards now: the stability bootstrap resamples colonies and not sites, fewer than two colonies make the trajectory class NA, the two-of-three seed vote, the dose-based D-class that does not use the sites, and MIXED and UNSTABLE never counting as in-band. The effective number of independent units is the number of colonies, which the readout prints.
5. **Judgement constants.** The factor-2 window, 0.1, 0.05, the doubling 2.0, the dust thresholds 0.25 and 0.5, the dose cap, the speed floor, `k`, `thetaB`, TAU_DEC and T0 have no source (the rule marks each J or L). They may change only before the freeze and only after a failed Stage 0 run (at most three Stage 0 runs, each logged; a Stage 0 PASS is final and the constants it read stand to the freeze); the readout prints a sensitivity table, and a different choice afterwards is a new rule, not a re-read.
6. **Criticality and evolvability.** The link is contested; a critical band is where the project would look, not where evolution is promised.
7. **Same-noise damage only.** A different coupling of the two copies' noise could give different numbers; the N twin (another seed) is the only other coupling measured, as a ceiling.
8. **mu and sigma are coupled**, as in M4, and `gateK` may be inert (detected by a state digest and listed). The group-C axes probably cannot move the early cascade; group D (`dtQ`, `spread`) is in the sweep for that reason (section 5).
9. **Vertical coupling.** The light pass couples a whole column in one step, so a kick in layer 0 and one in layer 7 are different experiments; sites are pooled over layers and no per-layer reading is made.
10. **The 2D references are a different kernel and a different block population** (R 9, one layer, 64 voxels per block against 512, 12 searched founders), and the density of the living region alone may separate the references from the carpet. The gate compares readings, not mechanisms, and the controls remove only part of this.
11. **Load and memory.** The Mac is shared; timings above are ranges, carpets under load ran at a third of their nominal rate in the pilot, and the lockstep rate is the measured 150 sim-steps/s and not 245. Ten sims use 2.35 GiB; the adapter's 20 GB is a single-buffer limit, so M1 measures the allocation; the sequential-dose fallback exists.
12. **C4 can stop the sweep, but proves less than it seems.** If the burn-in does not reproduce M4's living counts exactly, something differs (code, device, config) and no meter is read until it is explained. Four equal living counts are consistent with an identical burn-in and do not prove one; full state hashes are saved so a later cross-machine comparison can.
13. **Frozen scars and finite amplitude.** A world in which nothing happens has a response ratio of exactly 1, the project's target. The frozen control F and the extent test (FROZEN) exist for that, and the classes are named finite-amplitude; but the extent test is a judgement (one block over 900 steps) and the two kick sizes can disagree about one world (AMP-DEPENDENT).
14. **The development log is part of the evidence.** The probes that shaped this rule used seed 101 at small grids and seed 1 once (rule R0.2); none read a start state of a gate or sweep job, but they are listed, and seed 101 stays in the sweep (decided 2026-10-24, section 13).

## 11. What this does not show

- It does not show that a critical band is where evolution happens, or that its absence means the physics is wrong; it is a pre-stated trigger and an ordering of constants by effect on two meters.
- It measures finite-amplitude, same-noise, block-scale responses at one deciding step (30) and over 1,000 steps. It does not estimate a slope at the origin, and it has no independently labelled critical world. A CHAOTIC block-scale class says little about ecology: in the pilot the totals of two twins differed by 0.09 percent at step 1,000 while half their lineage sets differed.
- Under the net-sum decider it does not see rearrangement: a twin that moves biomass around at constant block density, or holds different lineages in the same places, reads ORDERED. Under the occupancy decider it sees rearrangement and also the dust cloud. Which one the readings come from is stated with every result.
- It says nothing about `kernelRadius`, the constants not swept (`thetaMass`, `massUnit`, `kCatHalf`, the rates, the energies, light and seasons; `dtQ` and `spread`, which the pilot found to carry the cascade, are swept only at two values each, one at a time, as group D), a decoupled mu and sigma, or any run longer than the 1,000-step trajectory and the two start times.
- It does not show that damage in the shared-noise coupling is what a different noise would give.
- It says nothing about lineage-level or genome-level perturbation beyond the descriptive `J_g` column; a genome-only perturbation and a `mutRate 0` arm beyond the single control are follow-ups.
- The entropy columns describe; they decide nothing.

- **R4.1, why the decider is a block count (probe and pilot figures; the rule keeps a bracketed reason).** In the probe (sparse 128 x 128 x 8 world, step 600) kicks of 1, 4 and 16 units left 190, 175 and 183 differing B words at t = 300 (100, 84 and 91 at t = 100) and an L1 of B of 473, 397 and 442: by step 100 the voxel-level damage no longer depends on the size of the kick. It measures the rounding-level difference the local dynamics generate, not what became of the kick. A block sum was expected to cancel the plus and minus parts of that dust inside the block. **The development pilot found that it does not once the cascade has spread** (seed 9001, B0 at 20,000 steps, rung P1, 330 living blocks: the 12-unit twin damaged 15, 26 and 171 blocks at t = 10, 30 and 100, the 1-unit twin 0, 5 and 149, the noise-swapped twin 172, 247 and 271; the damaged blocks' median `|dB_blk|`, as printed by the review's `scales.ts` and not saved, was about 13, 23 and 40 units at t = 100, 300 and 1,000, against thetaB = 6). Block-net dust reaches the threshold only after about 30 steps, which is why TAU_DEC is 30 and why the dust control (R6.12) is evaluated there.
- **R4.1, what the thresholds are and what a persistent deficit is not (the first draft's passive-retention argument, the retention table and pilot figures; the rule keeps a bracketed reason).** `thetaB = ceil(k / 2)` is a judgement constant. Draft 1 justified it by passive transport leaving at most 0.39 of a kick in its block at step 100; **that argument is withdrawn.** It used the real-valued weights (a voxel keeps `(64/80)^3 = 0.512` per step). The integer transport takes three nested floors (`share3`, `step3d.ts:70`; WGSL `shaders3d.ts:371`) and keeps every unsent quantum at its source, so at rest (zero displacement, weights 8, 64, 8 over 80) a voxel with B <= 16 keeps **all** of its biomass, B = 20 keeps 18, B = 27 keeps 23, B = 39 keeps 29, B = 60 keeps 42 and B = 100 keeps 64; the continuum value is reached nowhere in this world's range (the pilot's B is median 12, 90th percentile 25, maximum 45). A kick on a low-B voxel is therefore retained passively, and only displacement (`dtQ`, motility, Sobel terms) moves it; a block deficit that persists to step 100 is **not** evidence that the dynamics sustained or amplified it. The rule reads three things separately: **retention** (a persistent count with no growth of extent: the FROZEN form of R6.4, checked against the frozen control F, R4.10); **rounding propagation** (a response that does not depend on the size of the kick: DUST_LIMITED, R6.12); **amplification** (a response whose extent grows and whose size depends on the kick: the CHAOTIC branches and, with the extent test, CRITICAL). The unit tests check the retention table directly (R14).
