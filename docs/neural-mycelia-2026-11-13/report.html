Toward neural mycelia

What Fraser Price's demo shows, what is open source nearby, and which extensions would bring Cadence Garden closer to it without giving up the exact ledger.

The short answer

Build one new world: neural particles over the conserved field. Each particle is an integer packet of matter and energy that carries a genotype id. The genotypes live in a shared pool, so controllers can grow from 160 bytes to several kilobytes within browser memory limits, provided the number of distinct living genotypes stays bounded. That has to be measured. Bonds, oscillation and learning come later as separate tests. Each stage has to beat a heredity-blocked twin and a hard-coded trail-follower, and each has a stop rule.

Three facts first

  1. The "neural particle layer" in the pasted chat does not exist. Neither this checkout nor origin/main has neural-model.ts or a /particles/neural/ page. The particle playground on main is the older three-family CPU toy, with a ceiling of 240 particles and inherited attraction rules.
  2. Very little about Fraser's system can be verified. The 7 Nov 2026 post reads "the neural mycelia are in bloom". From his other public posts: he calls them "my naturally selected particle sims", oscillations "consistently emerge in/dominate" them, and one is a "sentient mold entropy harvester". The GitHub account the chat matched to him (fraserprice, display name Fraser Price, 35 repos) holds LLM-inference work and nothing on artificial life. I could not confirm it is the same person. The ~300k particles, the 1–30k parameters per net and the September thread all come from the pasted chat. I could not check them because X shows logged-out readers only recent posts.
  3. The closest open analogue is ALIEN, not the two repos the chat named. ALIEN (BSD-3, CUDA, active this week) has particles that bond into creatures, a neural net in each cell, construction from a genome and an energy budget that never creates energy. Pandora is the only nearby source already on WebGPU, and it is a good model for binning millions of particles.

Extend what we have

X1

Mycelium world: neural particles over the conserved field

Particles hold integer matter and energy, a fixed-point position and a genotype id. Each step they read field channels and gradients, run their controller, then move, eat, deposit, split or die. Matter crosses between particle and field only through exact transfers. A split divides the integers and gives the remainder to the parent. Death returns everything to the field cell underneath. The field is modelled on the kernel's: nutrient A, waste C and signal S diffuse as they do today. Biomass B, energy E and the genome, which the kernel moves by reintegration, move onto particles instead. Polymer P becomes the trail particles deposit and sense. This is a new rule set with its own identifier, not a mode of the existing one.

Sources
Fraser (goal), ALIEN (conserved energy), Pandora (binning), Flow-Lenia (mass carries heredity)
New vs I09
I09 began with inert particles and attraction rules. This version starts with controllers and coupling to the field, because a world without either repeats what the toy already shows.
First rung
A CPU reference with 4k particles on a 256² field. Conservation residual exactly 0, and a segmented replay that matches a continuous one byte for byte. Then GPU parity at 64k particles.
Controls
A heredity-blocked twin (children get fresh random weights), a frozen twin (no mutation), shuffled sensors, and a hard-coded Physarum trail-follower with the same energy budget
Gate setup
I06, I06b and I08 all ended "measurement-limited", so set these up before any comparison: freeze the readouts, measure a replicate noise floor and pass a positive control. The blocked twin must stay populated, or a difference only means alive versus extinct.
Stop if
On 3 seeds, cluster lifetime, I14 predictability and the period spectrum don't separate the selected world from its heredity-blocked twin by more than the noise floor. Also stop if the Physarum null produces the same network shapes.
X2

Genotype pool and a capacity ladder

Particles store a 4-byte genotype id. Weights live once in a pool with a parent id and a refcount, and mutation copies a genotype only when it changes it. A full pool makes a birth's mutation fail, and the failure is counted. That rule blocks new variation exactly when diversity peaks, so the pool only works if distinct living genotypes stay well under its size, which in turn caps the mutation rate. Plain i32 arithmetic already keeps GPU and CPU identical. WGSL's packed int8 dot product (the packed_4x8_integer_dot_product language feature) is an optional speed-up to check in each browser. The parent links also give the lineage inspector exact ancestry at no cost.

First measure
The count of distinct living genotypes over time at the chosen mutation rate, before any pool size is fixed
Question
The pasted chat says Fraser's nets carry 1–30k parameters (unverified). Whether net size matters is testable in either case: run arms at 160 B, 1 KiB and 4 KiB per genotype, matched on compute, with larger nets thinking every k steps.
Stop if
The arms do not differ on X1's readouts. If they don't, keep 160 B and move on to X3.
X3

Bonds that carry energy (the mycelium itself)

One controller output asks for a bond to a neighbour. Bonded particles share energy and matter through exact integer flows. When two lineages within genotype distance d touch, they may fuse. Bonded clusters are counted as the inferred organisms, and no cell identity is declared.

Sources
ALIEN cell networks, our bonds toy, Fraser's hyphae
Readout
Bond rate, cluster size distribution, energy moved per bond, and whether bonding is heritable when compared with the heredity-blocked twin
Stop if
Bonding is equal in the selected and blocked worlds, which would make it physics rather than biology.
X4

An oscillation observer, usable now on the grid kernel

Fraser reports that oscillations dominate his selected worlds. Add a per-region period spectrum (autocorrelation of biomass and energy) and test the I14 periodic fixtures against it. Then ask whether oscillation is selected, meaning stronger than in a heredity-blocked twin, or just the dynamics' default. This is small and needs no new world.

X5

Watchability: port the existing tools, then add colour by genotype

Colour each particle by a hash of its genotype id, with a lineage mode from the pool. Add free zoom and pan, and show population and steps per second. The lesion and feed brushes already exist and only need porting. Pandora's state-to-RGB mapping is a good second colour mode. Do this after X1's GPU rung so the look follows the physics and not the other way round.

New areas worth opening

N1

Learning during life, in integers

PD-NCA found that learning during the run turns a settling system into a cycling one. Our version would be an integer Hebbian update, Δw = (η · pre · post) >> s, with η heritable. Compare two arms: weights reset at birth (Baldwinian) and learned weights inherited (Lamarckian). Both use integer arithmetic, so replay and the ledger still hold.

Stop if
The η > 0 arms match η = 0 on X1's readouts.
When
After X1 R2. On the grid kernel this needs a RULE_VERSION fork, so try it in the particle world first.
N2

World search on the archipelago

PBT-NCA and ASAL search over whole worlds. Our islands could each run a Mycelium world while the coordinator proposes configurations. Label this as design and not evolution, use only descriptors that passed calibration (I14, I07), and treat MSPD as a candidate observer that first has to beat our nulls.

Caution
The 1 Nov report warned against "more novelty" as a single objective, and the I01 D1 descriptor failed calibration. This comes after X1–X3.
N3

Bodies that must swim

Microcosmos couples filament bodies to a lattice-Boltzmann fluid. At low Reynolds number only non-reciprocal strokes move a body, so movement gets a real cost and a real reward. The cheap version is overdamped motion in X1, where velocity is proportional to force and drag costs energy. A full fluid is a separate, large line, so park it.

Order of work

Changes to the 1 November programme

  • I09 becomes X1. The first rung changes from inert labelled particles to neural particles coupled to the field. Its ledger rules, determinism rules and "an aggregate is not a reproducer" caution stay as written.
  • I10 (trained NCA tissue) stays parked. PD-NCA's result enters through N1 as learning during life, without external training.
  • I12 stays parked. X2's capacity ladder is the cheaper way to ask whether the controller is the bottleneck.

Smaller lessons for the grid kernel

  • Flow-Lenia used patch mutations (10×10 beams) because its authors expected single-cell mutants to be swamped. Our kernel mutates per cell in the react pass, so a patch-mutation arm is a cheap thing to try. Any change goes behind an optional config key, so existing hashes don't move.
  • In Flow-Lenia (5 seeds), dissipation gave the least diversity and added food plateaued sooner. Treat that as a weak hint for the open-garden ideas (sun, food, meteor), not a finding.

Corrections to the pasted chat

  • "I've added a neural particle layer": not present anywhere in the repo.
  • The controller shape (10 → 8 → 8, int8) and the playground description (three families, 240-particle ceiling) are correct.
  • digital-ecosystem trains by backprop during the run, so it is learning, not evolution. Primordia's "explore" searches parameters and has no heredity.
  • The 3 GB figure is arithmetically right for 300k × 10k int8. The 300k and 10k themselves are unverified.
  • It missed ALIEN, Pandora, the Flow-Lenia journal paper, PD-NCA, PBT-NCA and Microcosmos.
