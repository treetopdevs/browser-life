A 3D Cadence Garden

Possible, in two sizes. A 3D view of today's flat world takes days and changes no physics. A real 3D world, a jar lit from above, is a new rule line and takes weeks. Visual companion to 3d-brainstorm-2026-10-22.md.

1Two different things called 3D

They look alike in a screenshot and cost very different amounts. The first only changes how the world is drawn. The second changes the world.

2Why depth could matter, not just look good

A flat world fakes one thing a volume gets for free: light coming from above and being used up on the way down. Everything below follows from that, as hypotheses with checks, not promises.

The core bet

Shade

Each layer absorbs light in proportion to the biomass and membrane in it. Whatever grows above takes light from whatever is below. In 2D the light map is fixed and nothing can shade anything.

Hypothesis

Layers nobody asked for

Light users near the top, decomposers below where dead matter collects. In 2D every role shares one surface. Stratification is something we can see and measure: roles by depth.

Optional rule

Sinking and a floor

A small downward pull on bound matter makes sediment. Recycling turns it back into nutrient, which must diffuse up to the light. A nutrient loop with a length, like a lake's.

Hypothesis

Insides

In 3D a membrane can close around a volume completely. Biomass inside a shell of polymer is a compartment, closer to a cell than any 2D ring. The ownership and cells sandboxes wanted exactly that.

Brand

The jar, literally

The garden is already a sealed jar with a sun. This is that jar as a glass cylinder you can turn, green near the surface and dark below.

3What today's code assumes is flat

The genome layout and every neighbourhood change, so a 3D world cannot be a new optional setting on RULE_VERSION 1. It is its own rule line, like the game kernel: a new CPU reference, a new GPU twin and new golden pins. The shader isn't the expensive part. Keeping the two equal bit for bit is.

4Two arithmetic bounds break

The rules prove nothing overflows a 32-bit word. Two steps of that proof assume a flat world, and both have fixes the new rule line is free to choose.

Bound 1 · drawn below

Energy arriving from neighbours

After transport a cell's energy is at most 9 × 2²⁸, because at most nine cells send to it. In 3D, 27 do, and 27 × 2²⁸ overflows. Fix: halve the energy cap per cell to 2²⁷.

Bound 2

The share each neighbour gets

A share is floor(q·w/D²), exact while (q mod D²)·w fits 32 bits, true in 2D because D² ≤ 2¹⁴. In 3D the overlap is a volume: at the default spread, 511,999 × 262,144 ≈ 1.3 × 10¹¹. Lowering the energy cap does not help. Fix: three nested per-axis shares, each inside today's proven bound; the source keeps the remainder, so conservation stays exact.

5What it costs to run

Today: 65,536 cells × 224 kernel taps per step, at about 2,000 steps a second on the Mac. The bars show kernel work per step against that. Tap counts are exact, from the repo's kernel rule extended to 3D; speeds are estimates until rung 1a measures them.

6Drawing it, and where three.js fits

Today's renderer draws straight from the simulation's buffers with no copy. A 3D view must keep that. three.js r186 can share the simulation's GPU device and can wrap an existing GPU texture with no copy, but it has no public way to read a raw GPU buffer. So the bridge is a tiny pack pass.

Rung 0

Use three.js

An orbit camera, lighting, shadows, fog and a glass jar come for free, and the pack pass gives a supported, copy-free path. Works in the lab's worker with an OffscreenCanvas.

The volume

three.js first, with a fallback

Raymarch a 3D texture, as three.js's own 128³ and 200³ volume examples do. Whether its texture wrapper takes a 3D texture is unverified; if it doesn't, write the raymarch as one more WGSL pass beside today's renderer.

Not this

Cubes or CPU meshes

262,144 instanced cubes looks like Minecraft, not life. three.js's marching-cubes addon runs on the CPU and would need a readback every frame.

7A staged path, each rung with a stop check

A 3D view of the flat world

Days · no physics change

A lab view mode: the world as a landscape you can orbit. Height from biomass and membrane, colour from lineage, nutrient and waste as tinted ground, light as the sky.

Check: is it easier or more fun to read than the flat view? If not, stop; nothing else depended on it.

1a

A 3D kernel benchmark

A day or two · throwaway code

Time today's 2D affinity pass alone. Then a 64³ affinity pass at R 3 and R 4, plus a trivial 27-source transport, in the lab and in Deno.

Check: 3D affinity at 64³, R 4 takes at most 4.5× today's affinity pass (the tap ratio). If not, try R 3 or the slab; if neither keeps its ratio, stop.

1b

The slab: a shallow pond

One to two weeks · first real 3D rule line

256 × 256 × 8, light from the top fading layer by layer. CPU reference, WGSL twin, golden cases, exact conservation, energy cap 2²⁷, nested per-axis shares, sixth-portion diffusion, 3D Sobel, MZ and SGZ, optional sinking.

Check: D = photosynthesis share of all role activity (photo, grow, decomp, resp, summed over the 10 censuses ending at step 300,000) in the top two layers minus the bottom two; no activity in either counts as a fail. An evolving seed passes if D beats the 3 neutral seeds' mean by more than their range. That is role sorting beyond what the light imposes. 3 of 3 pass: go. 0 of 3: pause. 1–2 of 3: 3 more seeds per arm, go only if 5 of 6 pass.

8What stays hard, and what this does not show

Two implementations

Every 3D rule exists in TypeScript and WGSL and must match bit for bit on every GPU. The 2D line took weeks of golden-case work; 3D repeats it.

Seeing inside

A full volume is murky. Slices, depth fog, isosurfaces and "peel the top layer" are not optional.

Touching it

Feeding, wounding and picking a lineage need a 3D cursor: a ray and a depth. A design problem, not just code.

Phones

A 64³ jar is about 111 MB of GPU memory and several times today's work. Many phones will want the flat view or a smaller jar.

Not shown

No 3D code ran. Speeds are estimates scaled from today's 2D speed; rung 1a measures them.

Nothing here shows a 3D world evolves anything a 2D world does not. Stratification and compartments are hypotheses with checks.
