A game of evolution

The ask is a fork, not a tuning: a sibling of the lab that shares the kernel, keeps the sealed jar and bit-for-bit replay, and declares what the lab would not. Visual companion to evolution-game-brainstorm-2026-10-16.md, which holds the argument in full.

  • Exploratory, not a plan
  • No physics, no pins, no prototype
  • Numbers from docs/sandbox-*.md, the scaffold registration and docs/plan.md

1The fork: what the game declares that the lab infers

The lab's north star is to infer individuals, reproduction and transitions from local rules and never declare them. The repo's own record argues for a sibling that declares some of those on purpose: the registered test was not met, eight sandbox screens found no Promising arm, regeneration erodes under every founder, and the one confirmed result came from the one line that imposes its unit, the pond cycle. The lab stays the lab.

What a game needs that the lab does not have

2What the repo already knows

Everything a shape leans on was measured here, in order of how much a game can build on it. The chips are the catalogue's: Confirmed only where a registered test said so, Measured for a number from a run, Seen for what was noticed without a measured cause, Negative for a test that failed. A qualifier like one seed sits beside the state and never replaces it.

3Substrate facts the shapes depend on

Four facts about the chemistry decide what each shape costs.

The genome has no free word

44 words: two lineage words, PARAM0 (μ and σ of the growth function), PARAM1 (the motility gain in byte 0; bytes 1 to 3 are the heritable kernel-ring offsets in the cells sandbox, zero in every base world) and 40 words holding 160 int8 controller weights (10 → 8 → 8). Anything new a lineage inherits is a layout change.

Every cell carries every reaction

Photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, growth, polymer building and signal emission run in a fixed order with world-wide rate constants (kPhoto 160, kResp 128, kDecomp 96, kGrow 128, kBuild 64 by default), each scaled by a controller output that can be zero. A lineage can run a reaction at nothing; it cannot lack the machinery, pay for carrying it, or gain it in one readable step. There is no "first decomposer" today. Waste also arises by passive decay and is recycled abiotically under light.

Mutation is per synthesised quantum

It mints a lineage id on every event, so lineages are cheap and deep: dozens to hundreds of mutations in 10⁶ steps. Declared cells (cellPeriod) move mutation to a cell's birth instead.

Dials do not turn mid-run

Levers exist as config: light mode with period and amplitude, seasons, tiles and migration, the pond cycle and its score, lossy takeover, kin tolerance, recurring wounds, heritable shape, declared cells, signal gain, mutation rate. Interventions exist: lesion, feed, drain, pick, each logged and replayed. The GPU pipelines are built from the config, and light and injury are shader constants, so a dial turned while the world runs is a new intervention kind with a pipeline rebuild, validated transitions, checkpoints that carry it and headless replay. Pond worlds refuse feeding, draining and migration.

4The design problem in one sentence

Evolution here is fast, cheap and good at stripping; the game must make the things we want to see pay, give them names and a time, and keep the dose of disturbance between dull and lethal.

5Five shapes

Each says what the player does, what evolves, what is declared, what it costs and its main risk. They combine; section 8 says how. The tags name the stage each shape belongs to.

1The breeder as a gameStage 0b

"Pond keeper."

Play
A jar of sixteen (or sixty-four) ponds. Every cycle, a few seconds, the best-scoring ponds reseed the rest. The player chooses the pressure for the next stretch (movement, reach, body size, seed mass, mass; later elongation, sensing and anything the metrics can score) or picks donors by eye for a cycle or two. A log of firsts announces thresholds; progress unlocks levers (a gentle sun, seasons, wounds, heritable shape), each at its known dose. A session ends with a card.
Evolves
Whatever the chemistry can do under group selection. Shown so far: speed, on one seed. Preserved by scoring it: packet mass. Descriptive only: body size. Not built: scores for shape and sensing.
Declared
Nothing new. The pond cycle is already imposed and the breeder already exists in the lab, with hand picking and the breeder preset.
Cost
Lab and observer work: a pressure selector per cycle (the score is fixed in the config today, so switching needs the pick-style intervention and its replay), a firsts detector with stated rules, lineage colour and a tree in the jar, a session card. No physics, no pins.
Risk
Only movement has been shown to respond, on one seed, on the larger preset. "Every body marching in step" may read as a lattice, not as life. Whether a player's eye breeds as well as the rule is untested. A pressure the chemistry cannot meet breeds nothing, and the game must say so. Reordering four donors changes a whole history, so two sessions looking different is not evidence that the pressure did it.

2Tracks in the genomeStage 1

"Capability genes." The physics bet.

Play
The world starts with one kind of life that can only photosynthesise, respire and grow. Every other capability is a gene a lineage can gain by mutation and must pay to keep: decomposition, polymer building, signal emission, motility, sensing, adhesion, a wall, and later eating. The player does not flip genes; the environment decides which ones pay, and the player sets the environment. The game announces each first, in the note's example line: "the first decomposers appeared at step 412,000 and now hold 14% of the living matter."
Evolves
Which genes a lineage carries, gained when they pay and shed when they do not, if the costs are set so. Gating does not make a rung reliable; it makes reaching one identifiable: a discrete, inherited, costly change the game can detect and date.
Declared
What a lineage can do. Cells and bodies stay inferred, so individuals and reproduction are still results of the physics.
Cost
A physics change: a genome word for the gene set (about ten consumers of Genome must carry it), a per-gene maintenance cost in react, reactions gated by their gene, a mutation operator for gene bits at a lower rate, the WGSL twin of all of it, new golden cases. It cannot leave the RULE_VERSION 1 pins untouched: a game kernel with its own version line, or RULE_VERSION 2. Weeks, and a Codex pass on the parity.
Risk
Calibration. A small cost flips everything on in the first minute; a large one leaves the world photosynthetic. Avida's answer is mild rewards for intermediates and a rich one at the top; here a reward is an energy bonus or a feedstock, never created matter. Eating is the hardest gene: nothing in the repo has shown predation, and the eating rule is a design in its own right. In this repo the evidence is nil so far: the role inference sees dominance at a census, not inherited acquisition, and the 209 "obligate" genomes of the ecology-first search were founding-density specialists (four times the seeding biomass rescued 11 of 12).

3EpochsStage 1

Designed regime shifts.

Play
The world moves through named ages, on a schedule, by the player's hand, or both: Dawn (uniform dim light), the Long Day (a gradient), the Turning Sky (a sweeping sun, slow then faster), the Seasons, the Wounds, the Meteor (one large lesion with a feed in its crater), the Flood and the Drought (feed and drain everywhere), the Drift (tiles that begin to exchange migrants, or stop). Each age opens a door; the game names the moment the population walks through it, or fails to.
Evolves
Whatever each age pays for. The record: a sun's speed sets survival (one seed per speed); one wandering-sun comparison of hand-built movers suggests sensing may pay and says nothing about sensing evolving; wounds raise turnover; seasons alone changed little.
Declared
Nothing in the physics. The ages are config over time.
Cost
The regimes exist one at a time, as worlds; the ages do not. A schedule fixed at planting needs the step function to read its settings by step (seasons already do this for light; sun mode, injury and the rest are shader constants), so it is a rule change with golden cases before any player touches it. Player-triggered ages are the dial intervention. A detector per door. Doses need a sweep: the storm first look used one seed per lever. The Meteor's feed, the Flood, the Drought and the Drift are refused in pond worlds.
Risk
Extinction, which a player experiences as a dead jar. The Flow-Lenia authors' later result is reported to say dissipation and shared limited resources lowered evolutionary activity (a search summary, unverified against the paper); if so, an age that only tightens scarcity may dull a world. Scripted stages were what critics held against Spore and Thrive: ages must open doors, not decide what comes through.

4Two substratesStage 2

A field and creatures. The fallback for predation.

Play
The chemistry field stays the medium and the producers: the mat, the plants, the waste, the recycling. Above it live declared creatures, small bodies of a few cells with a position, a genome and a controller, that eat the field, eat each other and die back into it. The Bibites and Biogenesis are the models.
Evolves
Creature controllers and bodies under predation, which the field alone has not produced.
Declared
Creatures, outright. Matter still moves from field to creature and back, so the jar stays sealed, but a creature is an object, not an inference.
Cost
A second kernel with its own determinism problem (agents ordered, collisions resolved with the counter PRNG, in integers), the coupling to the field in WGSL and in the reference, conservation across two layers, a renderer, every observer taught about the second population. The largest shape by far. Months.
Risk
It becomes a different game that happens to sit on the field, and the field's elegance (one rule set, two implementations, bit for bit) is diluted. The practitioner record warns that differentiation and multicellularity do not come free even with declared cells: Thrive's multicellular stage has only budding; EEvol's developer sees filaments and no differentiation. Practitioner evidence, not published.

5Named speciesStage 0b

The board: how the player sees what the other shapes make happen.

Play
The jar's living things have names, portraits and a family tree. Species are clusters of lineages by genome distance at a stated tolerance; a species persists across censuses, is named once and passes its name to descendants, splits when a cluster divides and both halves last, and goes extinct when no living genome of it remains. A species whose last countable body dies but which lives on as film has "gone to film", a different event. A "keep" on a species makes the game stop at its last stand.
Evolves
Nothing new.
Declared
A species boundary: a tolerance on genome distance and a persistence rule, stated on screen as the catalogue states "seen, not measured."
Cost
Observer work, and more than it looks: the existing clustering's ids are not stable from one frame to the next, so species need longitudinal matching, split and merge rules, names that follow the match, and a tolerance validated on evolved populations (neighbours within one founder's clade differ by up to all 42 words at the 90th percentile). Bodies hold only 15% to 48% of occupied cells, so "extinct" must tell a last body dying from a lineage living on as film. No physics.
Risk
Over-claiming: an inferred cluster named like a species is a claim the catalogue's honesty rule would flag. State the rule and the purity. A taxonomy that misfires (a species that "dies" and "reappears") confused at least one Bibites player; persistence rules must be strict.

6The capability ladder

Shape 2's bet, drawn. The starting set is what one kind of life can do at step zero; every other rung is a gene a lineage gains by mutation and pays to keep. The order is what the chemistry tends toward, not a rule it enforces: waste also arises by passive decay and light recycles it abiotically, so respiration need not come before decomposition. The note names one hard prerequisite, a wall needs polymer; everything else is soft gating, a gene useless until another is on and so shed by its cost. Hover or focus a rung for its line.

7Devices that force evolution, and what other games got wrong

From two read-only research passes over abstracts, project pages, store pages and devblogs; items marked unverified rest on search summaries. The devices are ranked by how much evidence they have and how cheaply they graft onto this chemistry. Lit numbers are the ones a stage below uses.

  1. A size-dependent selective force, clonal offspring, a bottleneck. Settling selection on yeast produced branched multicellular clusters in about two months, clonal and propagating through single-cell bottlenecks; Avida's Volvocales models evolved germ and soma when survival and reproduction traded off. The pond cycle already supplies nearly clonal packets (measured, not enforced) and a bottleneck. Missing: a force in the field that favours size or attachment within a cycle. A new optional rule, outside the frozen scaffold registration; it would test attached propagules under an imposed cycle, not the north star's endogenous transition.
  2. An energy-paid ladder of rewarded capabilities, with mild intermediate rewards. Lenski, Ofria, Pennock and Adami: complex functions evolve when simpler ones are rewarded too, no single rung is mandatory, and some deleterious steps become stepping stones. Goldsby's task-switching cost evolved division of labour between genetically identical neighbours. No application to a chemistry CA was found; the chemistry-native form is shape 2's genes with a maintenance cost. Risk: organisms game the detector.
  3. Staged environments kept barely passable, with transfer between them. POET co-evolves environments and agents and moves stepping-stone solutions across; minimal criterion coevolution does it with no fitness function at all. The game analogue is the player as the environment and ponds or islands as the places solutions move between. Not tested on Lenia.
  4. Varying what pays, and selective rather than random extinctions. Modularly varying goals evolved modular networks that adapt fast (one failed replication attempt on record). In Avida, extinctions that change the environment reshape recovery where random culls do not. Offer both a meteor and an ice age, and expect them to differ. Cheap: a schedule on existing config.
  5. Offline search to choose the constrained region and the founders. Leniabreeder, curiosity-driven Flow-Lenia and ASAL find more varied starts than random search; the M3 MAP-Elites search is this project's version. It finds interesting starts and does not drive evolution: a tool for designing the game's worlds, not a mechanic in them.

Two warnings from the same pass. Evoloops show the default fate of evolving replicators in a CA: selection converges on the smallest, fastest form, which is what erosion and the shrinking bodies under movement breeding look like here. And the Flow-Lenia authors' later paper is reported to find less evolutionary activity under dissipation and food limits than in the plain system (unverified against the full text), which would argue that an age should change goals, not just scarcity.

The games survey found no independently verified case of a new body plan, multicellularity or predation emerging from a forced pressure in a game: the stages are scripted, or the emergence is a developer's claim. The passes were limited and nothing was tested first hand, so this is a gap in the record, not a proof of absence. Either way the passes turned up no verified recipe to copy.

Failure modes, each one a design rule here

8Recommendation: a staged ladder

Each stage is a test the one before could not run. Stage 0a costs nothing and asks whether watching bred evolution is fun at all. Stage 1 is the physics bet. Stage 2 exists only if the field cannot carry predation. One follow-up runs in parallel and claims nothing for the frozen registration.

9Decisions for the user

Five choices change what gets built. Lit cards carry a recommendation the note states; the others have none.

1Fork or next phase?

A sibling of the lab with its own kernel line that may declare things, the lab's north star untouched; or the lab itself takes the turn and "never declared" is retired.

The note's line: the lab stays the lab; the game is a sibling.

2How much to declare?

Nothing new (Stage 0 only), capabilities (Stage 1), cells (the declared-cell worlds, built and in the catalogue under "Shape and cells"), or creatures (Stage 2).

3Which prototype first?

Stage 0a, an afternoon, no code, testing whether watching bred evolution is fun before any physics is written; or straight to tracks, the bigger bet, on the argument that the breeder's lattice-of-dots will not hold anyone's attention.

The note's line: Stage 0a first.

4What may the player touch?

Pressures and ages only ("slightly interactive"); plus the existing brushes (lesion, feed, drain); plus donor picks by eye. Anything more is a god game.

The note does not choose among the three; it only rules out, as a first move, a creature editor, parameter sliders over the raw physics and the full storm.

5Solo jar or shared garden first?

The breeder works in one browser today; ages do not exist yet in any form. The shared garden (bet 4) would make ages the commons' events and species the things people name together; it needs the coordinator to carry the breed arm and the dial interventions, which it does not.
