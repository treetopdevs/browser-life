1Three kinds that eat each other

The same idea twice: in plain words, then with the model's numbers.

In plain words

Rock beats scissors, scissors beat paper, paper beats rock. Nobody wins for good: whoever is on top is eaten by the one that beats it.

Cover a field with the three kinds. Where two touch, the winner clears the loser's square, and any kind can grow into an empty square beside it. The patches chase each other round and round. If each kind only ever meets its neighbours, the chase bends into spirals that keep turning, and all three survive.

Now let the kinds swap places with their neighbours, faster and faster. The spirals blur and the chase becomes a jumble, so chance decides: one kind dips too low and dies out. If scissors go, nothing is left to eat paper, and paper clears the rock. One kind is left. How fast the kinds mix decides whether three can live together.

Technically
  • The sites of an L × L periodic square lattice are empty or hold species 1, 2 or 3. Species s kills species (s mod 3) + 1: 1 kills 2, 2 kills 3, 3 kills 1. This is the May-Leonard model with dominance removal, as in Reichenbach, Mobilia and Frey (2007).
  • One reaction step picks a site and one of its four neighbours. With probability 1/2 the occupant reproduces into the neighbour if that is empty. Otherwise it kills the neighbour if that is the species it dominates, leaving the site empty. A step that changes nothing still counts. One generation is N = L² reaction steps.
  • Before every reaction step a random number of neighbour swaps happens, with mean ε / 2, where ε = M N / 2. So M = 2ε / N is the mobility.
  • The well-mixed equations orbit a heteroclinic cycle with growing swings. On the lattice, spiral waves hold the three together while M is low. Above a threshold Mc the swings win and two species die out. The paper puts Mc at (4.5 ± 0.5) × 10−4. The meaning of "mobility" differs between papers by about a factor of two.

2Three questions, in this order

Each has a plain version and a technical one. Only the first can be tried in this page, and only as a picture.

Q1Do we understand the model?

Plain. Build the simplest version, a grid of squares, and check that it loses two of the three kinds at about the mixing speed the paper reports. If it does, we have read the model right.

Technical. Lattice reference at L = 20, 30, 40, 60 (100 as a contingency), 11 values of M from 10−5 to 2 × 10−3, 600 runs each. Pext(L, M) is the fraction of runs with at most one species left after N generations; where the curves for growing L cross is Mc. Registered bands: 3 × 10−4 to 6 × 10−4 passes, 2.25 × 10−4 to 9 × 10−4 passes with a caveat. This is the only quantitative comparison with the literature.

Where it stands. Code built and tested. The sweep has not run.

Q2Does the kernel host it?

Plain. Our real simulation is a thin slab of living cells lit from above. It has no swap button, only a spread knob that blurs matter sideways. Add a predation rule and turn that knob: does living together collapse somewhere along it?

Technical. Slab 128 × 128 × 4, a type and a beats-mask in each lineage, a kill in the react pass. Outcome rows A1 to A11 along spread. The kernel has no exchange rate, so no Mc is measured: the check is qualitative. A pilot (Gate 0) comes first, and the scratch evidence expects the plan's own substrate to fail it.

Where it stands. Not built. Waits on Gate 0.

Q3Does the cycle grow?

Plain. Let who-eats-whom be inherited, and change now and then. Does a ring of three ever become a ring of four or five? The papers give no support. We register the question and expect a null.

Technical. Six arms of five seeds: Z (no mutation), A (edges), B (labels), C (both), N (C's operators with predation off), W (controller weights). GROWS needs a clean cycle of four or more types at the end in 4 of C's 5 seeds against none of N's, or in all 5 against at most 1 (one exact Fisher test at 0.05), and a mutant supply. INCONCLUSIVE is a likely honest verdict.

Where it stands. Not built. Runs only if the sweep finds a cliff or a dynamic window.

Run details
  1. Spirals. Press "Spirals" and give it about ten seconds. The three colours chase each other round; where they meet in rings the waves wind into spiral arms. The literature says the wavelength grows with M up to Mc; here you can watch that, and nothing on this page measures it (rule draft 2.5).
  2. Too much mixing. While Spirals runs, drag M to the right, past 3 × 10−4 and on to 2 × 10−3. The arms lose their shape, the shares in the chart start to swing, and given time chance removes two of the kinds.
  3. Chance. Press "Near the threshold", then "New seed" a few times. Same size, same M, different endings: on a small lattice a run can end by luck, which is why Q1 compares four sizes instead of trusting one.

5What the reviews saw, and what this page is not

These are scratch observations from the two reviews of 24 October 2026, made with throwaway code. They explain what the plan expects. None is a result of this line.

Scratch, not a result

Where the lattice crosses

Two independent scratch implementations of the lattice model put the crossing at M = 3.0 to 3.2 × 10−4, about 1.5 times below the paper's 4.5 × 10−4 and on the lower edge of the registered pass band.

Registered. Whether the sweep lands in LAT-PASS or LAT-CAVEAT will be decided by noise, and the write-up will not make the difference a finding. The convention of M and of a generation are the first suspects.

Scratch, not a result

The wavelength check failed

A spectral check of the label field found its peak at wavenumber 1 to 3 for every M (slope 0.08), so the first draft's wavelength rule was dropped.

Registered. No wavelength number is reported in v1. The live canvas shows the wavelength qualitatively and nothing else.

Scratch, not a result

Removal acts as replacement

A shader-text prototype of the kill (128 × 128 × 4, not bit-checked against a CPU twin) kept all three labels for 100,000 steps without the kill, while every killing rate tried (32, 128, 512) had lost a founding label by step 20,000, the last by step 3,000. The living fraction stayed above 98 % from step 4,000.

Registered. The plan's own substrate (S0) is expected to fail Gate 0. Nothing is built for the kernel until a rung does not.

Scratch, not a result

The mobility knob is a staircase

At 28 quanta of matter per cell the inert-stripe meter read 0, 0, 0, 0.018, 0.041, 0.039, 0.041 and 0.031 at spreads 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24 and 32: about three effective levels.

Registered. Gate 0 needs at least 5 levels, and offers rungs with finer quanta (S1, S2) for that.

Scratch, not a result

Noise, then domains

The label correlation length in the scratch carpet was about 2 cells. The images showed noise and then rough single-label domains, and no spiral arms.

Registered. The deep volume needs a median correlation length of at least 6 cells before it runs.

Scratch, not a result

Most mutants die at once

A mutant is a one-cell lineage and a critical branching process in this lottery (about one expected offspring per step), so most die at once.

Registered. The supply of mutant lineages is recorded, and INCONCLUSIVE is a likely honest verdict for Q3.

What this page does not show

  • No kernel run exists. The canvas is the lattice reference, a different model from the kernel. In the kernel, colonization and mobility are one transport, so a cliff may be inverted or absent, and the lattice's Mc has no counterpart.
  • The canvas is a picture of one run. It uses seeds 900 to 999999 only; the registered sweep's seeds start at 2,000,000 and are never used here.
  • Whether this model reproduces the literature's threshold is Q1's answer, and it is not in yet. Nothing here says it does.
  • Spirals and their wavelength are judged by eye. No spiral is detected or measured in the page.
