Bodies held together by a local rule

What does evolution do with a body?

Each cell carries four numbers (its genome) and copies them into new cells. Cells grow into empty tiles that are close enough to a root, bud off seed cells, take food from empty tiles beside them, pass stored food to their neighbours, and starve when food runs short. Switch mutation on and see whether bodies hold, change shape, or thin into a mat. The neutral control lets genes mutate and be inherited but makes the rules ignore them, so you can see what drift alone does.

Designed ingredients: roots, the distance signal, surface foraging, internal food transport and the body-size gene are engineered; bodies are not declared, they are whatever cells stay connected.

Components, interior share and coverage are measured by an observer after the fact; nothing in the rules reads them. A thin film shows as high coverage with one giant component.

Root basins group every cell with the root it drains to along the distance signal; touching bodies count separately there, so they show how many bodies a connected mat holds. Known limits: if the root is killed, the fragment re-roots at its edge and overgrows (about 1.4 to 2.3 times in v2); a lone body holds for thousands of steps and then a re-rooting event lets it spread; in a crowded world the bodies tile the whole world into one mat. Reported, not tuned away.
