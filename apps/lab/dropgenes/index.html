Droplet genes · first rung

Compartments that have to keep their genes

A compartment here is a patch of dense wall. Nothing in the rules declares one: a program finds them afterwards by looking for connected tiles of wall. A gene is a kind of template made of the same conserved matter as everything else. It copies itself from food where a tile has free sites, and wall is only made where every gene has copies close by. The parasite is a template that copies twice as fast and does nothing. In the recorded runs (a 64 x 64 arena, four seeds, 200,000 steps) with two genes and the parasite, the population of compartments lasted on all four seeds only at the larger setting and only with the gate: with the gate off it had ended by step 5,100, and at the default compartment setting offered here it lasted on one seed of four. Nothing in this world evolves yet. The number of genes is a setting, the genes are interchangeable, and the parasite is one fixed type.

Designed ingredients: the wall chemistry, the genes, the gate and the parasite are all written in. Compartments are not declared: a compartment is whatever patch of dense wall happens to hold together. The write-up is docs/droplet-genes-2026-11-21/RESULTS.md in the repository.

    Compartments, copies and the parasite share are read from the picture you see. A compartment counts as carrying every gene when it holds at least one copy of each somewhere; the rules are stricter, and make wall on a tile only when every gene has copies in the 3 x 3 patch around it. Splits and deaths are inferred by comparing the patches every 50 steps, so they are bookkeeping, not events the world knows about. The matter check adds up every piece in the arena exactly; it must read res 0.

    Limits of what you see. This is one arena size and one parasite rate, the top of the grid that was tried. The larger setting doubles three diffusion rates at once and was recorded at one site count, so which of its changes matters is not known. A world that is still populated after a few thousand steps here says little: one recorded world was lost after step 100,000. In the recorded runs, late on, between 14 and 25 percent of the copies sat on the thin wall around compartments, outside what the program counts as a compartment; here such copies are drawn as dim blue specks, pink where they are parasite copies.
