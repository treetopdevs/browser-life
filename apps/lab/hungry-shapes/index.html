Food allocation comparison

When food gets scarce, where can shapes grow?

Three matched worlds share one seed, founders, inherited programs and mutation. They differ only in how renewing food is offered: the original supply, the same scarce budget spread across the map, or concentrated in founder-centered patches.

Designed ingredients: bodies, growth, food, reproduction, inheritance and the founder-centered patches are explicit rules. The patches are supplied around known starting locations; they are not discovered niches.

All worlds begin with 20 food units on every tile. Those initial reserves can hide the effect of later scarcity.

Fixed first-seed result · historical record

Stop after seed 1201.

Original supply: 48 bodies alive at tick 3,000. Scarce uniform: 39 alive at tick 3,000. Scarce patches: extinct at tick 2,040, after reaching generation 8.

The patch arm did not meet the continuation gate of surviving through tick 3,000. The v1 revision trigger was not met. Decision: no seeds 1202 or 1203, and no v2 food design.

These are fixed seed 1201 results; resetting or advancing the interactive worlds above does not change this historical record.

The evidence is in the repository: docs/creative-prototypes/hungry-shapes.html. Its companion recording is kept outside git (docs/creative-prototypes/ARTIFACTS.md).

How to read the comparison

Supply and allocation are separate ingredients.

The original world offers twelve times as much food per pulse as either scarce world. The scarce uniform arm is a deliberately harsh, spread-out floor; the patch arm concentrates that same scarce budget around known founder sites.

“Offered” counts attempted food additions. “Accepted” is what fit below the per-tile food ceiling; “overflow” was offered but not accepted. Population and supply stop changing in an extinct world, and each card reports its actual last tick against the shared comparison clock.

This is an engineered toy. Living bodies, inherited programs, reproduction, age limits and food targeting are explicit rules. An observed shape or survival difference does not establish adaptation or emergence.
