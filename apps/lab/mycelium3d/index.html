Exploration, not a result

Mycelium in a jar.

The mycelium world, grown in three dimensions. Particles with small neural controllers eat, lay trail and split inside a closed jar. Light enters at the top and is shaded by what grows there, so food is made only near the surface. This is a new world under exploration: nothing on this page is a result.

A new world, no results claimed. The rules were written on 15 November 2026 and the default numbers were chosen so a population lasts. No registered test has been run on this world. Its screens so far are explorations judged by eye and by counts, and the sinking, trail and bond options are here to look at, not because anything has been shown about them.

What the particles do

Each bead is a particle with a body of matter, a store of energy and its own small neural controller (13 inputs, 8 hidden units, 9 outputs). It senses nutrient, waste and trail around it, the trail and nutrient above and below it, and how much light reaches it. Then it decides how to move east, south and down, how much to eat, how much trail to lay and whether to split. A child takes half its parent's matter and energy and a copy of its controller that sometimes mutates. Light turning waste back into nutrient is the only energy coming in, and it falls from the top: each layer passes on less of it, shaded by the trail and bodies above.

What was checked, and what was not

Matter is conserved exactly and energy is booked exactly; the two residuals in the census stay at 0, and the page stops if either ever is not. On your GPU the world runs as WebGPU code that matched the CPU reference bit for bit in the project's parity test (62 cases): that says the port is faithful where the test ran, not that it has been checked on every GPU. Without WebGPU the page runs the CPU reference itself, at the default size only. Nothing about evolution, trail use or lifting matter has been measured here yet.

A body is two or more particles joined by bonds, directly or along a chain; each particle holds at most two bonds, so a body is a chain or a ring. Bonded particles are those holding at least one bond, with their share of all particles. In the 3D view each bond is a pale link between its partners (in the particles and haze way of drawing only); a bond across a wrapped wall is drawn as two short stubs, one from each partner to the wall.

Layer 0 is the surface, where light enters. Body is the matter inside particles; nutrient (food), waste and trail are the matter in the water. The four add up to the same total every step. Light is the share of the unshaded surface light that reaches the layers, on average. Matter from 10,000 up is rounded to three figures (k is thousands, M millions). Births refused are splits turned down because the jar had no room for the child.

Why this is not a result
  • Nothing has been tested on this world. A crowd of beads or a haze of trail is not an organism, and a family that spreads may only be lucky.
  • A family is a set of particles with identical controllers. A mutation always starts a new one, so the family count measures mutation and survival together, not adaptation.
  • The default numbers were tuned by hand on the 32 × 32 × 16 world on one seed so a population lasts. The bigger sizes keep the same numbers per cell; they are less tested.
  • In the flat world, trail that only costs was purged by inheritance, the toll road kept trail that nothing was found using, and the owner conduit with the gate kept trail in 6 of 8 worlds. Those are flat-world explorations. They are why these options are offered here, and say nothing about this world.
  • Bonds join particles into chains and rings that move together. A body of bonded beads is a shape the rules allow, not an organism; in the exploration runs bodies were mostly lost again, with or without upkeep.
  • The question the jar is built to ask: sinking carries waste away from the light, so does anything evolve that lifts matter back up? Nothing here answers it yet.
