First rung

Does a group find the moving sun better than the same bodies alone?

Four bodies sit in a small jar lit by a sun that slides along it. Some can signal in the dark and move away from signal, so a group could, in principle, drift toward the light together. We run the same four bodies packed together and spread apart, in the same jar with the same competition, and ask whether the group does better than the spread-out bodies, beyond what three comparison strategies do. Bodies moving together is not the test: a sun pulling everything the same way looks like teamwork too.

Exploratory, CPU only, no GPU evidence, mutation off. A local replay, kept off the public site. What the pre-stated rules say

Can the naive reading be fooled? Fixtures

Synthetic tracks. The naive reading is "bodies move the same way as each other". When the sun alone drags every body along (common-forcing) it reads collective motion, but the residual coordination, which removes the sun's own movement and compares grouped against spread-out bodies, is inside the noise floor. Coupled bodies are detected; a frozen world is reported unavailable, never zero. In tracking-asymmetry the grouped bodies follow the sun fully and the spread-out ones only half: the residual comes out negative and is rejected, and without the sun subtraction it would read as coordination.
