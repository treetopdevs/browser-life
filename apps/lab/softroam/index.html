Soft Roam · local probe, not on the site

This is Roam moved off its grid. The same kind of body (cells joined together, grown from a plan, fed by patches of food that drop onto the ground) now lives on a smooth surface instead of a grid of tiles. Cells push each other apart, joined cells pull on each other like springs, so a body glides, bends and turns instead of hopping from square to square. Bodies that lose a cell can break in two, and the pieces swim apart.

Each coloured soft shape is a body, with a dark seam where it meets another. The blue haze is food on the ground: a patch drops now and then, and then slowly spreads out into haze until it is eaten. A cell that has eaten enough divides: the new cell is its daughter and the cell it came from is her mother. A daughter that stays joined grows the body; one that is let go starts a new body. Between a mother and a daughter the thin bright strand is matter flowing to the one with less (brighter means a bigger difference). A cell that starves, or simply wears out, dies: it fades away and its matter goes onto the ground as a small puff of food, so a body can break where a cell is lost.

In the seasons start a bright band half the world wide sweeps round the arena once a day, and the whole world brightens and dims once a year. Green leaf cells live on that light, hunters see prey from a distance and prey run from hunters, and a body only lets go a new body once it has grown big enough. Pick the garden to see the same bodies without any of that: the dials for the sun, senses and manners are set to off, and the seasons' own food and upkeep numbers go back to the garden's.

The big bodies start (the page's own) runs the same sun and year with bodies of 21 to 25 cells: armoured herds with shells down both flanks, groves with leaves sticking out to the light, and pack hunters with three mouths across the front. A body heals: where a cell dies the cross-braces hold the rest together and the cell that made it grows it again. A body with a mouth that sees prey turns as a whole after it, and a body that sees a hunter turns away. Several mouths together can open a body too big for one, two bodies that could each eat the other leave each other alone, and hunters prefer prey no other hunter is at. A share of the upkeep is kept for the leaves, and winter costs: every cell pays a little more in the dark of the year, so winter thins bodies and the numbers swing with the year.

Nothing is made or lost: every unit of matter is on the ground, inside a cell, in the store that drops the next patch, or in the store kept for leaves. The matter check below should always say balanced.
