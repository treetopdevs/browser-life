First causal rung

Does what a builder leaves behind help a receiver?

A builder lineage conditions a small world: it lays down polymer and reshapes the nutrient, waste and energy around it. Then the builder is taken away, a receiver is placed on empty ground, and we ask whether the leftovers, and not something else, help it. Every transfer moves matter and energy by exact accounting, and the same receiver is also run against copies of the world that keep the totals but lose the structure, or give the receiver the energy alone.

Exploratory, CPU only, no GPU evidence, mutation off. A local experiment, kept off the public site. A negative here means "no causal benefit detected in this small panel, horizon and field", not that a second link is impossible.

Second rung: free-energy-matched

Same question, with fairer comparators and a better-powered readout

The first rung could not tell whether a negative came from the field or from the receiver-alone controls holding more free energy (for one block, 22,169 against 9,917, at equal total energy). This rung adds comparators that match the field's free energy exactly, sums each arm over several replicate runs to push the noise floor down, and asks whether a hand-built polymer wall of a realistic dose can be seen at all before any fresh builder block is run.

Exploratory, CPU only, no GPU evidence, mutation off. Local, kept off the public site. A negative would only mean a consistent benefit was not detected under these rules, not that there is no benefit and not that a second link is impossible.

    Can the old inoculum comparator be fooled? far-field-energy

    far-field-energy is a field that differs from its controls only by extra free energy laid outside the inoculum footprint and halo, no polymer. The first rung's inoculum contrast reads it as a benefit; the free-energy-matched contrast does not. The full gate rejects it on the polymer, space and free-energy-matched inoculum contrasts (the polymer ablation contrast at 0 of 8 is one reason), so the claim is about the inoculum contrast alone.
