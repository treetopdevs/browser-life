    What we built into this world

    This is a separate 2D toy model inspired by Candidate 9’s cyclic ecology and immune-clone result. It does not run the 3D research kernel and is not evidence of improved survival in that system.

    We deliberately supplied food renewal, reproduction, heredity, mutation, local refuges and seasons. Jade eats Amber, Amber eats Violet and Hunter, Violet eats Jade and Protected. Protected eats Amber; Hunter eats Protected. Attacks have a chance to fail. Protected descendants with defense 2 or higher are deliberately immune to Violet; hunters bypass that special protection. Defense also reduces ordinary attack success, at an ongoing energy cost.

    At tick 100 we plant a highly defended family; at tick 300 we plant its costly attacker. Manual planting before a scheduled arrival takes its place. Their descendants inherit attack and defense (0–4), with a 4% chance of a one-point change per birth. Both traits cost food. Maximum attack and defense together cost more than a cell can gather from its patch each tick. Successful attacks replace prey with offspring even below the ordinary birth threshold, adding 3 energy units from an external supply before splitting parental energy.

    Refuges reduce attack success for everyone. Every 500 ticks a local drought slows food renewal for 120 ticks. No family is secretly revived. Extinction is possible; you can replant explicitly. Colors show broad founding families, not a full ancestry tree. Observations retain the last 300 population samples and 100 notices, not a replayable history.
