Walls made of matter

A cell that divides because of its shape

A wall here is a ring of lipid beads, and lipid is a fixed stock that walls and the open water trade back and forth. The dots inside are solute: they crowd against the wall and stretch it. A stretched wall takes up lipid more readily and gives it up less, so a crowded cell grows wall at the expense of a slack one. When a cell has more wall than its contents can fill, the wall folds, a neck closes, and one cell becomes two.

In the garden worlds the solute also lives: a particle copies itself from food only when it has company, makes lipid from food, and decays. With heredity on, every copy can shift how much of its effort goes to making lipid. In the first heredity world a particle that makes less lipid copies itself faster, and in headless runs those cells stopped dividing and nearly all died. In the second, copying speed is the same for every particle, and three runs of 1.5 million steps stayed alive.

Designed ingredients: the wall springs, the lipid exchange rule, the rule that a closing neck reconnects, the outside pressure and the chemistry are all engineered. Cells are not declared: a cell is whatever a ring of bonds happens to enclose, and nothing in the rules tells a cell to divide or die.

In the thread worlds a thread is a clear ring, coloured by its idle marker (gene 1; the lipid share is gene 0). Solute copies only within reach of a thread with no wall between, takes that thread's lipid share, and a cell with no thread stops making solute. Threads copy when they have stored enough food and no other thread is near; a thread with too little solute around it decays.

Wall colour shows strain: orange is stretched, pale is at rest, violet is squeezed. Cell counts, contents and families are read by an observer that walks the rings; the rules never see them. Matter total counts every bead, solute particle, food, waste and lipid unit: it only changes when you drop something in.

Known limits: this is a flat world, so a cell's wall is a line and its volume an area. Cells do not fuse. A wall leaks a little solute where it sheds beads. Positions are floating point, so a run repeats exactly on one machine and any standard JavaScript engine, but there is no GPU twin.
