Each coloured piece is a body: a few cells joined together, outlined where it meets another body. A body moves as one piece, eats the food under it (a blue haze in the garden look, green in the others), and shares what it eats among its cells. Cells that are full divide; while the body is small the new cell stays joined, and once it is adult size the new cell is let go as a new body. A body that runs short gives up cells one at a time and then dies.

With a grown start, a body's outline comes from a plan every one of its cells carries: a short list of steps that says where a cell puts its next daughter, whether it keeps her or lets her go as a new body, and which step each of them does next. The planted plans are written by hand. With copy errors on, a new body's plan sometimes differs from its parent's in one place, and the outlines that follow are ones nobody drew.

New, 2026-11-24: matter has to travel. With seeping on, a body no longer shares one store. Each cell eats into its own store and pays its own upkeep, and matter seeps along bonds from fuller cells to emptier ones. A cell that runs short dies. So a big body reaches more food and wins more touch contests, but its far parts are fed slowly, and when a cell that held two parts together dies, the body breaks in two. When a cell dies, its mother goes back to the step of her plan that made it and grows it again, and a piece cut off starts its plan again, like a cutting. There is no size limit.

New, later the same day: the dead feed the ground. With this on, a cell that dies leaves what it held on the ground where it died, as food. Busy bodies lose many cells, so they leave a trail behind them, and patches come a little less often (the same matter is now on the ground instead). A slower body that follows the trail can live on it. Kept daughters can now also get copy errors in their genes, so how much a body chases and moves can change over a run.

New, 2026-11-24 evening: kinds of cell. Each step of a growth plan now also says what the daughter becomes. A shell (drawn pale) does not eat, costs a quarter of the usual upkeep on its open sides, and cannot be fed on. A mouth (drawn red) does not eat from the ground either, but is the only kind of cell that can feed on another body by touch (still only on one at most half its size, and never through a shell). Plain cells eat from the ground and can be fed on. So a body that hunts must also carry cells that graze, and shells cost a body its grazing. A cell that is let go keeps the kind its plan gives it, and a body of one cell feeds itself from the ground whatever its kind, so a shell let go is an armoured seed: it grazes, cannot be eaten, and stops grazing once it has grown a body. Every other founder of each type is a hunter: the daughter its plan's first step keeps is a mouth (one mouth at the front of a line, plate, cross or block; on a bush every forward tip, since its daughters run that step again; specks keep no daughters, so have no hunters). No founder has shells. Shells, and mouths anywhere else, appear only through copy errors in plans.

With touch feeding on, a body that is at least twice the size of one it touches takes matter from it through every side where they meet, so small bodies and half-grown ones are food for large ones. A new body is safe for its first 100 steps, and spends them moving straight away from its parent.

Food comes only in patches. Everything bodies use up goes to one store, and that store drops it back as a new patch somewhere else (with "the dead feed the ground" on, what dead cells held stays where they died instead). A body cannot wait for a patch to come to it.
