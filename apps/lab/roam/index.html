Each coloured piece is a body: a few cells joined together, outlined where it meets another body. A body moves as one piece, eats the food (green) under it, and shares what it eats among its cells. Cells that are full divide; while the body is small the new cell stays joined, and once it is adult size the new cell is let go as a new body. A body that runs short gives up cells one at a time and then dies.

Food comes only in patches. Everything bodies use up or leave behind goes to one store, and that store drops it back as a new patch somewhere else. A body cannot wait for food to come to it.

The table gives each family's average genes. chase: speed toward stronger food smell, as a share of top speed. drive: speed straight ahead. turn: how often the body picks a new heading (-1 never). adult: the size at which new cells are let go. shape: above 0 new cells go in front or behind (long bodies), below 0 to the side (wide bodies). A cell loses matter through every side that faces outward, so a compact body lasts longer between meals than the same cells strung out or apart.
