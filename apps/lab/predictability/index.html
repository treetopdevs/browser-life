An observer benchmark

The same observer, nine movies and one ecology

Each movie is read by the same few simple predictors that see only the past. How hard a movie is to predict depends on what it is made of, and on how the observer looks at it. Randomness is hard to predict without being alive.

CPU only: deterministic integer fixtures, no GPU evidence. Local experiment, kept off the public site. It checks whether cheap readouts can be fooled; it does not say whether anything here is alive.

Why this is not yet evidence of life

  • Independent noise can defeat temporal prediction without being alive.
  • Duplicated motifs repeat the same predictive structure rather than adding any: nine gliders make exactly nine times the errors of one.
  • Losses depend on the observer (resolution, horizon and method), and rankings across different alphabets (2 values against 16) are not comparable.
  • The check that a ranking could flip with the resolution is weak here: at the finest view nearly every Life movie is predicted exactly, ties do not count as flips, and a view where nothing can be scored drops out of the ranking instead of counting as a change. "No flip" says little.
  • Resistance to deflate compression is not algorithmic incompressibility. Compression is shown apart from prediction and never blended with it.
  • Life is a calibration world. It does not make chemistry or energy bookkeeping unnecessary.
  • That our simple predictors fail on a movie does not show computational irreducibility.
  • The ecology clip is one small world and one seed, an illustration and not an ensemble result.
