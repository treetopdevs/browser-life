How to read it

Labels say how far to trust a row

38 results so far: 10 measured 8 negative 8 open 6 exploratory 3 unclear 2 confirmed 1 not yet measured

Confirmed is kept for tests whose rules were frozen before their data. Negative means a test with pre-stated rules came back no. Measured and Exploratory are bounded counts and exploratory tests: real numbers, few seeds, no claim beyond them, though some also fixed their rules before running. Open and Unclear mean the test could not tell, usually because the instrument failed its own check first. That is different from a no.

Nothing on this page is evidence of open-ended evolution.

Can rules this simple grow something that mends itself?

  • Confirmed · 27 Sep

    Regenerating founders A screen found genetically distinct seeds that regrow after a wound removes about 30% of a body, and die without light. Passing this gate does not establish open-ended evolution.

2 · The long run

Left alone, does it keep evolving?

  • Negative · 28 Sep

    Open-ended evolution (M4) All 170 registered runs finished with exact conservation, but the milestone was not met: neutral runs, where mutations change nothing, also counted as “growing”.

    Sources: docs/plan.md, experiments/preregistration.md

  • Measured · 29 Sep

    Where variation stalls Genomes are copied faithfully, but mutation rarely changes what a genome does: the mutants of only 1 of the 12 founders reliably took up a new role.

    Sources: docs/plan.md

  • Measured · 30 Sep

    Founders and environments What the founders were mattered more than how varied they were. Whether new roles keep arriving after niches first fill is mixed.

    Sources: docs/plan.md, docs/reassessment-2026-09-30.md

  • Negative · 4 Oct

    A search for self-renewing habitats None of the 24 main pilot cases qualified as a self-renewing habitat. A negative for this panel only, with one pilot seed per habitat.

    Sources: evolvability-discovery-2026-10-04/README.md

  • Negative · 24 Oct

    Recovery, retested A mutant that had looked better at regrowing tied its founder: both recovered in 28 of 32 trials, with four wins either way. A futility screen, not proof that recovery cannot improve.

    Sources: recovery-accessibility-2026-10-24/RESULTS.md

3 · The instruments

Were our instruments seeing what we thought?

  • Measured · 29 Sep

    What counts as an individual In 135 of 200 sampled births, the parent our tracker credited had contributed no copied material at the time of the birth. A tracked birth is not, by itself, evidence that an individual reproduced.

    Sources: docs/plan.md

  • Measured · 1 Oct

    Measures that need work None of the four activity, novelty and role measures met every validation requirement; three flagged too many neutral runs.

    Sources: docs/plan.md

  • Negative · 18 Oct

    Change read from the mutation tree Three measures read from who descends from whom all failed their checks; in one world every one of them ranked neutral runs as more “selective” than real ones.

    Sources: docs/clade-measures-2026-10-18.md

  • Exploratory · 1 Nov

    Which “how predictable” scores are fooled? On test movies with known answers, nine copies of one glider gave exactly nine times the errors of one. Error count alone shows no new structure. Predictability atlas →

    Sources: predictability-atlas-2026-11-01/EVIDENCE.md

  • Exploratory · 2 Nov

    Can a measure see repeated building blocks? Two readouts told a hand-built hierarchy from duplicated random shapes; a naive copies-times-cost score got it backwards every time. Nothing was read from real ecology clips. Assembly observer →

    Sources: assembly-observer-2026-11-02/EVIDENCE.md

4 · A hand on the dial

Can we turn it up by hand?

  • Measured · 1 Oct

    A life cycle for groups Under an imposed pond life cycle, heritable differences between ponds appeared in 6 of 6 histories. Random-donor ponds showed them too, so the cycle’s causal role is not shown.

    Sources: docs/scaffold-protocol-v1.md, docs/scaffold-heredity-replication-v1.md

  • Confirmed · 3 Oct

    The pond result, registered With rules frozen first and 24 histories per arm, both primary hypotheses were confirmed: cycled worlds founded new ponds more often than the controls. A separate genome-swap test found a small benefit; it does not measure the genome’s share of the advantage.

    Sources: docs/scaffold-registration-v1.md

  • Not yet measured · 3 Oct

    Can ponds select themselves? The next step takes our hand off the ranking. Its entry checks passed; the evolutionary stage is prepared and has not run.

    Sources: docs/scaffold-transition-hunt-v1.md

  • Measured · 4 Oct

    Breeding, storms and bodies Sandbox looks, one seed each. Eight screens of rules meant to make evolution keep bodies produced no arm that met its pre-stated bar for promising.

    Sources: docs/sandbox-wild.md, docs/sandbox-ownership.md, docs/sandbox-cells.md

  • Measured · 16 Oct

    A first gene A gainable decomposition gene swept to about 99% of living cells in all three seeds. It paid for the lineage, not for the world: the worlds ended as a decomposing film.

    Sources: docs/evolution-game-brainstorm-2026-10-16.md, docs/announce-gene-v0-2026-10-16.md

  • Unclear · 16 Oct

    A second gene Confounded by its own rule: a gene that costs the same and does nothing reached about half the living cells in two of three control seeds.

    Sources: docs/evolution-game-brainstorm-2026-10-16.md

  • Unclear · 21 Oct

    A gene that does With and without forced building, the paid gene stayed at or under 1% of living cells. Unclear: no difference these seeds can see, not proof that there is none.

    Sources: docs/evolution-game-brainstorm-2026-10-16.md

  • Negative · 22 Oct

    A world that rewards building We looked for a world where building pays. In the second screen, seven worlds and 103 runs, none passed its pre-stated test, so the gene arms never ran.

    Sources: docs/evolution-game-brainstorm-2026-10-16.md

5 · A second pair of eyes

Can a machine notice for us?

  • Negative · 17 Oct

    An anomaly bell A bell meant to ring when a run strays from neutral rang on 30 of 70 neutral runs, where its bar allowed at most 2. It rings on drift.

    Sources: docs/naturalist-bell-v0-2026-10-17.md

  • Measured · 18 Oct

    A quieter bell With its two noisiest signals dropped, the bell rang on none of 70 new neutral runs and on all 10 treatment runs. It tells kinds of run apart; it does not hear something arise inside one.

    Sources: docs/naturalist-bell-v1-plan-2026-10-17.md

  • Negative · 18 Oct

    Can the bell hear a wound? Given twin worlds that differ by one deliberate wound, the bell rang on 4 of 10 for the largest wound, where its rule needed 8 of 10.

    Sources: docs/naturalist-bell-v2-2026-10-18.md

  • Exploratory · 31 Oct

    Can an image model see a takeover? On one known takeover it passed, thinly. A rise in its score means “something visibly changed”, not “life happened”. Program soup →

    Sources: docs/soup-asal-calibration-2026-10-31.md

  • Negative · 2 Nov

    Can we predict what a nudge will do? A simple trend rule was wrong on 73 of 96 held-out cases and scored worse than a baseline that ignores the evidence.

    Sources: prediction-confirm-2026-11-02/RESULT.md, prediction-envelope-2026-11-01/EVIDENCE.md

Things to run

Pages you can run or watch

Most of these are explorations and prototypes. They are here to look at; what you see on them is not evidence, and several run small separate worlds that do not use the jar’s physics.

  • The lab The main jar: plant a world, wound it, feed it, save it.

  • World catalogue Every starting world, with notes on what has been seen in it.

  • Program soup A grid of tiny programs that run each other. Watch for self-copiers.

  • Mycelium world Particles with small neural controllers that eat, lay trail and split.

  • Slab viewer The two thin 3D worlds, drawn layer by layer. Neither passed its gate.

  • The shared garden One world grown in turns by whoever is tending it. It is open: visitors can drop a stone in it or send a sun.

  • Evolving ecosystem An engineered toy ecosystem of five families, with an archive of saved runs.

  • Food bands Run the food-delivery screens live, side by side, with the food drawn as it moves.

  • Hungry shapes Bodies run by small programs, under even or patchy food.

  • Particle playground Particle worlds with oases, seasons, bonds and motifs.

  • Spatial particle worlds The particle playground in a volume.

  • Growing families Bodies that develop from a seed, compared side by side.

  • Boundary fields A standalone field prototype about edges.

  • A counted aquarium A small world where every unit of matter is counted.

  • Nutrient currents A toy with selective nutrient currents. Its recorded replay needs files that are not on this site.

  • Vision screen A saved gallery from calibrating a vision model as a judge.

  • Prototype library A directory of the creative prototypes.
