How to read it

Labels say how far to trust a row

60 results so far: 24 exploratory 12 measured 9 open 8 negative 3 confirmed 3 unclear 1 not yet measured

Confirmed is kept for tests whose rules were frozen before their data. Negative means a test with pre-stated rules came back no. Measured and Exploratory are bounded counts and exploratory tests: real numbers, few seeds, no claim beyond them, though some also fixed their rules before running. Open and Unclear mean the test could not tell, usually because the instrument failed its own check first. That is different from a no.

Nothing on this page is evidence of open-ended evolution.

2 · The long run

  • Negative · 28 Sep

    Where variation stalls Genomes are copied faithfully, but mutation rarely changes what a genome does: the mutants of only 1 of the 12 founders reliably took up a new role.

    Founders and environments What the founders were mattered more than how varied they were. Whether new roles keep arriving after niches first fill is mixed.

3 · The instruments

Were our instruments seeing what we thought?

  • Measured · 29 Sep

    What counts as an individual In 135 of 200 sampled births, the parent our tracker credited had contributed no copied material at the time of the birth. A tracked birth is not, by itself, evidence that an individual reproduced.

    Sources: docs/plan.md

  • Measured · 1 Oct

    Measures that need work None of the four activity, novelty and role measures met every validation requirement; three flagged too many neutral runs.

    Sources: docs/plan.md

  • Negative · 18 Oct

    Change read from the mutation tree Three measures read from who descends from whom all failed their checks; in one world every one of them ranked neutral runs as more “selective” than real ones.

    Sources: docs/clade-measures-2026-10-18.md

  • Exploratory · 1 Nov

    Which “how predictable” scores are fooled? On test movies with known answers, nine copies of one glider gave exactly nine times the errors of one. Error count alone shows no new structure. Predictability atlas →

    Sources: predictability-atlas-2026-11-01/EVIDENCE.md

  • Exploratory · 2 Nov

    Can a measure see repeated building blocks? Two readouts told a hand-built hierarchy from duplicated random shapes; a naive copies-times-cost score got it backwards every time. Nothing was read from real ecology clips. Assembly observer →

    Sources: assembly-observer-2026-11-02/EVIDENCE.md

  • Exploratory · 19 Oct

    A null matched on demography No treatment run had a neutral run of similar size and lineage count to match, in either world, so the existing runs cannot supply a matched null. The confound stays open; a fail would not have shown selection absent either.

    Sources: docs/clade-measures-2026-10-18.md

  • Open · 25 Oct

    A recovery check that stopped itself A planned mass-targeted recovery test stopped at its engineering stage: with the physical states matching, two observation schedules followed different remnants. A technical stop, with no recovery claim.

    Sources: recovery-severity-2026-10-24/STAGE0.md, recovery-severity-2026-10-24/TRACKER-DIAGNOSIS.json, recovery-severity-2026-10-24/V4-IMPLEMENTATION.md

  • Exploratory · 2 Nov

    Can the observer tell rebuilding from copying? On a toy with answers we set, a readout that needs the class back after total destruction and a deletable store was right on 128 of 128; a naive level check on 48 of 128. It cannot tell a stored recipe from a stored image, and heredity is untested.

    Sources: assembly-destruction-2026-11-02/PROTOCOL.md, assembly-destruction-2026-11-02/evidence.json, assembly-destruction-2026-11-02/REVIEW.md

Things to run

Pages you can run or watch

Most of these are explorations and prototypes. They are here to look at; what you see on them is not evidence, and several run small separate worlds that do not use the jar’s physics.

  • The lab The main jar: plant a world, wound it, feed it, save it.

  • World catalogue Every starting world, with notes on what has been seen in it.

  • Program soup A grid of tiny programs that run each other. Watch for self-copiers.

  • Mycelium world Particles with small neural controllers that eat, lay trail and split.

  • Slab viewer The two thin 3D worlds and the taller jar, drawn layer by layer or as a volume you can turn. Neither thin world passed its gate, and the jar has only been judged by eye.

  • Mycelium in a jar The mycelium world in 3D, lit from the top, drawn as a volume you can turn, with bonds and a cursor to wound or feed it. Explored with screens, not tests: on twelve fresh seeds a world of bodies was not seen again.

  • The shared garden One world grown in turns by whoever is tending it. It is open: visitors can drop a stone in it or send a sun.

  • Evolving ecosystem An engineered toy ecosystem of five families, with an archive of saved runs.

  • Food bands Run the food-delivery screens live, side by side, with the food drawn as it moves.

  • Hungry shapes Bodies run by small programs, under even or patchy food.

  • Particle playground Particle worlds with oases, seasons, bonds and motifs.

  • Spatial particle worlds The particle playground in a volume.

  • Growing families Bodies that develop from a seed, compared side by side.

  • Boundary fields A standalone field prototype about edges.

  • A counted aquarium A small world where every unit of matter is counted.

  • Nutrient currents A toy with selective nutrient currents. Its recorded replay needs files that are not on this site.

  • Vision screen A saved gallery from calibrating a vision model as a judge.

  • Prototype library A directory of the creative prototypes.
