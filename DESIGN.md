# Design system

## Direction

The frame is a garden (2026-10-06, replacing the dark-graphite field notebook of 2026-10-04): every world is a sealed jar under a lamp, the lab is the bench the jar stands on, and the public site is the garden around it. The register is utilitarian with friendliness, in the manner of Ableton Live and classic Facebook: flat, chunky, plainly labelled controls; lit states instead of decoration; one committed colour; big friendly type; a voice that says what things do in ordinary words. Nothing is toy-like and nothing is a museum: the chips still say "seen, not measured" where that is the truth.

The lab keeps its three-column bench: Plant a world, In your hand and Keep a copy on the left; the jar, its transport and How it's going in the centre; the honest answers on the right. Every evidence panel is titled with the question it answers (Is the jar still sealed? Does it replay the same? What's in this cell? Who's living here?), and every control says what it will do before it is used and what it did afterwards.

## Colour and type

- Day is the default: warm paper chrome (`--bg`, `--surface`, `--raised`, hue 88) around a dark jar (`--field`, which stays dark in both themes). Night keeps the same roles in warm dusk colours. The toggle in every header reads Day or Night and follows the person across pages.
- One committed colour, the sun (`--sun`, with `--sun-ink` for the same hue as text on paper and `--sun-soft` for tinted backgrounds). The sun marks what is lit: the tool in your hand, the chosen view, the one primary action, a check in progress, and on the site the numbers, eyebrows, the invitation band and the highlighted word in the hero. It is never decoration.
- The name is Cadence Garden (cadence.garden). Its mark is a sealed jar under a rising half-sun with three green cells growing in a beat: the jar in `--field` with an ink outline and lid, the sun in `--sun`, the cells in `--life-fill`. It is inline SVG (`.brand-mark`, styled in `tokens.css`) so it follows the theme; `public/favicon.svg` is the same drawing with fixed colours and a dark-scheme outline. The wordmark is "Cadence" in 800 and " Garden" in 500 muted.
- Green belongs to the living: the cells in the brand mark, Play while the world is growing (`--life-fill` under ink, and the label changes to Pause), the growing status dot, and a clean pass (`--success`, `.badge.ok`). Red (`--error`) is a failure and nothing else.
- The pools keep their colours (blue nutrient, green biomass, white membrane, brown waste) and mean pools and nothing else; a label accompanies every key. On the home page the ingredients mosaic uses the same four colours plus the sun, so the site teaches the lab's legend. History charts are drawn in the text colour.
- Tokens live in `apps/lab/src/tokens.css`, imported by `style.css` (lab) and `site.css` (site). Inputs and selects take `--control-line`, which holds 3:1 on `--surface` and `--bg` (they never sit on `--raised`); `--soft` text holds 4.5:1 on `--surface` and `--bg`, and `--muted` on `--raised` too. Only `oklch` colours; no pure black or white anywhere.
- One typeface, Rubik (variable, latin subset, self-hosted from `/fonts/`; the privacy page says so), with the system stack behind it; a monospace stack for coordinates, measurements and status. The lab's scale is six fixed steps (`--t-micro` 11px to `--t-title` 20px); nothing is set smaller than 11px. The site sets display headings at weight 800 with tight tracking, eyebrows in Rubik small caps rather than monospace, and body copy at 17px with a 58 to 66ch measure.

## Components and behaviour

- Buttons are chunky flat chips with an 8px radius: `--raised` at rest, `--hover` on hover, the sun under ink for `.primary` and for a selected `.seg` button (`.on`, `aria-pressed`), 45% opacity when disabled. Focus is a 2px ink ring with an offset, for keyboard focus only.
- Play is the one primary action above the jar and lights green while growing; while a pond cycle waits, Breed takes over in a bar under the field and Play steps back to a plain button. Controls that cannot act until then stay visible, disabled, with the reason beside them.
- The Starting world field explains its choice: under the select, the question the world asks, its traits as chips (light, size, who is planted, and any rule that differs from the base rules), and a chip saying whether it ran in a registered test (Registered), is one of the first worlds (Base world) or is exploratory (Sandbox). Browse all worlds opens the catalogue over the bench, a dialog with the families down the side (Start here, Steady jars, Islands and ponds, A moving sun, Who owns the matter, Shape and cells, Storms and the breeder) and the worlds of one family as cards: what each asks, its traits lever by lever with the numbers under them, how it differs from its base world (computed from the two configs), what to look for, what has been seen so far in the journal's chip colours, and the technical description folded away for researchers. Choose this world chooses in the select and hands focus to Plant, which then says whether it would change anything. `apps/lab/src/worlds.ts` holds the traits (read off each preset's config, so they cannot drift) and the hand-written notes; the public Worlds page (`/worlds/`) is the same catalogue rendered as a page, with a link into the lab that preselects the world (`/lab/?world=<id>&seed=<n>`). A world's name (`name` in `packages/schema/src/presets.ts`) is plain words a visitor can say aloud, with what sets a variant apart in parentheses (Even light (searched founders), Storm (meteors)); no milestone labels or family prefixes, since the select and the catalogue already group by family. "Searched founders" means the twelve founders the founder search confirmed, "hand-built" the generalist and the movers. Names are display-only: ids, presetIdentity and run bundles never read them, and `worlds.test.ts` fails a name that repeats or leaks a code name.
- Plant says whether it would change anything: "Plant it again" when the chosen starting world and seed are the ones on screen, "Plant this world" lit in sun when they are not, with a note naming the world still on screen. The seed number is drawn afresh on every visit (1 to 9999, short enough to read out) unless the link names one, so visitors grow different histories; the field's hint says so.
- The tool in your hand says what it does under the toolbar (`.tool-says`), the hover line on the field repeats the verb for a click there, and a brush stroke answers with a toast naming what it did and how many changes by hand are unsaved; the run strip carries that count ("By hand") beside Jar and Replay until the next save.
- An action that would lose unsaved steps, and deleting a saved copy, ask inline beside the action (`.guard`), with a save-first choice. No native dialogs.
- A browser that keeps no files for the page (private browsing; WebKit's ephemeral sessions refuse the Origin Private File System) still runs the world, downloads it and opens a file. Save stays visible and disabled, with the reason beside it in plain words in place of the empty-shelf note, the inline question's save-first choice becomes download-first (Download, then plant), and the lineage drawer says why it offers no jump. A storage failure refuses the one request with a toast; it never stops the world.
- A badge is green only for a clean pass. A true statement that is not one (a jar fed by hand, which reads "fed by hand, all accounted"; a replay check of an earlier segment; a computed curve) takes the neutral `.badge.note`; a check in progress takes `.badge.busy` in sun colours.
- The copy never overstates the physics: a jar is sealed until you open it with Feed or Drain, which the lab logs; a wound turns bodies to waste and their stored energy to heat; the accounting is checked as the world runs; replay is bit for bit on supported GPUs; the same world and seed give the same history until something is done by hand.
- The field can be operated with a pointer or keyboard. Arrow keys move the field focus, Enter uses the tool in hand, plus and minus zoom. Ordinary keyboard behaviour of focused form controls and links takes precedence over lab shortcuts.
- The inspected cell is marked on the field with a square; the round marker is the keyboard focus point. History charts run from zero, with the scale top printed.
- Site pages: a sticky paper header with Home, Lab, Worlds, How it works, Journal, Join in and Status; `.page-intro`, `.prose-grid` with a small sun-ink aside label, `.evidence-card` entries with a `.state` chip that says in words how sure a result is, and a sun `.invitation` band. Cards are not nested and nothing is a uniform card grid: the mosaic's tiles differ in size and fill.
- The transport (Play, Step, +100, Speed, the step count) sits above the jar; the view picker and its Showing line sit under it. The views are Everything, Lineages, Nutrient, Waste, Energy, Signal, Pull and Light (keys 1 to 8); Light draws the sun the physics uses at the current step, in the sun's own yellow, so a moving or seasonal sun can be watched.
- At narrow widths, the jar and its transport come first, then the answers, then the controls. A failure stays on the field until dismissed or until a world loads. Navigation and view modes remain horizontally reachable without page overflow.
- Respect reduced-motion settings, visible keyboard focus, and text contrast in both themes. Motion is 80 to 180ms, ease-out, and conveys state only.
- Link previews and search: each public page states its title, description and canonical link once in its own `<head>`, and `apps/lab/src/social-meta.ts` (a Vite plugin) derives the Open Graph and Twitter card tags from them, so a shared link says what the page says. Every page shares one social card, `public/social-card.jpg` (1200 by 630, under 300 KB for WhatsApp): the wordmark, "Artificial life in your browser", the hero line, and a real capture of Random soup in a jar under the half-sun. Titles and descriptions name artificial life where it is true of the page; `apps/lab/test/social-meta.test.ts` holds their lengths, the sitemap and the card. The home page and the lab carry schema.org JSON-LD for the site and the lab as a free web application. A lab link that names a world (`/lab/?world=<id>`) previews as that world: every world has its own card (`public/og/worlds/<id>.jpg`, the same layout with the world's family, name, question and first traits, and the real world grown on the GPU for a few thousand steps, framed where its living mass is), the build writes a copy of the lab page per world at `/lab/world/<id>/` with that world's title, description and card, and Caddy serves it when a link names a known world. The Worlds page does the same for `/worlds/?world=<id>`: each world's name there links to that address and a Copy link button beside Open in the lab copies it (and says so), and a visitor who follows one lands on that world's card, ringed and labelled "The world your link names". `pnpm gen:cards` redraws the cards and the site card from `social-card.html` (dev only) and records what each shows in `public/og/worlds/manifest.json`; the unit test fails when the manifest no longer matches the catalogue.
- Voice: the site speaks as "we", whoever is looking that day plus whoever joins in. The About page names Nicholas Zographos once and speaks in a light first person: forty years of cellular automata, Spore, bigger bets counted exactly, AI as a naturalist, shared worlds, and above all the pleasure of watching life grow, artificial and real. The frame is a gardener returning to see what changed, never ownership: no byline, no "my garden", no "one-person project"; the footer tagline stays "A garden of tiny sealed worlds." The home page's structured data names him as author for search engines only. Flavour is sprinkled, not poured: outside About, a few plain-spoken asides per page and never a rewrite (a wound "feels mean", a joined island means "a busy fan", steady worlds "get boring", "come back to see what changed"), the journal stays sober, and nothing overstates what a world has shown or what the site promises (the privacy page is the measure of every promise, such as "no cookies").
- How sure a result is, in one vocabulary on the journal and the Worlds catalogue (`SEEN_LABELS` and `SEEN_TONE` in `apps/lab/src/worlds.ts`): Confirmed in green (a registered test or a milestone gate, such as Milestone 3, that passed; an exploratory run that meets a criterion it wrote down before its first run is still Measured), Measured in sun, Negative in red, and Seen, Unclear and Not yet measured in grey. The journal's chips add the date after a middle dot.
- Words: the lab's names win in copy, so a wound (not a lesion), a sandbox world (not a preset), a seed number. Registered means hypotheses and decision rules written down before the data. An island is a browser tab lending its GPU to queued experiments, and the shared garden's islands are that idea grown up; the Four islands world plays it out inside one jar. Milestones are spelled out the first time on a page (Milestone 3, then M3).
- Mechanics: British spelling (colour, behaviour, centre, favour) and curly apostrophes and quotation marks, in the pages and in the strings the lab and the catalogue build in code.
- One documented exception: the discovery worker page (`apps/lab/discovery`) is built on its own for the coordinator and keeps inline styles in the same paper and sun palette with system fonts, following the system colour scheme rather than the Day/Night toggle.


### Same-run storage conflicts (2026-10-17)

Continuing the latest checkpoint may reuse its run ID in more than one tab. A manifest now carries an opaque
`storageRevision`: a save compares the revision it read with the recoverable on-disk manifest while holding the
origin records Web Lock, then writes a fresh UUID revision using the existing `.prev` copy-then-rewrite path.
Legacy manifests use their serialized content as an initial comparison token; a fresh branch clears the token.
A stale save is refused before touching either manifest version. Its world and intervention log remain in memory,
and Download can preserve its checkpoint; opening the latest saved copy resumes the winning history. Histories
are never merged automatically: independent intervention sequences do not describe one deterministic replay.
Checkpoint and edge filenames use UUIDs so writes preceding the comparison cannot replace another tab's files.
Pruning checks the same revision and holds the records lock through deletions and manifest update; failed
removals remain in the worker manifest for retry. Without Web Locks, manifest writes refuse safely; simulation,
reading existing copies, Download and Open remain available. No OPFS behavior or simulation rules change.
Already-open tabs running older code must reload to participate in revision checks.


### Pond keeper v0 (2026-10-17)

The evidence rail gains one more panel under Who's living here?, titled with its question, What happened first?: a log of firsts (a first body, a first split, a first lineage to last, and so on) counted by a detector whose rules were written down before the code (`docs/pond-keeper-v0-2026-10-17.md`; the full list sits folded under "What counts as a first"). Each row is the step in mono, the name and one plain line of detail, newest first. A first that already held when this page began watching is muted and says "Already there when watching began"; a log that did not start with the world (a copy opened from an earlier visit) says so in a neutral `.badge.note`, "Watching since t=…". The panel never scores, ranks or praises: the lede says a first is a verdict on a census, and the session card says it again.

Under the log, Session card opens `<dialog class="card">` over the bench (the catalogue's pattern: titled head, scrolling body, footer, backdrop click and Escape close it, no native dialogs). Its sections are plain labels: What was bred (the world's pressure in the breeder's own measure names, how many pond cycles took your donors and how many the rule's), How far (bodies, largest body, deepest generation and lineages at the first census watched and now), What died, Firsts, By hand, and the latest replay check (green only for a clean pass of the present stretch, `.badge.note` for an earlier one, red for a mismatch), then the honesty line: reordering four donors changes a whole history, so a first reached in one session and missed in another says little about the pressure. Nothing on it is a score. The footer's Download session file is the card's one `.primary`; while a pond cycle waits it is disabled with the reason beside it ("The file waits for the pond cycle: breed first"), and a refusal from the worker (a world opened from a file, a stopped log) is said in the same place and in a toast. Once saved, the footer names the file and prints the `tools/run.ts` command that replays it.

The sun marks the offer: after ten minutes of wall time with the world growing and no cycle waiting, Session card lights (`.lit`) and its hint says why; opening the card takes the offer. Nothing else on the page moves for it.


### Pond keeper v1 (2026-10-17)

Four play verbs and a ladder, added to the v0 panels (`docs/pond-keeper-v1-2026-10-17.md`). In Breed by hand, above the Breeder switch, **The rule breeds for** is a select of the measures in the breeder's own words (the planted score first, marked "As planted", then each measure alone, then the combinations "together"), with a line under it saying what the chosen score ranks by, and a **Set** button: choosing in the select commits nothing (arrowing through it must not log six pressures), and Set is disabled while the choice is already in force. It is a different thing from "Rank the ponds by", which only orders the ponds you see while picking: its hint says so, and a line under it says how many times the dial was set and from which cycle (a neutral `.badge.note`, because the world no longer runs on its planted rule). In a world whose arm the dial cannot move it is disabled with the reason beside it, never hidden. Setting it while a cycle waits re-ranks "The rule’s choice" at once and keeps the picks the player has made.

**Breed on its own** (1, 5 or 20 cycles, or until stopped) answers each waiting cycle with the rule's choice, under the dial and the kept lineage, worked out by the worker from the cycle actually waiting (never from a survey the page may hold from before a dial or Keep change), with no question: the player asked for the number in advance, and Stop is always there. It never opens the pick bar, moves the view or changes the tool. It says why it ended in plain words (you pressed Stop, you paused, the world was replaced, the world failed or something else went wrong, Breeder mode went off, or the count was reached); a cycle counts once the world has applied it. Its buttons are disabled with the reason beside them until Breeder mode is on.

**Keep a lineage** is set from Biggest lineages, from a cell looked at, or from a lineage's drawer, and cleared from the panel. The panel names the kept lineage with its colour, says where it lives as of the latest cycle that waited, and says "Gone from the jar" in a neutral badge when it is. Keep adds the pond holding most of that lineage to the rule's donors; it never changes a hand pick, and the hint says that a cycle adjusted this way counts as the player's on the card.

**What’s next?** sits above the log in What happened first?: the first three rungs of the ladder not yet reached, each a name, a bar and the numbers in words, followed by "seen so far" (a best since watching began) or "now" (a reading at the latest census), so a bar that falls back is not a surprise. A bar is ink on the raised ground, not sun: nothing there is lit. The panel shows progress and never scores, and the card's "What was bred" gains a Dial row only once the dial has been set.


### Pond keeper v2 (2026-10-17)

One more panel under What happened first?, titled with its question, **Who’s kin to whom?**, and a ladder with five more rungs (`docs/pond-keeper-v2-2026-10-17.md`). Under the title the list is headed **Family tree**: one row per family in tree order, indented by depth (a thin rule down the left of a child, and the indent stops growing after five levels; the row still says "from <parent>"). A row is a swatch, the family’s name, a status chip, then two plain lines: its cells, its share of the jar, its bodies and its purity (a whole percent, floored, or a dash when the family has no body), and when it was founded and where it came from ("from Reed", "a starting family", or "of unknown descent"). The swatch wears the lab’s own colour for the founding lineage when the lineage list has drawn it, and a dotted empty square when it has not: the panel never invents a colour. The chips stay neutral: Living in `.badge.note`, Gone to film and Extinct in the plain badge (film dashed), because green belongs to a clean pass and the family tree has no pass. An extinct family is dimmed, and once there are more than four the panel folds them behind "Show N extinct" (a button with `aria-expanded`). Past 120 rows the panel says how many it did not draw, and the counts line above the tree still includes them.

Under the tree, **What the tree has seen** is its own log, newest first: the step, a kind in words (Split, Gone to film, Back from film, Extinct, Found) and one plain line. The last eight show, and "Show all" opens the rest. Folded under both, **How families are told apart** states the rule in ordinary words: a family is a branch of descent, told apart by descent and not by genome distance (distance groups are not stable and no tolerance has been checked on evolved populations, so two families can be closer in their genes than two lineages inside one), the 16-cell, 5,000-step split rule, what purity is, what film means, how names are given, and what happens when the lab’s record overflows. The panel says "seen, not measured" in its lede and in that fold, and it never ranks a family or calls one better. A tree that began late says "Watching since t=…" in a `.badge.note`, and one that stopped says why in the same place (the log of firsts goes on, and no session file is offered).

Next up shows the new rungs in the same words. A rung that counts a ratio shows one decimal ("2.4 of 3 times as long as wide · seen so far"); a share is "40% of a pond’s body cells, needs 50% · now". The wound rung, while it reads 0, adds a line saying how to move it: pick the Wound tool and brush a pond that holds a body. The session card gains a **Families** section (named, with how many started and how many split off; living; gone to film; extinct) and says again that families are told apart by descent, seen and not measured. A session file is now version 3 and carries the tree; versions 1 and 2 still open and replay.
