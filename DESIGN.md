# Design system

## Direction

The frame is a garden (2026-10-06, replacing the dark-graphite field notebook of 2026-10-04): every world is a sealed jar under a lamp, the lab is the bench the jar stands on, and the public site is the garden around it. The register is utilitarian with friendliness, in the manner of Ableton Live and classic Facebook: flat, chunky, plainly labelled controls; lit states instead of decoration; one committed colour; big friendly type; a voice that says what things do in ordinary words. Nothing is toy-like and nothing is a museum: the chips still say "seen, not measured" where that is the truth.

The lab keeps its three-column bench: Plant a world, In your hand and Keep a copy on the left; the jar, its transport and How it's going in the centre; the honest answers on the right. Every evidence panel is titled with the question it answers (Is the jar still sealed? Does it replay the same? What's in this cell? Who's living here?), and every control says what it will do before it is used and what it did afterwards.

## Colour and type

- Day is the default: warm paper chrome (`--bg`, `--surface`, `--raised`, hue 88) around a dark jar (`--field`, which stays dark in both themes). Night keeps the same roles in warm dusk colours. The toggle in every header reads Day or Night and follows the person across pages.
- One committed colour, the sun (`--sun`, with `--sun-ink` for the same hue as text on paper and `--sun-soft` for tinted backgrounds). The sun marks what is lit: the tool in your hand, the chosen view, the one primary action, a check in progress, and on the site the numbers, eyebrows, the invitation band and the highlighted word in the hero. It is never decoration.
- The name is Cadence Garden (cadence.garden). Its mark is a sealed jar under a rising half-sun with three green cells growing in a beat: the jar in `--field` with an ink outline and lid, the sun in `--sun`, the cells in `--life-fill`. It is inline SVG (`.brand-mark`, styled in `tokens.css`) so it follows the theme; `public/favicon.svg` is the same drawing with fixed colours and a dark-scheme outline. The wordmark is "Cadence" in 800 and " Garden" in 500 muted.
- Green belongs to the living: the cells in the brand mark, Play while the world is growing (`--life-fill` under ink, and the label changes to Pause), the growing status dot, and a clean pass (`--success`, `.badge.ok`). Red (`--error`) is a failure and nothing else.
- The pools keep their colours (blue nutrient, green biomass, white membrane, brown waste) and mean pools and nothing else; a label accompanies every key. On the home page the ingredients mosaic uses the same four colours plus the sun, so the site teaches the lab's legend. History charts are drawn in the text colour.
- Tokens live in `apps/lab/src/tokens.css`, imported by `style.css` (lab) and `site.css` (site). Inputs and selects take `--control-line`, which holds 3:1 on `--surface` and `--bg` (they never sit on `--raised`); `--soft` text holds 4.5:1 on `--surface` and `--bg`, and `--muted` on `--raised` too. Only `oklch` colours; no pure black or white anywhere.
- One typeface, Rubik (variable, latin subset, self-hosted from `/fonts/`; the privacy page says so), with the system stack behind it; a monospace stack for coordinates, measurements and status. The lab's scale is six fixed steps (`--t-micro` 11px to `--t-title` 20px); nothing is set smaller than 11px. The site sets display headings at weight 800 with tight tracking, eyebrows in Rubik small caps rather than monospace, and body copy at 17px with a 58 to 66ch measure.

## Components and behaviour

- Buttons are chunky flat chips with an 8px radius: `--raised` at rest, `--hover` on hover, the sun under ink for `.primary` and for a selected `.seg` button (`.on`, `aria-pressed`), 45% opacity when disabled. Focus is a 2px ink ring with an offset, for keyboard focus only.
- Play is the one primary action above the jar and lights green while growing; while a pond cycle waits, Breed takes over in a bar under the field and Play steps back to a plain button. Controls that cannot act until then stay visible, disabled, with the reason beside them.
- The Starting world field explains its choice: under the select, the question the world asks, its traits as chips (light, size, who is planted, and any rule that differs from the base rules), and a chip saying whether it ran in a registered test (Registered), is one of the first worlds (Base world) or is exploratory (Sandbox). Browse all worlds opens the catalogue over the bench, a dialog with the families down the side (Start here, Steady jars, Islands and ponds, A moving sun, Who owns the matter, Shape and cells, Storms and the breeder) and the worlds of one family as cards: what each asks, its traits lever by lever with the numbers under them, how it differs from its base world (computed from the two configs), what to look for, what has been seen so far in the journal's chip colours, and the technical description folded away for researchers. Choose this world chooses in the select and hands focus to Plant, which then says whether it would change anything. `apps/lab/src/worlds.ts` holds the traits (read off each preset's config, so they cannot drift) and the hand-written notes; the public Worlds page (`/worlds/`) is the same catalogue rendered as a page, with a link into the lab that preselects the world (`/lab/?world=<id>&seed=<n>`). A world's name (`name` in `packages/schema/src/presets.ts`) is plain words a visitor can say aloud, with what sets a variant apart in parentheses (Even light (searched founders), Storm (meteors)); no milestone labels or family prefixes, since the select and the catalogue already group by family. "Searched founders" means the twelve founders the founder search confirmed, "hand-built" the generalist and the movers. Names are display-only: ids, presetIdentity and run bundles never read them, and `worlds.test.ts` fails a name that repeats or leaks a code name.
- Plant says whether it would change anything: "Plant it again" when the chosen starting world and seed are the ones on screen, "Plant this world" lit in sun when they are not, with a note naming the world still on screen. The seed number is drawn afresh on every visit (1 to 9999, short enough to read out) unless the link names one, so visitors grow different histories; the field's hint says so.
- The tool in your hand says what it does under the toolbar (`.tool-says`), the hover line on the field repeats the verb for a click there, and a brush stroke answers with a toast naming what it did and how many changes by hand are unsaved; the run strip carries that count ("By hand") beside Jar and Replay until the next save.
- An action that would lose unsaved steps, and deleting a saved copy, ask inline beside the action (`.guard`), with a save-first choice. No native dialogs.
- A badge is green only for a clean pass. A true statement that is not one (a jar fed by hand, which reads "fed by hand, all accounted"; a replay check of an earlier segment; a computed curve) takes the neutral `.badge.note`; a check in progress takes `.badge.busy` in sun colours.
- The copy never overstates the physics: a jar is sealed until you open it with Feed or Drain, which the lab logs; a wound turns bodies to waste and their stored energy to heat; the accounting is checked as the world runs; replay is bit for bit on supported GPUs; the same world and seed give the same history until something is done by hand.
- The field can be operated with a pointer or keyboard. Arrow keys move the field focus, Enter uses the tool in hand, plus and minus zoom. Ordinary keyboard behaviour of focused form controls and links takes precedence over lab shortcuts.
- The inspected cell is marked on the field with a square; the round marker is the keyboard focus point. History charts run from zero, with the scale top printed.
- Site pages: a sticky paper header with Home, Lab, How it works, Journal, Join in and Status; `.page-intro`, `.prose-grid` with a small sun-ink aside label, `.evidence-card` entries with a `.state` chip that says in words what kind of statement it is (observed, measured, pending, negative), and a sun `.invitation` band. Cards are not nested and nothing is a uniform card grid: the mosaic's tiles differ in size and fill.
- The transport (Play, Step, +100, Speed, the step count) sits above the jar; the view picker and its Showing line sit under it. The views are Everything, Lineages, Nutrient, Waste, Energy, Signal, Pull and Light (keys 1 to 8); Light draws the sun the physics uses at the current step, in the sun's own yellow, so a moving or seasonal sun can be watched.
- At narrow widths, the jar and its transport come first, then the answers, then the controls. A failure stays on the field until dismissed or until a world loads. Navigation and view modes remain horizontally reachable without page overflow.
- Respect reduced-motion settings, visible keyboard focus, and text contrast in both themes. Motion is 80 to 180ms, ease-out, and conveys state only.
- Link previews and search: each public page states its title, description and canonical link once in its own `<head>`, and `apps/lab/src/social-meta.ts` (a Vite plugin) derives the Open Graph and Twitter card tags from them, so a shared link says what the page says. Every page shares one social card, `public/social-card.jpg` (1200 by 630, under 300 KB for WhatsApp): the wordmark, "Artificial life in your browser", the hero line, and a real capture of Random soup in a jar under the half-sun. Titles and descriptions name artificial life where it is true of the page; `apps/lab/test/social-meta.test.ts` holds their lengths, the sitemap and the card. The home page and the lab carry schema.org JSON-LD for the site and the lab as a free web application.
- One documented exception: the discovery worker page (`apps/lab/discovery`) is built on its own for the coordinator and keeps inline styles in the same paper and sun palette with system fonts, following the system colour scheme rather than the Day/Night toggle.
